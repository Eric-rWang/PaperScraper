### 1.1 — Non-invasive, Continuous Venous Oxygen Saturation and Oxygen Extraction Estimation from the Internal Jugular Vein *(previously surfaced — the flagship new-generation true-venous device)*
- **Authors:** ⚠ search-derived (bioengineering group associated with the jugular-optical-sensing line).
- **Year / Venue:** 2025 · **IEEE EMBC 2025** proceedings. IEEE Xplore doc 11254941; PubMed 41335695; DOI 10.1109/EMBC58623.2025.11254941. ⚠ egress-blocked; metadata cross-checked on ≥2 snippets.
- **Link:** https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/11254941/ · https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41335695/ · https://doi.org/10.1109/embc58623.2025.11254941
- **Relevance:** **8.5/10** — directly targets non-invasive, continuous **true venous** sO₂ at the IJV plus cerebral O₂ extraction.
- **Novelty:** Flexible/conformal neck sensor array reads the **IJV pulse + breathing-induced modulation** (peripheral veins being non-pulsatile) to estimate SvO₂ ≈ **65.7–80.9 %** (SD 1.67 % for IJV-pulse, 4.26 % for breathing modulation) and a per-subject **cerebral O₂-extraction ratio (O₂ER)** in 6 healthy adults; co-registers carotid artery and IJV at one neck site, working in both upright and supine positions. Authors explicitly flag artery–vein **crosstalk mitigation** and larger-cohort blood-gas validation as next steps.
- **Integration insights:** The extraction-aware wearable endpoint of the whole Topic-1 program; the artery serves as an in-situ reference (same logic as APM+, §2.2). §1.3 (sternal-marrow reservoir) and §1.2 (jugular/cerebral NIRS) are the alternative-site and surface-NIRS counterparts to this vessel-targeted pulse device. The retinal A-V paper (standing context) is the ocular analogue of its cerebral-O₂ER endpoint — extraction read at a different accessible vascular bed.

### 1.2 — Near-Infrared Spectroscopy for Estimating Mixed Venous Oxygen Saturation and Cardiac Output During Off-Pump Coronary Artery Bypass Grafting: A Prospective Observational Study *(previously surfaced — first logged 2026-09-08)*
- **Authors:** ⚠ search-derived — first author Chahyun Oh et al. (per index snippet); not page-confirmed (PubMed egress-blocked).
- **Year / Venue:** 2026 · ***Journal of Clinical Monitoring and Computing*** (Springer; search-derived from DOI stem 10.1007/s10877-026-01477-1). PubMed 42606675. (Search snippet dates the publication to August 2026.)
- **Link / DOI:** https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10877-026-01477-1 · https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42606675/ ⚠ egress-blocked; metadata via index snippet.
- **Relevance:** **7.5/10** — non-invasive SvO₂ surrogate via **jugular + cerebral NIRS**, validated head-to-head against **pulmonary-artery-catheter SvO₂** in adults.
- **Novelty:** Tests two non-invasive NIRS surrogates in 25 OPCAB patients — (a) **rSjvO₂** over the left IJV (bias 2.0 %, wide LoA −14.9/+18.9 %) and (b) **baseline-calibrated cerebral NIRS (cal-rScO₂)** on the forehead (bias 0.8 %, LoA −7.8/+9.4 %, good SvO₂ < 65 % discrimination and trending). **NIRS-derived cardiac output failed** (≈33 % error). Re-confirmed this run: accuracy varied with jugular anatomy (lowest error at IJV depth ≈ 1.38 cm); the calibrated cerebral-NIRS surrogate is framed as useful for SvO₂ *trending*, jugular NIRS useful in selected patients.
- **Integration insights:** The most rigorous 2026 adult validation yet of jugular/cerebral NIRS as an SvO₂ *trend* monitor. Tellingly, **baseline-calibrated cerebral NIRS out-tracked a sensor placed right over the IJV** — a strong argument for the vessel-*resolving* imaging of Topic 2 (PA, vis-OCT) over surface averaging. Clean SvO₂ < 65 % discrimination is the clinically actionable low-SvO₂-alarm endpoint. Note the contrast with the retinal papers (standing context): NIRS-derived *cardiac output* failed here, whereas the retinal A-V *difference* tracked cardiac output — the extraction ratio may carry the hemodynamic signal that absolute NIRS StO₂ does not.

### 1.3 — Monitoring Changes in Mixed Venous Oxygen Saturation Using Photoplethysmography on the Sternum — A Proof of Concept Study *(previously surfaced — first logged 2026-09-08)*
- **Authors:** ⚠ search-derived — not page-confirmed (PubMed egress-blocked). Likely the Scandinavian intraosseous-sternal-PPG group behind the earlier porcine sternal-oximetry work (*J. Clin. Monit. Comput.* 10.1007/s10877-023-00980-z).
- **Year / Venue:** 2026 · ***Medical & Biological Engineering & Computing*** (Springer); DOI **10.1007/s11517-026-03648-3**. PubMed 42527850.
- **Link / DOI:** https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11517-026-03648-3 · https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42527850/ ⚠ egress-blocked; metadata via index snippet (re-confirmed this run).
- **Relevance:** **8/10** — non-invasive, continuous **true mixed-venous SvO₂** with a pulmonary-artery-catheter reference; a genuinely new anatomical site (sternum) for venous oximetry.
- **Novelty:** Exploits **sternal intramedullary (intraosseous) blood**, whose saturation tracks central/mixed-venous saturation, read **transcutaneously with a PPG probe** on the sternum. In anesthetized pigs, SvO₂ was driven down by stepwise hypoxia and staged hemorrhage while the non-invasive sternal signal (nSsO₂) was calibrated against pulmonary-artery SvO₂: **linear nSsO₂–SvO₂ correlation, ~87 % concordance** for tracking SvO₂ changes.
- **Integration insights:** A distinct physical route to non-invasive venous sO₂: the **bone marrow acts as a naturally venous-weighted optical reservoir**, sidestepping the artery–vein disambiguation problem. Trend-grade (~87 %) performance sets a realistic accuracy bar; a natural fusion target for the pulse-modulation math of §1.5.

### 1.5 — Proof-of-Concept Non-Invasive Estimation of Peripheral Venous Oxygen Saturation (arterio-venous compliance pulse-modulation) *(previously surfaced — foundational method ancestor)*
- **Year / Venue:** 2017 · *BioMedical Engineering OnLine* 16:60; DOI 10.1186/s12938-017-0351-x; PMC5437414.
- **Link:** https://biomedical-engineering-online.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/s12938-017-0351-x · https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5437414/
- **Relevance:** **7/10** — the original non-invasive peripheral-venous (SpvO₂) optical concept.
- **Novelty:** Uses the **compliance difference between arteries and veins** to induce artificial respiration-like modulation that makes venous blood pulsatile, then reads the induced PPG to build a calibration model for regional venous sO₂; validated in 8 healthy volunteers with arm-lift/occlusion maneuvers against blood-gas references. Estimated SpvO₂ ran ≈3.1 ± 4.2 % below arterial saturation. (Its earlier sibling — *A new method for noninvasive venous blood oxygen detection*, 10.1186/s12938-016-0208-8, 2016 — and the 2007 during-bypass venous-oximetry paper, PubMed 17664681, both in-corpus.)
- **Integration insights:** The method ancestor of the §1.3 sternal-PPG and §1.1 IJV-pulse devices — every "make the vein pulsatile, then read it like a pulse oximeter" approach traces here. Its modest accuracy is exactly what the deep-diffuse-optics pulsation-decomposition ideas in Topic 2 aim to improve.

> **DNN + Monte-Carlo IJV NIRS (not ranked, in corpus):** *Quantifying Changes in Oxygen Saturation of the Internal Jugular Vein In Vivo Using Deep Neural Networks and Subject-Specific 3D Monte Carlo Models* (*Optics Letters* 49(10):2669, 2024). > **Wearable venous-optics anchor (not ranked, in corpus):** *Towards Simultaneous Non-Invasive Arterial and Venous Oxygenation Monitoring with a Wearable Optical E-Tattoo* (Tan et al., UT Austin / Lu group; IEEE EMBC 2023, doc 10340010; PubMed 38083768; patent US2025/0025074). ### 2.1 — Noninvasive In Vivo Photoacoustic Measurement of Internal Jugular Venous Oxygenation in Humans *(previously surfaced — the Topic-2 anchor)*
- **Year / Venue:** 2023 · preprint arXiv:2303.10775 (+ SPIE lineage). ⚠ arXiv egress-blocked; abstract re-confirmed on two index snippets this run.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.10775
- **Relevance:** **9/10** — human, in vivo, PA, *venous* (IJV).
- **Novelty:** Dual-wavelength PAT + ultrasound images the IJV in **7 healthy volunteers** at 5 fps; average IJV sO₂ ≈ **72 ± 7 %** (range 62–79 %), with US co-registration enabling temporal sO₂ tracking — true venous oximetry at depth, non-invasively. Since >90 % of IJV drainage is intracranial, its saturation mirrors global cerebral O₂ consumption.
- **Integration insights:** Proof that PA reaches a clinically relevant central vein through intact tissue; the imaging counterpart to the §1.1 pulse-based IJV device — same vessel, different sensing physics. GenMC (§2.5) is the fluence-quantification layer this US-guided PA-of-the-IJV paradigm needs to become quantitative.

### 2.2 — Arterial Blood-Mediated Deep-Tissue Photoacoustic Oximetry (APM+): In-Vivo Human Radial-Vein sO₂ *(previously surfaced)*
- **Authors:** Sastry, Olick-Gibson et al. (Caltech Optical Imaging Laboratory, L.V. Wang group).
- **Year / Venue:** 2026 · *Advanced Science* (Wiley), 10.1002/advs.76366; PMC13334587; PubMed 42397001. ⚠ egress-blocked; abstract re-confirmed via index snippet this run.
- **Link:** https://advanced.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/advs.76366 · https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13334587/
- **Relevance:** **9/10** — solves the fluence problem for deep-tissue PA sO₂; reports in-vivo human values on the radial artery–vein pair (n = 8).
- **Novelty:** Uses high, known arterial oxygenation as a local in-situ **fluence reference** to correct spectral coloring, cutting median sO₂ error to **2.9 %** (vs 9.8 % for linear unmixing); human **venous sO₂ median 72.3 %** (IQR 8.9 %, vs 34.4 % for linear unmixing) on the radial vein, robust through ex-vivo tissue up to 1.5 cm. Recovered the parabolic laminar-flow wavefront inside a deep vein in vivo in real time.
- **Integration insights:** Directly attacks the single biggest barrier to quantitative deep venous PA — unknown local fluence — using the artery as a self-calibrator. GenMC (§2.5) attacks the *same* fluence problem by a different route — a learned Monte-Carlo surrogate driven by co-registered US anatomy rather than an arterial reference — so the two are complementary strategies to the identical bottleneck.

### 2.4 — Adaptive Spectroscopic Visible-Light OCT for Clinical Retinal Oximetry (ADS-vis-OCT) *(previously surfaced)*
- **Authors:** Chen Q., Nolen S.L., Spencer S.A., Yi J. (Johns Hopkins).
- **Year / Venue:** 2023 · *Communications Medicine* (Nature), s43856-023-00288-8; PubMed 37095177; PMC10126115.
- **Link:** https://www.nature.com/articles/s43856-023-00288-8 ⚠ egress-blocked; metadata via snippet (re-confirmed this run).
- **Relevance:** **8/10** — resolves *per-vessel arterial and venous* retinal sO₂ non-invasively.
- **Novelty:** Adaptively removes vessel-specific spectral contaminants; RMSE ≈ 2.1 % vs pulse oximeter in major arteries, repeatability SD ≈ 2.3 % in *veins*; ex-vivo phantom agreement within 1 % bias across 0–100 % sO₂. Demonstrated across glaucoma, ischemia, DR, CRVO, sickle-cell retinopathy.
- **Integration insights:** Sets the accuracy ceiling for optical venous oximetry in an accessible vascular bed; the adaptive spectral-correction idea is transferable to PA/DOT venous inversion where spectral coloring dominates error (cf. §2.2). Its compute cost is directly addressed by the DGFT acceleration paper (standing lead below, same group), and its Beer-Lambert model core is now upgraded by the dcBLL diffusion-correction paper (standing lead below). The retinal application papers (standing context) are the clinical-deployment counterparts in the same vascular bed.

### 2.5 — GenMC: Real-Time Generative Monte Carlo Surrogate for Quantitative Photoacoustic Imaging *(previously surfaced — first logged 09-27; PA fluence-correction method with in-vivo venous sO₂ and skin-tone-bias correction)*
- **Authors:** Mengjie Shi, Feng He, Tom Vercauteren, Wenfeng Xia (King's College London). ⚠ author list search-derived (arXiv egress-blocked; cross-confirmed on ≥2 snippets).
- **Year / Venue:** 2026 · preprint **arXiv:2609.28261** (physics.med-ph), submitted Sept 23 2026. ⚠ arXiv egress-blocked; abstract cross-checked on ≥2 index snippets.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.28261 · https://arxiv.org/html/2609.28261
- **Relevance:** **7.5/10** — a genuinely new PA *fluence-quantification method* directly enabling quantitative venous sO₂ at depth; demonstrates in-vivo **venous oxygenation ≈ 74.49–77.93 %** and explicitly corrects a bias that most inflates *venous* estimates. Scored below the four PA/vis-OCT anchors because it is a preprint fluence-correction enabler rather than a dedicated venous-validation study, but above the retinal *application* papers because it advances the measurement modality itself and reports venous values.
- **Novelty:** A **conditional generative adversarial network** learns to estimate optical **fluence distributions** from tissue anatomy plus literature-derived optical properties, with **anatomical priors taken from co-registered ultrasound**. Trained on Monte-Carlo-generated synthetic data, it produces high-fidelity fluence maps in **< 30 ms/frame — a ~4-orders-of-magnitude speed-up over conventional MC** — making per-frame fluence compensation feasible in real time. Critically, it targets the **melanin problem**: elevated melanin in darker skin causes strong superficial attenuation and acoustic clutter that **systematically overestimate venous and arterial sO₂** in conventional PA oximetry, and GenMC is shown to mitigate this, improving accuracy and temporal stability in vivo. **Validation refinement noted this run (09-30 snippets):** blood-mimicking phantoms **+ 37 human volunteers spanning Fitzpatrick skin types III–V**.
- **Integration insights:** (1) Attacks the **same fluence/spectral-coloring bottleneck as APM+ (§2.2)** but by a learned-simulation route driven by co-registered US anatomy — the very US-guided geometry the §2.1 human-IJV PA paradigm already uses, making GenMC a natural quantitative layer for deep-vein PA oximetry. (2) Its **real-time (<30 ms) budget** is the throughput analogue of the DGFT vis-OCT accelerator (standing lead) — both make quantitative venous inversion fast enough for continuous monitoring. (3) The **skin-tone-bias correction** is the single most program-relevant contribution: equitable venous oximetry across skin tones is a documented failure mode of optical methods (cf. the melanin-corrected-StO₂ standing lead), and here it is addressed *for the venous compartment specifically*. Caveats: preprint (not peer-reviewed), venous values are in-vivo demonstrations rather than blood-gas-referenced validation, and it is a quantification method rather than a standalone venous monitor.

**State of the field, restated (unchanged).** The corpus continues to cluster around one structural problem — **compartment disambiguation**, separating the venous signal from arterial and tissue background — attacked at four scales: (1) **single-vessel resolution** (vis-OCT §2.4 + dcBLL, PACT §2.3, TD-HSI, fundus/retinal-HSI methods, and the retinal applications) resolves the vein directly; (2) **temporal isolation** (respiration-modulated NIRS §1.2, IJV-pulse device §1.1, the 2017 compliance-modulation ancestor §1.5) separates venous pulsation in time; (3) **an anatomical venous reservoir** (sternal marrow §1.3; portal-venous liver; cerebral venous sinus) sidesteps disambiguation; and (4) **spatial-heterogeneity mapping** (SFDI VOT). APM+ (§2.2) and GenMC (§2.5) cut across all four via fluence quantification — APM+ with an arterial anchor, GenMC with a learned US-driven surrogate.

**The fluence-correction cluster remains the center of gravity.** The corpus holds four distinct 2026 attacks on the single biggest barrier to quantitative deep venous PA — unknown local optical fluence / spectral coloring: (1) **APM+ (§2.2)** uses the artery as an in-situ fluence reference; (2) **GenMC (§2.5)** learns a fast fluence surrogate from US anatomy + Monte-Carlo priors; (3) the **learned-inversion family** (Hybrid-Net, PINN, distribution-informed) infers fluence-free sO₂ directly; and (4) **ISDC / maximum-fluence / FD-PAM** apply analytic depth-and-spectral compensation. Underpinning all four is a **ground-truth/phantom layer** — the sulfate-salt phantoms (Davenet et al.), blood-mimicking dye phantoms, and the IPCR inline dissolved-gas reference — that supplies the venous-range calibration these fluence methods are validated against. In the vis-OCT domain, **dcBLL** and the **DGFT accelerator** are the scattering-physics and throughput counterparts. Two threads unify these: quantitative venous PA is fundamentally a *fluence-estimation* problem, and it is increasingly a *real-time* one (GenMC's <30 ms echoes DGFT's ~6× speed-up) — both prerequisites for continuous monitoring rather than one-shot imaging.

**The recurring theme (unchanged).** **Oxygen *extraction* (the A-V difference), not absolute SvO₂, remains the shared clinically-actionable readout** across accessible vascular beds: the §1.1 IJV device reports a cerebral O₂-extraction ratio; SFDI's VOT reads a microvascular-vs-macrovascular extraction gap; and the retinal A-V difference (standing context) tracks cardiac output where NIRS-derived cardiac output failed (§1.2).

**Bottom line for the program.** The enabling-technology layer is very well populated (PA/vis-OCT fluence-and-scattering quantification, phantoms, learned inversion, wearable diffuse optics). The still-scarce item remains a genuinely new venous *measurement modality* — a new way to read the vein, not another arterial-SpO₂ method, implantable tissue oximeter, blood-gas correlation, phantom, or clinical correlation of an existing readout. The last three scans (09-28, 09-29, 09-30) added nothing on that front, consistent with the low-volume niche and the short one-day windows. No padding, no over-claiming: the ranked ten carry forward with honest scores, and the watch continues.
