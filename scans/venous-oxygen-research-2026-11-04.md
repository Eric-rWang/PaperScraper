### 1.1 — Non-invasive, Continuous Venous Oxygen Saturation and Oxygen Extraction Estimation from the Internal Jugular Vein *(previously surfaced — the flagship new-generation true-venous device; re-confirmed this run)*
- **Authors:** ⚠ search-derived (bioengineering group associated with the jugular-optical-sensing line).
- **Year / Venue:** 2025 · **IEEE EMBC 2025** proceedings. IEEE Xplore doc 11254941; PubMed 41335695; DOI 10.1109/EMBC58623.2025.11254941. ⚠ egress-blocked; metadata from search-index snippets (SvO₂ estimates **65.70–80.91 %**, "within expected literature ranges," 6 healthy adults, flexible sensor array reading IJV pulsations combined with arterial saturation to yield cerebral O₂ extraction; peripheral veins non-pulsatile so standard pulse oximetry cannot read SvO₂; authors flag larger-cohort + blood-gas validation and artery–vein crosstalk mitigation as next steps — all re-confirmed this run).
- **Link:** https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/11254941/ · https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41335695/ · https://doi.org/10.1109/embc58623.2025.11254941
- **Relevance:** **8.5/10** — directly targets non-invasive, continuous **true venous** sO₂ at the IJV plus cerebral O₂ extraction.
- **Novelty:** Flexible/conformal neck sensor array reads the **IJV pulse + breathing-induced modulation** (peripheral veins being non-pulsatile) to estimate SvO₂ ≈ **65.7–80.9 %** (SD 1.67 % for IJV-pulse, 4.26 % for breathing modulation) and a per-subject **cerebral O₂-extraction ratio (O₂ER)** in 6 healthy adults; co-registers carotid artery and IJV at one neck site, working upright and supine.
- **Integration insights:** The extraction-aware wearable endpoint of the whole Topic-1 program; the artery serves as an in-situ reference (same logic as APM+, §2.2). Sibling registry trial **NCT06004466** (National Taiwan University Hospital — plethysmographic device combining ultrasound imaging with optical tissue estimates for continuous non-invasive IJV saturation; re-confirmed listed this run) remains the independent clinical-translation counterpart; no published results yet. The **University of Canterbury neck-worn device** (SpO₂ + SpvO₂ + O₂ extraction; Christchurch Hospital ICU pilot underway, re-hit this run) is a further independent neck-site bet. The UT-Austin **wearable optical e-tattoo** (2023 EMBC 10.1109/embc40787.2023.10340010; 2025 patent US2025/0025074, re-confirmed this run — "pressureless, non-invasive e-tattoo for simultaneous arterial and venous oxygenation") is the conformal-array sibling pursuing the same neck/wrist artery–vein pair with spatial filtering for crosstalk; the **Vanderbilt 48-channel PPG e-tattoo** seminar line is the scaled-array continuation. **Standing context:** the NIH **K99EB035645** award (*"Next-generation photoacoustic imaging for real-time, non-invasive monitoring of brain oxygen metabolism,"* carotid+IJV → OEF/CMRO₂; first logged 11-02) is the funded continuation of exactly this O₂ER endpoint via the §2.1 PA route — a grant, not a paper.

### 1.2 — Near-Infrared Spectroscopy for Estimating Mixed Venous Oxygen Saturation and Cardiac Output During Off-Pump Coronary Artery Bypass Grafting: A Prospective Observational Study *(previously surfaced — first logged 2026-09-08; re-confirmed this run)*
- **Authors:** ⚠ search-derived — not page-confirmed (PubMed/Springer egress-blocked).
- **Year / Venue:** 2026 · ***Journal of Clinical Monitoring and Computing*** (Springer); DOI **10.1007/s10877-026-01477-1**. PubMed 42606675. ⚠ metadata via index snippet ("published online August 2026; jugular NIRS bias 2.0 %, LoA −14.9/+18.9 %; baseline-calibrated cerebral NIRS the more promising surrogate; NIRS cardiac output clinically insufficient").
- **Link / DOI:** https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10877-026-01477-1 · https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42606675/
- **Relevance:** **7.5/10** — non-invasive SvO₂ surrogate via **jugular + cerebral NIRS**, validated head-to-head against **pulmonary-artery-catheter SvO₂** in adults.
- **Novelty:** Tests two non-invasive NIRS surrogates in 25 OPCAB patients — (a) **rSjvO₂** over the left IJV (bias 2.0 %, wide LoA −14.9/+18.9 %) and (b) **baseline-calibrated cerebral NIRS (cal-rScO₂)** on the forehead (bias 0.8 %, LoA −7.8/+9.4 %); the calibrated cerebral measure reached **AUC 0.970 for detecting SvO₂ < 65 %** (sensitivity 95.9 %, specificity 89.8 %). **NIRS-derived cardiac output failed** (≈33 % error). Authors suggest ultrasound pre-screening to select patients in whom a sensor over the IJV is anatomically feasible.
- **Integration insights:** The most rigorous 2026 adult validation yet of jugular/cerebral NIRS as an SvO₂ *trend* monitor. Baseline-calibrated cerebral NIRS **out-tracked a sensor placed right over the IJV** — a strong argument for the vessel-*resolving* imaging of Topic 2 over surface averaging. A 2023 retrospective (*Sci. Rep.* s41598-023-49078-1 / PMC10709586) independently reports cerebral rScO₂ tracking SvO₂ changes — consistent trend-grade agreement, no absolute accuracy. Clean SvO₂ < 65 % discrimination is the clinically actionable low-SvO₂-alarm endpoint.

### 1.3 — Monitoring Changes in Mixed Venous Oxygen Saturation Using Photoplethysmography on the Sternum — A Proof of Concept Study *(previously surfaced — first logged 2026-09-08; re-confirmed this run)*
- **Authors:** ⚠ search-derived — not page-confirmed. Likely the Scandinavian intraosseous-sternal-PPG group behind the earlier porcine sternal-oximetry work (*J. Clin. Monit. Comput.* 10.1007/s10877-023-00980-z).
- **Year / Venue:** 2026 · ***Medical & Biological Engineering & Computing*** (Springer); DOI **10.1007/s11517-026-03648-3**. PubMed 42527850. ⚠ egress-blocked; metadata via prior-run index snippet ("nSsO₂ calibrated against pulmonary-artery SvO₂ in anesthetized pigs during hypoxia and haemorrhage; further technical development warranted").
- **Link / DOI:** https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11517-026-03648-3 · https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42527850/
- **Relevance:** **8/10** — non-invasive, continuous **true mixed-venous SvO₂** with a pulmonary-artery-catheter reference; a genuinely new anatomical site (sternum) for venous oximetry.
- **Novelty:** Exploits **sternal intramedullary (intraosseous) blood**, whose saturation tracks central/mixed-venous saturation, read **transcutaneously with a PPG probe** on the sternum. In anesthetized pigs, SvO₂ was driven down by stepwise hypoxia and staged hemorrhage while the non-invasive sternal signal (nSsO₂) was calibrated against pulmonary-artery SvO₂: **linear nSsO₂–SvO₂ correlation, ~87 % concordance** for tracking SvO₂ changes.
- **Integration insights:** A distinct physical route to non-invasive venous sO₂: the **bone marrow acts as a naturally venous-weighted optical reservoir**, sidestepping artery–vein disambiguation — the surface, non-invasive analogue of the deep mucosal reservoirs used by the esophageal-RRS and PA-TEE probes. The 2025 bone-marrow MSOT paper (Nano Letters, PMC12893716; Topic-2 standing lead) independently demonstrated that bone-marrow sO₂ is non-invasively optoacoustically measurable and dynamically informative, strengthening the physical premise of this sternal route. The NIRSense CHF wearable's sternal sensor (NCT07874386), and the Noninvasix **LIVOx** chest-worn central-venous-oximetry probe (FDA breakthrough designation Feb 2022), are two further independent bets on the chest/sternal site. Trend-grade (~87 %) performance sets a realistic accuracy bar.

### 1.5 — Proof-of-Concept Non-Invasive Estimation of Peripheral Venous Oxygen Saturation (arterio-venous compliance pulse-modulation) *(previously surfaced — foundational method ancestor; re-confirmed this run)*
- **Year / Venue:** 2017 · *BioMedical Engineering OnLine* 16:60; DOI 10.1186/s12938-017-0351-x; PMC5437414. ⚠ egress-blocked; sibling PMC4952148 re-confirmed this run via index snippet.
- **Link:** https://biomedical-engineering-online.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/s12938-017-0351-x · https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5437414/
- **Relevance:** **7/10** — the original non-invasive peripheral-venous (SpvO₂) optical concept.
- **Novelty:** Uses the **compliance difference between arteries and veins** to induce artificial respiration-like modulation that makes venous blood pulsatile, then reads the induced PPG to build a calibration model for regional venous sO₂; validated in 8 healthy volunteers with arm-lift/occlusion maneuvers against blood-gas references. Estimated SpvO₂ ran ≈3.1 ± 4.2 % below arterial saturation. (Siblings in corpus, re-hit this run: *A new method for noninvasive venous blood oxygen detection*, 10.1186/s12938-016-0208-8, 2016, PMC4952148/PubMed 27436186, RMSE 5.31, r = 0.72; *Comparison of non-invasive peripheral venous saturations with venous blood co-oximetry* by Kyriacou's group, PMC5655584, r = 0.81; the 2007 Echiadis et al. artificial-finger-pulse venous-oximetry paper during cardiac-surgery bypass, PubMed 17664681.)
- **Integration insights:** The method ancestor of the §1.3 sternal-PPG and §1.1 IJV-pulse devices — every "make the vein pulsatile, then read it like a pulse oximeter" approach traces here. The artery–vein crosstalk problem it first exposed is the same one the §1.1 spatial-filtering e-tattoo and the §2.5 spectral-ratio rule now attack. Its modest accuracy is exactly what the deep-diffuse-optics pulsation-decomposition ideas in Topic 2 aim to improve.

### 2.1 — Noninvasive In Vivo Photoacoustic Measurement of Internal Jugular Venous Oxygenation in Humans *(previously surfaced — the Topic-2 anchor; re-confirmed this run)*
- **Authors:** Garcia-Uribe et al. (Wang lab, WUSTL/Caltech). ⚠ arXiv egress-blocked; abstract + author line re-confirmed this run on index snippets (WUSTL profiles, Caltech epub, arXiv listing).
- **Year / Venue:** 2023 · preprint arXiv:2303.10775 (+ SPIE/Caltech-WUSTL lineage; precursor SPIE 8943E..1M, 2014).
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.10775 · https://arxiv.org/pdf/2303.10775 · https://authors.library.caltech.edu/records/5jp3j-jnz35
- **Relevance:** **9/10** — human, in vivo, PA, *venous* (IJV).
- **Novelty:** Dual-wavelength photoacoustic tomography co-registered with ultrasound (5 fps) in 7 healthy volunteers; average internal-jugular sijvO₂ **72 ± 7 %** (per-volunteer 62–79 %; a conventional PA-amplitude method gives ≈66–67 % IJV vs ≈96 % carotid). >90 % of IJV drainage comes from intracranial structures, so the IJV reading can track global brain oxygen consumption; framed as a non-invasive alternative to fluoroscopy-guided jugular catheterization. Authors note the approach could extend to hepatic, femoral and renal veins. A larger PA/US cohort (SKKU; 165 carotids in 117 volunteers) reports IJV **71.3 ± 6.4 %** vs carotid **93.0 ± 3.5 %**.
- **Integration insights:** The reference demonstration that deep-vein sO₂ is PA-measurable in humans. Everything else in Topic 2 either improves its quantification (GenMC standing lead; APM+ §2.2), extends the flow/vessel-type readout (PACT §2.3; ULM+PA preprint standing lead), moves to another bed (retina §2.4, skin microvasculature §2.5), or translates it to a wearable (LIVOx / e-tattoo, Topic-1 standing context). **Standing context:** the NIH **K99EB035645** award (*"Next-generation photoacoustic imaging for real-time, non-invasive monitoring of brain oxygen metabolism,"* first logged 11-02) is the funded continuation of exactly this line — carotid + IJV PA imaging with OEF/CMRO₂ computation from a new device. The Fick-principle CMRO₂-from-IJV-flow-plus-saturation route (*J. Biomed. Opt.* 23(1):016009) is the method precedent for that OEF/CMRO₂ endpoint.

### 2.2 — Arterial Blood-Mediated Deep-Tissue Photoacoustic Oximetry (APM+): In-Vivo Human Radial-Vein sO₂ *(previously surfaced; re-confirmed this run)*
- **Authors:** Sastry et al. (Wang lab, Caltech). ⚠ metadata from Caltech PDF header + PMC snippet (received Jan 2026, accepted Jun 8 2026; re-confirmed this run via search snippet — Caltech coilab PDF, Advanced Science listing).
- **Year / Venue:** 2026 · *Advanced Science* (Wiley), 10.1002/advs.76366; PMC13334587; PubMed 42397001. Online early July 2026.
- **Link:** https://advanced.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/advs.76366 · https://coilab.caltech.edu/documents/34878/Sastry-2026-AdvancedScience.pdf · https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42397001/
- **Relevance:** **8.5/10** — human, in vivo, PA, radial artery–vein pair with *venous* sO₂ reported.
- **Novelty:** Uses the arterial blood of an accompanying artery (high, known saturation) as an in-situ spectral reference to correct wavelength-dependent fluence ("spectral coloring") for the adjacent deep vein. In 8 healthy adults (dual-wavelength 3D PAT of the wrist), APM+ gave a **median venous sO₂ of 72.3 % (IQR 8.9 %)**; the LUM comparator gave 75.2 % with much wider spread (IQR 34.4 %). Phantom (true 75 %): **APM+ 72 ± 2 %**; phantom median error **2.9 % (APM+) vs 9.8 % (LUM)**; validated through up to 1.5 cm ex vivo tissue. The paper notes conventional PA oximetry gives only a single arterial estimate and loses accuracy in darker skin; authors explicitly propose extending to the **carotid–IJV pair plus flow to estimate oxygen consumption** — the same O₂ER endpoint as §1.1 — noting "most major arteries have accompanying veins."
- **Integration insights:** The artery-as-reference trick is the PA analogue of the arterio-venous compliance idea (§1.5) and the co-registered artery of §1.1. Its phantom-error figures remain the best in-corpus demonstration that per-vessel fluence correction beats linear unmixing for the venous compartment. The TU-Delft "known chromophore as fluence marker" line (Thomas et al., *Photoacoustics* 2025) is the independent methodological precursor.

### 2.4 — Adaptive Spectroscopic Visible-Light OCT for Clinical Retinal Oximetry (ADS-vis-OCT) *(previously surfaced; re-confirmed this run)*
- **Authors:** Rubinoff et al. ⚠ metadata from PMC/Nature snippet.
- **Year / Venue:** 2023 · *Communications Medicine* (Nature), s43856-023-00288-8; PubMed 37095177; PMC10126115.
- **Link:** https://www.nature.com/articles/s43856-023-00288-8 · https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37095177/
- **Relevance:** **8/10** — resolves *per-vessel arterial and venous* retinal sO₂ non-invasively.
- **Novelty:** Adds an adaptive spectral-contamination-removal step to vis-OCT, giving accurate, repeatable sO₂ in retinal arteries and veins with no pre-/post-calibration in vivo (phantom bias ≈1 % across 0–100 %; RMSE vs pulse oximeter 2.1 % major arteries; repeat SD 2.5 % small arteries / 2.3 % small veins in 18 volunteers). Standing modeling/technique upgrades: **dcBLL** (*Med. Biol. Eng. Comput.* 64:2177, 2026; PubMed 42008039), **DGFT** depth-gated Fourier transform (Nolen et al., *Opt. Lett.* 50(4):1061, 2025; PubMed 39951728), the 2025 bioRxiv PLIM-SLO phosphorescence-lifetime cross-validation of vis-OCT venous sO₂ (2025.05.29.656845), and a vis-OCT clinical-translation review (PMC12456912, 2025).
- **Integration insights:** The retina is the one bed where per-vessel *venous* sO₂ is already clinically mapped non-invasively; its adaptive spectral-unmixing is directly transferable to the PA/RSOM vessel-resolving problem elsewhere. **Standing context:** an **OHSU NIH R01** (last reviewed 2026-06-13, re-confirmed this run) building a **wide-field vis-OCT scanner with deep-learning arterial/venous sO₂ estimation** for retinopathy-of-prematurity / ischemic-retinal-disease risk is the funded continuation of this line — a grant, not a paper, confirming the wide-field + deep-learning direction is resourced and still described as preclinical.

### 2.5 — Recording Skin Oxygenation by Dual-Wavelength Ultra-Wideband Optoacoustic Mesoscopy (DW-RSOM) *(previously surfaced — first surfaced 2026-10-15; carried unchanged)*
- **Authors:** ⚠ search-derived — Aguirre / Berezhnoi / Ntziachristos and colleagues (TUM / Helmholtz Munich RSOM group).
- **Year / Venue:** 2026 · ***npj Imaging*** (Nature portfolio); DOI **10.1038/s44303-026-00184-5**; PubMed **42432228** (2026 Jul 10;4(1):51); PMC **13415847**. ⚠ egress-blocked; metadata from prior-run Nature + PubMed snippets.
- **Link:** https://www.nature.com/articles/s44303-026-00184-5 · https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42432228/ · https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13415847/
- **Relevance:** **7/10** — human, in-vivo, non-invasive optoacoustic imaging that **explicitly resolves venule-compartment oxygenation**. *Flagged:* **microvascular / skin venule-level sO₂**, not a named-vein SvO₂/ScvO₂ — closer to a vessel-resolved StO₂.
- **Novelty:** First dual-wavelength (**515 nm + 532 nm**, two diode-pumped solid-state lasers) ultra-wideband raster-scan optoacoustic mesoscopy — prior RSOM was single-wavelength. In 6 healthy volunteers during post-occlusive reactive hyperemia and a vascular occlusion test, maps microvascular sO₂ at single-microvessel level, **thresholding the 515/532 nm absorption ratio at 0.9 to label vessels below as venules, above as arterioles**. Imaging-based mean sO₂ **80 ± 10 %** vs **87.5 ± 2.5 %** by blood-gas (p = 0.097); rose to **88 ± 7 %** after cuff release.
- **Integration insights:** The first corpus entry to perform **arteriole-vs-venule spectral separation at the microvascular scale** non-invasively — the fine-grained complement to the deep-vein PA anchors (§2.1–§2.3) and per-vessel retinal vis-OCT (§2.4). The 515/532 nm ratio rule is a concrete, transferable artery–vein disambiguation rule; its occlusion/hyperemia protocol is the same maneuver used to make veins readable in §1.5.

> **Standing lead — GenMC: Real-Time Generative Monte Carlo Surrogate for Quantitative Photoacoustic Imaging** *(first logged 09-27; dataset release logged 11-01; re-confirmed this run).* Shi, He, Vercauteren, Xia (King's College London). 2026 · preprint **arXiv:2609.28261**. PA fluence-quantification enabling quantitative venous sO₂ at depth; a generator–discriminator network predicts multi-wavelength fluence maps in ≈30 ms (vs ≈5 min for Monte-Carlo), used to fluence-compensate raw PA images before sO₂ calculation. ## Cross-topic synthesis

- **Eleventh consecutive sparse run; corpus stable.** Eight searches this run across all in-scope modalities surfaced **nothing new in any category** — no new paper, preprint, dataset, grant or registry record. The two topics continue to converge on one engineering problem — **separating the venous signal from the arterial/tissue background without a catheter** — attacked three ways: (a) make the vein pulsate and read it like a pulse oximeter (§1.1, §1.5, sternal §1.3); (b) image the vein directly and unmix per-vessel (§2.1 PA-IJV, §2.4 vis-OCT, §2.5 RSOM, TD-HSI); (c) use a *known* adjacent artery as an in-situ fluence/spectral reference (§2.2 APM+). The artery-as-reference idea is the common thread linking the two flagship lines.
- **The two flagship lines are both resourced and both still preclinical/proof-of-concept.** (1) **PA internal-jugular + carotid → cerebral O₂ extraction / CMRO₂** (§2.1 anchor → §2.2 APM+ method → §1.1 wearable endpoint), now funded by NIH **K99EB035645**. (2) **Wide-field vis-OCT with deep-learning per-vessel A/V sO₂** (§2.4), funded by the OHSU **R01**. Both grants are standing context, not new this run.
- **Consistent ~70–72 % human venous-sO₂ convergence** across independent modalities remains the strongest cross-validation signal in the corpus: PA-IJV **72 ± 7 %** (§2.1), APM+ radial-vein **72.3 %** (§2.2), vis-OCT retinal vein **≈72 %** (§2.4), GenMC fluence-corrected **≈78 %** (standing lead). Uncorrected linear unmixing still overestimates wildly (GenMC's ≈99.98 % without fluence correction) — the quantitative case for the fluence-correction and artery-reference methods that dominate Topic 2.
- **Realistic accuracy bar is trend-grade, not absolute.** The best non-invasive methods cluster at a few-percent bias with wide limits of agreement (jugular NIRS LoA ≈ ±15–19 %, §1.2; sternal PPG ~87 % concordance, §1.3). Clinically actionable today is **low-SvO₂ discrimination** (cal-rScO₂ AUC 0.970 for SvO₂ < 65 %, §1.2), not catheter-replacement absolute accuracy.
- **Honesty note.** Per the brief, this file is **not padded** with arterial-only SpO₂ work to reach five per topic. Genuine non-invasive *venous* optical work is a low-publication-volume niche; the honest corpus already exceeds five strong entries per topic, every ranked entry is a re-verified prior-surfaced paper with an honest score, and no new venous-specific publication appeared in the scan window.
