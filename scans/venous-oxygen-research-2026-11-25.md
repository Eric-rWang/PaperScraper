### 1.1 — Non-invasive, Continuous Venous Oxygen Saturation and Oxygen Extraction Estimation from the Internal Jugular Vein *(previously surfaced — the flagship new-generation true-venous device; re-confirmed this run)*
- **Authors:** ⚠ search-derived (bioengineering group associated with the jugular-optical-sensing line).
- **Year / Venue:** 2025 · **IEEE EMBC 2025** proceedings. IEEE Xplore doc 11254941; PubMed 41335695; DOI 10.1109/EMBC58623.2025.11254941.
- **Link:** https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/11254941/ · https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41335695/ · https://doi.org/10.1109/embc58623.2025.11254941
- **Relevance:** **8.5/10** — directly targets non-invasive, continuous **true venous** sO₂ at the IJV plus cerebral O₂ extraction.
- **Novelty:** Flexible/conformal neck sensor array reads the **IJV pulse + breathing-induced modulation** (peripheral veins being non-pulsatile) to estimate SvO₂ ≈ **65.7–80.9 %** (SD 1.67 % for IJV-pulse, 4.26 % for breathing modulation) and a per-subject **cerebral O₂-extraction ratio (O₂ER)** in 6 healthy adults; co-registers carotid artery and IJV at one neck site. Authors flag artery–vein **crosstalk mitigation** and larger-cohort blood-gas validation as next steps.
- **Integration insights:** The extraction-aware wearable endpoint of the whole Topic-1 program; the artery serves as an in-situ reference (same logic as APM+, §2.2). Sibling registry trials remain the independent clinical-translation counterparts: **NCT06004466** (National Taiwan University Hospital, plethysmographic IJV device, 100 planned participants), **ACTRN12625000866404** (Australian flexible optical IJV sensor vs venous blood gas, with oxygen-extraction-ratio agreement as an outcome), and **NCT06072755** (§1.NEW, U. Wisconsin commercial-pulse-oximeter neck probe) — no published results yet for any. The UT-Austin **wearable optical e-tattoo** (2023 EMBC 10.1109/embc40787.2023.10340010; 2025 patent US2025/0025074) and the **Vanderbilt 48-channel PPG e-tattoo** are conformal-array siblings pursuing the same neck artery–vein pair. The De Gruyter jugular-optical probe (cdbme-2024-2072) is the proof-of-concept external-jugular analogue. **Standing context:** NIH **K99EB035645** (carotid+IJV → OEF/CMRO₂) is the funded continuation via the §2.1 PA route — a grant, not a paper.

### 1.2 — Near-Infrared Spectroscopy for Estimating Mixed Venous Oxygen Saturation and Cardiac Output During Off-Pump Coronary Artery Bypass Grafting: A Prospective Observational Study *(previously surfaced — first logged 2026-09-08; re-confirmed this run via PubMed snippet)*
- **Authors:** Oh et al. ⚠ author line search-derived — not page-confirmed (PubMed/Springer egress-blocked).
- **Year / Venue:** 2026 · ***Journal of Clinical Monitoring and Computing*** (Springer); DOI **10.1007/s10877-026-01477-1**. PubMed 42606675.
- **Link / DOI:** https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10877-026-01477-1 · https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42606675/
- **Relevance:** **7.5/10** — non-invasive SvO₂ surrogate via **jugular + cerebral NIRS**, validated head-to-head against **pulmonary-artery-catheter SvO₂** in adults.
- **Novelty:** Tests two non-invasive NIRS surrogates in 25 OPCAB patients — (a) **rSjvO₂** over the left IJV (bias 2.0 %, wide LoA −14.9/+18.9 %) and (b) **baseline-calibrated cerebral NIRS (cal-rScO₂)** on the forehead (bias 0.8 %, LoA −7.8/+9.4 %); the calibrated cerebral measure reached **AUC 0.970 for detecting SvO₂ < 65 %** (sensitivity 95.9 %, specificity 89.8 %) with good trending ability, usable after a single-point calibration. **NIRS-derived cardiac output failed** (≈33 % error).
- **Integration insights:** The most rigorous 2026 adult validation yet of jugular/cerebral NIRS as an SvO₂ *trend* monitor. Baseline-calibrated cerebral NIRS **out-tracked a sensor placed right over the IJV** — a strong argument for the vessel-*resolving* imaging of Topic 2 over surface averaging. A 2026 *J Pediatr* study (PubMed 42476299, single-ventricle children) independently found **frequency-domain NIRS StO₂ strongly correlated with invasive SvO₂ (R = 0.74, bias −2.8 %)** while continuous-wave NIRS did not (R = 0.29, n.s.) — a clean demonstration that NIRS *method* (FD vs CW) governs SvO₂-tracking fidelity. Clean SvO₂ < 65 % discrimination is the clinically actionable low-SvO₂-alarm endpoint.

- **Relevance:** **8/10** — non-invasive, continuous **true mixed-venous SvO₂** with a pulmonary-artery-catheter reference; a genuinely new anatomical site (sternum) for venous oximetry.

### 1.5 — Proof-of-Concept Non-Invasive Estimation of Peripheral Venous Oxygen Saturation (arterio-venous compliance pulse-modulation) *(previously surfaced — foundational method ancestor; re-confirmed this run via PMC snippet)*
- **Year / Venue:** 2017 · *BioMedical Engineering OnLine* 16:60; DOI 10.1186/s12938-017-0351-x; PMC5437414.
- **Link:** https://biomedical-engineering-online.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/s12938-017-0351-x · https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5437414/
- **Relevance:** **7/10** — the original non-invasive peripheral-venous (SpvO₂) optical concept.
- **Novelty:** Uses the **compliance difference between arteries and veins** to induce artificial respiration-like modulation that makes venous blood pulsatile, then reads the induced PPG to build a calibration model for regional venous sO₂; validated in 8 healthy volunteers with arm-lift/occlusion maneuvers against blood-gas references (reported r² ≈ 0.95 between the venous-modulation ratio and measured SvO₂). Estimated SpvO₂ ran ≈3.1 ± 4.2 % below arterial saturation. (Siblings re-hit this run: the EMBC digit-cuff venous-modulation paper 10.1109/embc.2015.7319858; 10.1186/s12938-016-0208-8 / PMC4952148; the 2007 Echiadis finger-pulse paper, PubMed 17664681; the PMC5655584 co-oximetry comparison — weak correlation r ≈ 0.10, "significant refinement needed".)
- **Integration insights:** The method ancestor of the §1.3 sternal-PPG and §1.1/§1.NEW IJV-pulse devices, and the conceptual grandparent of the §2.1 wearable VO₂/O₂-extraction PPG line — every "make the vein pulsatile (or exploit a DC/perfusion component), then read it like a pulse oximeter" approach traces here. The artery–vein crosstalk problem it first exposed is the same one the §1.1 e-tattoo and the Topic-2 spectral-ratio methods now attack.

### 1.6 — Multi-Optical Sensing for Estimating SvO₂ and Hematocrit Trends in Extracorporeal Circulation Tubing *(previously surfaced — first logged 2026-11-21; re-confirmed this run)*
- **Authors:** Yin et al. ⚠ full author line search-derived — not page-confirmed (egress-blocked).
- **Year / Venue:** 2026 · ***Optics Express*** **34**(23):44351–44364; DOI **10.1364/OE.615247**; PubMed **43040720**.
- **Link / DOI:** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43040720 · https://doi.org/10.1364/OE.615247 · https://opg.optica.org/oe/abstract.cfm?doi=10.1364/OE.615247
- **Relevance:** **4.5/10** — a **true venous SvO₂** optical measurement with an explicit drift-correction ML method, but ⚠ **in-line extracorporeal (clamp-on CPB/ECMO tubing), not a transcutaneous in-vivo venous monitor.** Included because it is SvO₂-specific and its multi-wavelength + attention-CNN approach is directly transferable to wearable venous oximetry; honestly scored below the in-vivo anchors.
- **Novelty:** A **clamp-on, four-wavelength transmissive** optical front-end paired with a **channel-attention multi-task 1D-CNN** estimates venous SvO₂ and hematocrit (HCT) trends non-invasively through the wall of extracorporeal circulation tubing. System-level internal testing: **R² = 0.986 (SvO₂) / 0.936 (HCT)**, MAE 1.00 % / 0.90 %; the deep-learning model on 63 paired records / 1,036 signal windows reached **R² = 0.999 / 0.996**, MAE 0.42 % / 0.34 %. Critically, **fusing multiple signal components reduced SvO₂ drift over a 6-hour monitoring window** vs baseline optical signals alone. ⚠ Headline R² from a small internal dataset (63 records); no independent external validation.
- **Integration insights:** The method — **multi-wavelength optical + attention-based multi-task CNN with explicit long-horizon drift correction** — attacks exactly the baseline-drift/calibration-stability problem that blocks continuous *wearable* venous oximetry (§1.1 IJV e-tattoo, §1.3 sternal PPG). The CPB/ECMO venous-return line is a clean, artifact-free testbed for the spectral-ratio → SvO₂ inversion that in-vivo transcutaneous devices must perform through confounding tissue. The extracorporeal counterpart of the JTEHM in-line sensor (PubMed 41668774), but adds venous-specific SvO₂ focus, multi-task HCT co-estimation and the drift-reduction result. **Watch for in-vivo transcutaneous translation and external validation; treat as a transferable-technique entry, not a non-invasive in-vivo venous measurement.**

## Cross-topic synthesis

- **A two-day window with zero new in-scope items — the expected cadence for this niche.** Thirteen searches surfaced only identifiers already in the ≈364-strong corpus. This is not a search failure: genuine non-invasive **venous** (SvO₂/ScvO₂) optical work is published at low volume, and the previous scan (2026-11-23) already logged the one recent new item (TOUCAN-1). The ranked top-5 of both topics are unchanged and re-verified via search snippets.
- **The fluence/scattering bottleneck remains the field's center of gravity.** APM+ (§2.2, arterial-reference fluence correction, naming UOT/DOT as adjuncts), GenMC and Hybrid-Net (learned fluence), dcBLL (diffusion-corrected Beer-Lambert), and the §2.5 random-matrix multiple-scattering gating all solve one problem: light transport corrupts the raw spectral signal, and uncorrected linear unmixing overestimates venous sO₂ (often non-physically >100 %). This run re-confirmed the APM+ numbers that make the point concrete — **linear unmixing 84 % vs APM+ 72 % venous** at the same radial artery–vein pair. Every credible venous-oximetry advance carries an explicit fluence/scattering-correction step.
- **The off-the-shelf-hardware / wearable angle is a persistent theme.** TOUCAN-1 repurposes two commercial pulse oximeters (finger + neck) to the IJV; §2.6 uses a three-LED/one-photodiode StO₂ front-end; the §1.1 e-tattoo and §1.3 sternal PPG pursue the same cheap-wearable endpoint. The common bottleneck these cheap devices face is the same one the expensive ones face — **separating venous from arterial and tissue signal**.
- **Topic 1 remains the true bottleneck.** No new **published** surface, named-vein, true-SvO₂/ScvO₂ method appeared — the clinically decisive niche (jugular/sternal/peripheral) is still carried by the §1.1–§1.6 corpus plus a thickening cluster of IJV registry trials (NCT06004466, ACTRN12625000866404, NCT06072755), all trend-monitors needing larger blood-gas validation / results publication. The honest state of the field is unchanged: **vessel-resolving / microvascular imaging (Topic 2) advances faster than surface absolute-SvO₂ sensing (Topic 1)**, and the §2.1 human-IJV PA measurement remains the one result that most directly bridges the two.
- **Convergent endpoint — oxygen extraction.** The §1.1 neck O₂ER device, the §1.NEW combined-arteriovenous VO₂ trial, the §2.2 artery-referenced APM+ (carotid–IJV + flow), the §2.3 PACT flow+sO₂ work, the MOBILE depth-resolved occlusion readout, the ULM+PA co-registration lead, and the fRSOM/DW-RSOM/§2.6 occlusion-challenge platforms all point at the same destination: **co-registered per-vessel flow and oxygenation → regional O₂ extraction / CMRO₂ / VO₂**, non-invasively — the field's real goal, beyond a single SvO₂ number.
