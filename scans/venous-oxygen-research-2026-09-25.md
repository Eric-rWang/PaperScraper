## Topic 1 — Non-invasive monitoring of venous blood oxygen (SvO₂ / ScvO₂ / peripheral venous)

### 1.1 — Non-invasive, Continuous Venous Oxygen Saturation and Oxygen Extraction Estimation from the Internal Jugular Vein *(previously surfaced — the flagship new-generation true-venous device)*
- **Authors:** ⚠ search-derived (bioengineering group associated with the jugular-optical-sensing line).
- **Year / Venue:** 2025 · **IEEE EMBC 2025** proceedings. IEEE Xplore doc 11254941; PubMed 41335695; DOI 10.1109/EMBC58623.2025.11254941. ⚠ egress-blocked; metadata cross-checked on ≥2 snippets.
- **Link:** https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/11254941/ · https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41335695/ · https://doi.org/10.1109/embc58623.2025.11254941
- **Relevance:** **8.5/10** — directly targets non-invasive, continuous **true venous** sO₂ at the IJV plus cerebral O₂ extraction.
- **Novelty:** Flexible/conformal neck sensor array reads the **IJV pulse + breathing-induced modulation** (peripheral veins being non-pulsatile) to estimate SvO₂ ≈ **65.7–80.9 %** (SD 1.67 % for IJV-pulse, 4.26 % for breathing modulation) and a per-subject **cerebral O₂-extraction ratio (O₂ER)** in 6 healthy adults; co-registers carotid artery and IJV at one neck site, working in both upright and supine positions. Authors explicitly flag artery–vein **crosstalk mitigation** and larger-cohort blood-gas validation as next steps.
- **Integration insights:** The extraction-aware wearable endpoint of the whole Topic-1 program; the artery serves as an in-situ reference (same logic as APM+, §2.2). §1.3 (sternal-marrow reservoir) and §1.2 (jugular/cerebral NIRS) are the alternative-site and surface-NIRS counterparts to this vessel-targeted pulse device. The retinal A-V paper (§2.5) is the ocular analogue of its cerebral-O₂ER endpoint — extraction read at a different accessible vascular bed.

### 1.2 — Near-Infrared Spectroscopy for Estimating Mixed Venous Oxygen Saturation and Cardiac Output During Off-Pump Coronary Artery Bypass Grafting: A Prospective Observational Study *(previously surfaced — first logged 2026-09-08)*
- **Authors:** ⚠ search-derived — not page-confirmed (PubMed egress-blocked).
- **Year / Venue:** 2026 · venue **not page-confirmed** (⚠ search-derived; anesthesia/monitoring journal). PubMed 42606675. (Search snippet dates the publication to August 2026.)
- **Link / DOI:** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42606675/ ⚠ egress-blocked; DOI not captured.
- **Relevance:** **7.5/10** — non-invasive SvO₂ surrogate via **jugular + cerebral NIRS**, validated head-to-head against **pulmonary-artery-catheter SvO₂** in adults.
- **Novelty:** Tests two non-invasive NIRS surrogates in 25 OPCAB patients — (a) **rSjvO₂** over the left IJV (bias 2.0 %, wide LoA −14.9/+18.9 %) and (b) **baseline-calibrated cerebral NIRS (cal-rScO₂)** on the forehead (bias 0.8 %, LoA −7.8/+9.4 %, good SvO₂ < 65 % discrimination and trending). **NIRS-derived cardiac output failed** (≈33 % error).
- **Integration insights:** The most rigorous 2026 adult validation yet of jugular/cerebral NIRS as an SvO₂ *trend* monitor. Tellingly, **baseline-calibrated cerebral NIRS out-tracked a sensor placed right over the IJV** — a strong argument for the vessel-*resolving* imaging of Topic 2 (PA, vis-OCT) over surface averaging. Clean SvO₂ < 65 % discrimination is the clinically actionable low-SvO₂-alarm endpoint. Note the contrast with the retinal papers (§2.5/§2.6): NIRS-derived *cardiac output* failed here, whereas the retinal A-V *difference* tracked cardiac output — the extraction ratio may carry the hemodynamic signal that absolute NIRS StO₂ does not.

### 1.3 — Monitoring Changes in Mixed Venous Oxygen Saturation Using Photoplethysmography on the Sternum — A Proof of Concept Study *(previously surfaced — first logged 2026-09-08)*
- **Authors:** ⚠ search-derived — not page-confirmed (PubMed egress-blocked). Likely the Scandinavian intraosseous-sternal-PPG group behind the earlier porcine sternal-oximetry work (*J. Clin. Monit. Comput.* 10.1007/s10877-023-00980-z).
- **Year / Venue:** 2026 · ***Medical & Biological Engineering & Computing*** (Springer); DOI **10.1007/s11517-026-03648-3**. PubMed 42527850.
- **Link / DOI:** https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11517-026-03648-3 · https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42527850/ ⚠ egress-blocked; metadata via index snippet.
- **Relevance:** **8/10** — non-invasive, continuous **true mixed-venous SvO₂** with a pulmonary-artery-catheter reference; a genuinely new anatomical site (sternum) for venous oximetry.
- **Novelty:** Exploits **sternal intramedullary (intraosseous) blood**, whose saturation tracks central/mixed-venous saturation, read **transcutaneously with a PPG probe** on the sternum. In anesthetized pigs, SvO₂ was driven down by stepwise hypoxia and staged hemorrhage while the non-invasive sternal signal (nSsO₂) was calibrated against pulmonary-artery SvO₂: **linear nSsO₂–SvO₂ correlation, ~87 % concordance** for tracking SvO₂ changes.
- **Integration insights:** A distinct physical route to non-invasive venous sO₂: the **bone marrow acts as a naturally venous-weighted optical reservoir**, sidestepping the artery–vein disambiguation problem. Trend-grade (~87 %) performance sets a realistic accuracy bar; a natural fusion target for the pulse-modulation math of §1.5.

### 1.5 — Proof-of-Concept Non-Invasive Estimation of Peripheral Venous Oxygen Saturation (arterio-venous compliance pulse-modulation) *(previously surfaced — foundational method ancestor)*
- **Year / Venue:** 2017 · *BioMedical Engineering OnLine* 16:60; DOI 10.1186/s12938-017-0351-x; PMC5437414.
- **Link:** https://biomedical-engineering-online.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/s12938-017-0351-x
- **Relevance:** **7/10** — the original non-invasive peripheral-venous (SpvO₂) optical concept.
- **Novelty:** Uses the **compliance difference between arteries and veins** to induce artificial respiration-like modulation that makes venous blood pulsatile, then reads the induced PPG to build a calibration model for regional venous sO₂; validated in 8 healthy volunteers with arm-lift/occlusion maneuvers against blood-gas references. Estimated SpvO₂ ran ≈3.1 ± 4.2 % below arterial saturation. (Its earlier sibling — *A new method for noninvasive venous blood oxygen detection*, 10.1186/s12938-016-0208-8, 2016 — and the 2007 during-bypass venous-oximetry paper, PubMed 17664681, both in-corpus.)
- **Integration insights:** The method ancestor of the §1.3 sternal-PPG and §1.1 IJV-pulse devices — every "make the vein pulsatile, then read it like a pulse oximeter" approach traces here. Its modest accuracy is exactly what the deep-diffuse-optics pulsation-decomposition ideas in Topic 2 aim to improve.

> **Non-optical non-invasive venous anchor (not ranked, in corpus):** *Development and Validation of a Noninvasive Model of Mixed Venous Oxygen Saturation in Heart Failure* (McDiarmid et al.; *JACC: Advances* 5(1):102484, Jan 2026; 10.1016/j.jacadv.2025.102484; PMC12869880; PubMed 41609283). **Cardiac-MRI T₂ mapping** yields imaging-derived SvO₂ (iSvO₂) validated in a 628-patient HF cohort — non-invasive and *true mixed-venous*, but MRI-based, not optical. First logged 06-11.
> **Second IJV optical device (not ranked, in corpus):** *Estimation of Venous Oxygen Saturation Through Non-Invasive Optical Sensing at the Jugular Veins* (De Gruyter, *Current Directions in Biomedical Engineering* 2024; 10.1515/cdbme-2024-2072). Pulse-oximeter-like probe over the **external jugular vein**; correctly identified the EJV waveform and returned SvO₂ ≈ 70.4–75.3 % in 3 subjects — the earlier sibling of §1.1. Re-surfaced this run; already logged.
> **DNN + Monte-Carlo IJV NIRS (not ranked, in corpus):** *Quantifying Changes in Oxygen Saturation of the Internal Jugular Vein In Vivo Using Deep Neural Networks and Subject-Specific 3D Monte Carlo Models* (*Optics Letters* 49(10):2669, 2024). Multi-channel NIRS + per-subject Monte-Carlo model to track SijvO₂ changes non-invasively — the model-based cousin of §1.1. Re-surfaced this run; already logged.
> **Wearable venous-optics anchor (not ranked, in corpus):** *Towards Simultaneous Non-Invasive Arterial and Venous Oxygenation Monitoring with a Wearable Optical E-Tattoo* (Tan et al., UT Austin / Lu group; IEEE EMBC 2023, doc 10340010; PubMed 38083768; patent US2025/0025074). Wrist e-tattoo measuring artery + vein pulses simultaneously; addresses artery/vein **crosstalk via spatial filtering**. Re-surfaced this run; already logged.
> **Device/regulatory anchor (not ranked):** *Noninvasix LIVOx optoacoustic central venous oxygenation monitor* (ScvO₂ for septic shock; FDA Breakthrough Device Designation, 2022; chest-worn optoacoustic probe). Re-checked this run — no 2026 peer-reviewed validation data yet surfaced; watch.

### 2.1 — Noninvasive In Vivo Photoacoustic Measurement of Internal Jugular Venous Oxygenation in Humans *(previously surfaced — the Topic-2 anchor)*
- **Year / Venue:** 2023 · preprint arXiv:2303.10775 (+ SPIE lineage). ⚠ arXiv egress-blocked; abstract re-confirmed on two index snippets.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.10775
- **Relevance:** **9/10** — human, in vivo, PA, *venous* (IJV).
- **Novelty:** Dual-wavelength PAT + ultrasound images the IJV in **7 healthy volunteers** at 5 fps; average IJV sO₂ ≈ **72 ± 7 %** (range 62–79 %), with US co-registration enabling temporal sO₂ tracking — true venous oximetry at depth, non-invasively.
- **Integration insights:** Proof that PA reaches a clinically relevant central vein through intact tissue; the imaging counterpart to the §1.1 pulse-based IJV device — same vessel, different sensing physics.

### 2.2 — Arterial Blood-Mediated Deep-Tissue Photoacoustic Oximetry (APM+): In-Vivo Human Radial-Vein sO₂ *(previously surfaced)*
- **Authors:** Sastry, Olick-Gibson et al. (Caltech Optical Imaging Laboratory, L.V. Wang group).
- **Year / Venue:** 2026 · *Advanced Science* (Wiley), 10.1002/advs.76366; PMC13334587; PubMed 42397001. ⚠ egress-blocked; abstract re-confirmed via index snippet.
- **Link:** https://advanced.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/advs.76366 · https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13334587/
- **Relevance:** **9/10** — solves the fluence problem for deep-tissue PA sO₂; reports in-vivo human values on the radial artery–vein pair (n = 8).
- **Novelty:** Uses high, known arterial oxygenation as a local in-situ **fluence reference** to correct spectral coloring, cutting median sO₂ error to **2.9 %** (vs 9.8 % for linear unmixing); human **venous sO₂ median 72.3 %** (IQR 8.9 %, vs 34.4 % for linear unmixing) on the radial vein, robust through ex-vivo tissue up to 1.5 cm. Recovered the parabolic laminar-flow wavefront inside a deep vein in vivo in real time.
- **Integration insights:** Directly attacks the single biggest barrier to quantitative deep venous PA — unknown local fluence — using the artery as a self-calibrator, the same artery-as-reference logic §1.1 applies in the pulse domain.

### 2.4 — Adaptive Spectroscopic Visible-Light OCT for Clinical Retinal Oximetry (ADS-vis-OCT) *(previously surfaced)*
- **Authors:** Chen Q., Nolen S.L., Spencer S.A., Yi J. (Johns Hopkins).
- **Year / Venue:** 2023 · *Communications Medicine* (Nature), s43856-023-00288-8; PubMed 37095177; PMC10126115.
- **Link:** https://www.nature.com/articles/s43856-023-00288-8 ⚠ egress-blocked; metadata via snippet.
- **Relevance:** **8/10** — resolves *per-vessel arterial and venous* retinal sO₂ non-invasively.
- **Novelty:** Adaptively removes vessel-specific spectral contaminants; RMSE ≈ 2.1 % vs pulse oximeter in major arteries, repeatability SD ≈ 2.3 % in *veins*; ex-vivo phantom agreement within 1 % bias across 0–100 % sO₂. Demonstrated across glaucoma, ischemia, DR, CRVO, sickle-cell retinopathy.
- **Integration insights:** Sets the accuracy ceiling for optical venous oximetry in an accessible vascular bed; the adaptive spectral-correction idea is transferable to PA/DOT venous inversion where spectral coloring dominates error (cf. §2.2). Its compute cost is directly addressed by the DGFT acceleration paper (standing lead below, same group). The retinal papers (§2.5, §2.6) are the clinical-application counterparts in the same vascular bed — lower-tech (dual-wavelength fundus) routes to a per-vessel arterial+venous readout.

### 2.5 — Retinal Arteriovenous Oxygen Saturation Difference Is Inversely Associated with Cardiac Output Measured by Echocardiography: A Non-Invasive Pilot Study *(previously surfaced — first logged 2026-09-23 · per-vessel retinal venous oximetry, clinical-correlation application)*
- **Authors:** Křivohlavý T., Lada M., Malý M., et al. ⚠ search-derived (Military University Hospital Prague, Dept. of Internal Medicine + Dept. of Ophthalmology). Recruitment Sept 2024–Jan 2026; final analysis n = 14 with complete retinal-oximetry + echo data (cross-confirmed on index snippet this run).
- **Year / Venue:** 2026 · ***Scientific Reports*** 16:26403; DOI **10.1038/s41598-026-56918-3**; single-centre prospective observational pilot. ⚠ nature.com egress-blocked; metadata cross-checked on ≥2 index snippets.
- **Link / DOI:** https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-026-56918-3 · https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-026-56918-3
- **Relevance:** **6.5/10** — measures **true per-vessel retinal venous sO₂** non-invasively (not tissue-level StO₂), and its **arteriovenous saturation difference is an oxygen-extraction proxy** tied to systemic hemodynamics. Scored below the four PA/vis-OCT anchors because the imaging *technique* (dual-wavelength fundus oximetry, Oxymap T1) is well-established and the study is a small clinical-correlation pilot rather than a new measurement modality; scored above §2.6 because its A-V-difference framing ties venous readout directly to a hemodynamic endpoint (cardiac output).
- **Novelty:** Non-invasive **dual-wavelength retinal (fundus) oximetry** (Oxymap T1; ~570 nm insensitive / ~600 nm oxygen-sensitive) measures per-vessel retinal arterial and venous saturation within a peripapillary annulus (1.5–3× disc diameter); the **retinal A-V oxygen-saturation difference** — a marker of retinal oxygen extraction — is shown to be **inversely associated with echocardiography-measured cardiac output**. Builds on the Van Keer et al. line (*Acta Ophthalmol.* 2019, PubMed 30280510); historical anchor is the 2001 hemorrhage/resuscitation study (*J. Appl. Physiol.*, 10.1152/japplphysiol.01197.2001).
- **Integration insights:** (1) **The A-V difference is the ocular analogue of the cerebral O₂-extraction ratio** that the §1.1 IJV device targets and of the microvascular-vs-macrovascular extraction gap that SFDI (standing lead) maps — extraction, not absolute SvO₂, is emerging as the shared clinically-actionable readout across accessible vascular beds. (2) A **low-cost, widely-deployable counterweight to the high-end §2.4 vis-OCT** in the same retinal bed. (3) Contrasts with §1.2, where NIRS-derived *cardiac output* failed while NIRS SvO₂-trend held — here the venous-weighted *extraction* metric is what tracks cardiac output. Caveat: retinal saturation is a regional CNS-bed measure, a proxy for — not a direct read of — central/mixed venous SvO₂. Small cohort (n = 14).

### ★ 2.6 — Retinal Vessel Oxygen Saturation Is Affected in Multiple Sclerosis and Optic Neuritis *(NEW this run — per-vessel retinal venous oximetry, disease-biomarker application)*
- **Authors:** Einarsdottir A.B. (Einarsdottir et al.) — Dept. of Ophthalmology, Odense University Hospital, Denmark. ⚠ full author list search-derived (Wiley/PMC egress-blocked).
- **Year / Venue:** 2026 · ***Acta Ophthalmologica*** **104**:700–704; DOI **10.1111/aos.70121**; PubMed **41773828**; PMC **13460377**. ⚠ metadata cross-checked on ≥2 index snippets (Wiley + PMC), not page-read.
- **Link / DOI:** https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/aos.70121 · https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13460377/ · https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41773828/
- **Relevance:** **5.5/10** — reports **true per-vessel retinal venular sO₂** non-invasively (not tissue-level StO₂), but the study's purpose is an **MS/optic-neuritis disease biomarker**, not venous-oxygen monitoring; the imaging technique (spectrophotometric dual-wavelength retinal oximetry, Oxymap-type) is well-established. Scored just below §2.5 because it lacks the explicit venous↔systemic-hemodynamic (extraction/cardiac-output) framing that makes §2.5 more directly informative for the monitoring goal.
- **Novelty:** Non-invasive **spectrophotometric retinal oximetry** measures haemoglobin sO₂ in retinal arterioles **and venules** in **29 MS patients** (12 with a history of optic neuritis) vs healthy controls / unaffected fellow eyes. Finds retinal vessel oxygen saturation is **altered in MS and in optic-neuritis eyes** — i.e., a measurable metabolic/oxygenation signature in an accessible CNS vascular bed. Adds a fresh disease cohort to the retinal-venular-oximetry evidence base (cf. the 2020 MS/ON follow-up, PMC7754403).
- **Integration insights:** (1) A third 2026 data point (with §2.5 and the fundus-camera instrument paper, standing lead) confirming that **per-vessel retinal venular sO₂ is a routinely-obtainable non-invasive readout** — the accessible-bed foundation the program leans on. (2) Reinforces the retina as a *CNS oxygen-extraction* window: like §2.5's cerebral-hemodynamic link and §1.1's cerebral O₂ER, it reads venous-side oxygenation at a brain-adjacent bed, here as a neuro-inflammatory biomarker. (3) Its use of a commercial spectrophotometric oximeter re-underscores the accessibility-vs-accuracy trade-off against §2.4 vis-OCT — the cruder fundus method is clinically deployable today but does not resolve capillary-level or deep-vein sO₂. Caveat: regional retinal measure, small cohort, disease-association design (not a monitoring validation).

## Cross-topic synthesis

**Headline (2026-09-25):** One genuinely new in-scope paper (§2.6 — Einarsdottir et al., retinal vessel oximetry in MS/optic neuritis, *Acta Ophthalmol.* 104:700–704) and one out-of-scope hit checked and excluded (Usatinsky et al.'s implantable tissue-StO₂ oximeter). The new item is a modest disease-biomarker *application* of established dual-wavelength retinal oximetry — real, in-scope (per-vessel venular sO₂, non-invasive), but not a new measurement modality, hence an honest 5.5/10. **Topic 1 was quiet** (no new true-venous paper). Both topics' ranked fives are carried forward from 09-24 unchanged; §2.6 is appended below them at its honest rank.

**A three-paper retinal cluster is now visible.** Within two weeks the scan has logged three 2026 retinal-venular-oximetry items — the fundus A-V-difference↔cardiac-output pilot (§2.5), the conventional-fundus-camera instrument paper (standing lead), and now the MS/ON biomarker study (§2.6) — all resting on the same commercial dual-wavelength spectrophotometric platform. Together they establish that **per-vessel retinal venular sO₂ is a routinely-obtainable, non-invasive, clinically-deployable readout today**, and that the retina functions as a *CNS oxygen-extraction window* (echoing §1.1's cerebral O₂ER). The trade-off they collectively expose is accessibility vs. accuracy: none resolves capillary-level or deep-vein sO₂ the way §2.4 vis-OCT or §2.2 PA can, but all are usable in a clinic now.

**State of the field, restated.** The corpus continues to cluster around one structural problem — **compartment disambiguation**, separating the venous signal from arterial and tissue background — attacked at four scales: (1) **single-vessel resolution** (vis-OCT §2.4, PACT §2.3, TD-HSI, fundus-camera/retinal-HSI methods, and the §2.5/§2.6 retinal applications) resolves the vein directly; (2) **temporal isolation** (respiration-modulated NIRS §1.2, IJV-pulse device §1.1, the 2017 compliance-modulation ancestor §1.5) separates venous pulsation in time; (3) **an anatomical venous reservoir** (sternal marrow §1.3; portal-venous liver) sidesteps disambiguation by reading a naturally venous-weighted source; and (4) **spatial-heterogeneity mapping** (SFDI VOT). APM+ (§2.2) cuts across all four via the artery as an in-situ fluence anchor. Today's excluded implantable device (Usatinsky) is a reminder that the *blood-volume-change* problem — decoupling optical sO₂ from perfusion/volume swings — is the same compartment problem seen from the hardware side, and is being solved for tissue StO₂ before it is solved non-invasively for the vein.

**The recurring theme.** **Oxygen *extraction* (the A-V difference), not absolute SvO₂, is emerging as the shared, clinically-actionable readout** across accessible vascular beds: the §1.1 IJV device reports a cerebral O₂-extraction ratio; SFDI's VOT reads a microvascular-vs-macrovascular extraction gap; the §2.5 retinal A-V difference tracks cardiac output where NIRS-derived cardiac output failed (§1.2); and §2.6 shows the same retinal venular readout carries a neuro-inflammatory disease signature.

**Bottom line for the program.** The enabling-technology layer — throughput (DGFT), instruments (fiber-array PACT, ULM+PA matrix array, wearable FD-NIRS, fundus oximetry), ground-truth (dye/sulfate phantoms, IPCR), learned inversion (Hybrid-Net, PINN), and fluence/depth correction (ISDC, maximum-fluence, FD-PAM) — is well-populated, and the *retinal clinical-application* layer is now filling in fast (three items in two weeks). The scarce, still-awaited item remains a genuinely new venous *measurement modality* — a new way to read the vein, not another arterial-SpO₂ method, implantable tissue oximeter, enabler, phantom, or clinical correlation of an existing readout. New in-scope items are arriving roughly once every two-to-three scans; today's is a modest one, honestly reported rather than padded.
