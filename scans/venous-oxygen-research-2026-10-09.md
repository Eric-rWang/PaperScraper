### 2.1 — Noninvasive In Vivo Photoacoustic Measurement of Internal Jugular Venous Oxygenation in Humans *(previously surfaced — the Topic-2 anchor)*

### 2.2 — Arterial Blood-Mediated Deep-Tissue Photoacoustic Oximetry (APM+): In-Vivo Human Radial-Vein sO₂ *(previously surfaced)*
- **Authors:** Sastry, Olick-Gibson et al. (Caltech Optical Imaging Laboratory, L.V. Wang group).
- **Year / Venue:** 2026 · *Advanced Science* (Wiley), 10.1002/advs.76366; PMC13334587; PubMed 42397001. ⚠ egress-blocked; abstract re-confirmed via Wiley + Caltech coilab PDF snippets this run.
- **Link:** https://advanced.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/advs.76366 · https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13334587/ · https://coilab.caltech.edu/documents/34878/Sastry-2026-AdvancedScience.pdf
- **Relevance:** **8.5/10** — human, in vivo, PA, radial artery–vein pair with *venous* sO₂ reported.
- **Novelty:** Leverages the known-high **arterial sO₂ to locally calibrate optical fluence** in tissue (APM+; "+" = intravascular fluence correction), circumventing wavelength-dependent spectral coloring; needs only two wavelengths and no hardware changes to an existing dual-wavelength PAT system, and accuracy is independent of imaging depth (given an artery at similar depth) and of skin tone (simulation). **Median estimation error 2.9 % vs 9.8 % for traditional linear unmixing (LUM)** in ex-vivo-tissue phantoms. **Explicit per-method venous sO₂ figures (captured 10-08): 98.5 % (LUM) / 77.3 % (APM) / 69.2 % (APM+)** — the intravascular fluence correction pulls an un-physiological ~98 % linear-unmixing venous estimate down into the physiological venous band. In 8 healthy adults, estimated venous sO₂ concentrated in the healthy 60–80 % band (median 72.3 %, IQR 8.9 %). Authors note most major arteries have an accompanying vein (brachial–basilic in the arm, carotid–IJV in the neck), explicitly scoping extension to those pairs.
- **Integration insights:** Directly attacks the spectral-coloring bottleneck that limits all deep PA venous oximetry; the arterial-prior idea is the physical-calibration counterpart to GenMC's learned-simulation fluence estimate (§2.5). The explicit neck-pair extension ties straight to the §2.1 PA-IJV paradigm and the §1.1 neck device. The 98.5→69.2 % LUM→APM+ venous swing is the single clearest corpus illustration of *why* uncorrected deep PA venous estimates are untrustworthy.

### 2.4 — Adaptive Spectroscopic Visible-Light OCT for Clinical Retinal Oximetry (ADS-vis-OCT) *(previously surfaced)*
- **Authors:** Chen Q., Nolen S.L., Spencer S.A., Yi J. (Johns Hopkins).
- **Year / Venue:** 2023 · *Communications Medicine* (Nature), s43856-023-00288-8; PubMed 37095177; PMC10126115.
- **Link:** https://www.nature.com/articles/s43856-023-00288-8 · https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10126115 ⚠ egress-blocked; metadata via snippet.

### 2.5 — GenMC: Real-Time Generative Monte Carlo Surrogate for Quantitative Photoacoustic Imaging *(previously surfaced — first logged 09-27; PA fluence-correction with in-vivo venous sO₂ and skin-tone-bias correction)*
- **Authors:** Mengjie Shi, Feng He, Tom Vercauteren, Wenfeng Xia (King's College London). ⚠ author list search-derived (arXiv egress-blocked; cross-confirmed on ≥2 snippets).
- **Year / Venue:** 2026 · preprint **arXiv:2609.28261** (physics.med-ph), submitted Sept 2026. ⚠ arXiv egress-blocked; abstract cross-checked on ≥2 index snippets (re-confirmed this run via arXiv PDF snippet).
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.28261 · https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.28261
- **Relevance:** **7.5/10** — a genuinely new PA *fluence-quantification method* directly enabling quantitative venous sO₂ at depth; demonstrates in-vivo **venous oxygenation ≈ 74.49–77.93 %** and explicitly corrects a bias that most inflates *venous* estimates. Validated in blood-mimicking phantoms and **37 human volunteers spanning Fitzpatrick skin types III–V**. Scored below the four PA/vis-OCT anchors because it is a preprint fluence-correction enabler rather than a dedicated venous-validation study, but above the retinal *application* papers because it advances the measurement modality itself and reports venous values.
- **Novelty:** A **conditional GAN** learns tissue optical **fluence distributions** from anatomy + literature optical properties, with **anatomical priors from co-registered ultrasound**. Trained on Monte-Carlo synthetic data, it yields fluence maps in **< 30 ms/frame (~4 orders faster than conventional MC)**, enabling per-frame fluence compensation in real time. It targets the **melanin problem** — elevated melanin in darker skin causes superficial attenuation/clutter that **systematically overestimates venous and arterial sO₂** — and mitigates it in vivo. Re-confirmed this run: without correction, conventional linear unmixing produces non-physical sO₂ > 100 % for both venous and arterial targets, which the method's energy-normalisation approach addresses.
- **Integration insights:** (1) Attacks the **same fluence/spectral-coloring bottleneck as APM+ (§2.2)** but by a learned-simulation route driven by co-registered US anatomy — the US-guided geometry the §2.1 human-IJV paradigm and the NTUH NCT06004466 device already use. (2) Its real-time budget is the throughput analogue of the DGFT vis-OCT accelerator. (3) The **skin-tone-bias correction** is the single most program-relevant contribution — equitable venous oximetry across skin tones — addressed here *for the venous compartment specifically*. Caveats: preprint, venous values are in-vivo demonstrations not blood-gas-referenced, and it is a quantification method, not a standalone monitor.

## Cross-Topic Synthesis

- **Nothing new in the one-day window.** This is the eighth consecutive run (09-28, 09-29, 10-01, 10-02, 10-04, 10-06, 10-08, 10-09) with no newly *published* in-scope optical paper (the 10-01 CANVOS and sublingual-vein entries were newly *surfaced* older work). Today's scan adds only a *registry refinement* — a clearer design description for the NTUH plethysmographic IJV-oximetry trial (NCT06004466: US imaging + tissue optical properties + per-subject scattering coefficient) — not a new paper. The honest reading is that non-invasive *true-venous* optical oximetry remains a low-volume niche, not that the search missed something. No padding with arterial-only SpO₂ work.
- **The two topics are converging on one problem: fluence/spectral-coloring correction for the venous compartment.** APM+ (§2.2) makes this vivid: uncorrected linear unmixing reports an impossible ~98 % venous sO₂, the arterial-prior + intravascular correction brings it to a physiological 69 %; GenMC (§2.5) independently reports LUM producing non-physical sO₂ > 100 % for venous targets before its energy-normalisation fix. GenMC (learned Monte-Carlo fluence + explicit melanin/venous-bias fix across Fitzpatrick III–V), dcBLL (analytic diffusion-corrected Beer-Lambert) and the Zhou-2020 diffusion-dipole baseline (re-surfaced this run) are the learned and analytic routes to the same bottleneck. Multiple independent attacks — physical, learned, analytic — on the error source that limits every deep PA and vis-OCT venous estimate. This is the most active methodological front and the one most likely to produce the next genuine advance.
- **Vessel-type disambiguation ("which vessel is the vein?") is the recurring structural problem** linking Topic-1 surface methods (§1.1 IJV-pulse; §1.2 jugular-NIRS, where baseline-calibrated *cerebral* NIRS out-tracked a sensor placed directly over the IJV; the e-tattoo artery–vein pairing; the OL 49(10):2669 multi-channel-NIRS SijvO₂ model) with Topic-2 imaging (§2.1 PA-IJV, §2.3 PACT flow-from-oxygenation, §2.4 per-vessel vis-OCT, TD-HSI single-vessel). The clinical lesson from §1.2 — that vessel-*resolving* imaging beats surface averaging — is the strategic case for Topic 2. Notably, the NTUH device and GenMC both solve this the same way: **co-registered ultrasound as the anatomical prior.**
- **Wearable/conformal form factor is the quietly recurring Topic-1 theme.** The §1.1 IJV flexible array, the UT-Austin 48-channel e-tattoo, and the miniaturized FD-DOS/FD-NIRS optode (now explicitly targeting a wearable source/detector) all push toward a skin-conformable, continuous, artery–vein-paired neck/wrist sensor — the physical embodiment the CANVOS trial is now testing clinically.
- **Clinical translation is the live edge.** Two registry-stage IJV trials (CANVOS ACTRN12625000866404; NTUH NCT06004466) are the first interventional/observational, blood-gas-referenced validations of the "optical over the IJV" paradigm that §1.1 and the De Gruyter EJV probe pursue. Their first results would be the most consequential near-term event for this corpus — worth watching across coming scans.
- **Accuracy bars to beat, from the corpus:** commercial jugular NIRS (Mespere VO100, §1.4; percentage error 8–14 %) and sternal-PPG trend-grade ~87 % concordance (§1.3) set the clinical floor; ADS-vis-OCT venous repeatability ~2.3 % (§2.4) and APM+ median error 2.9 % / physiological venous recovery (§2.2) set the imaging ceiling in accessible/shallow beds. The open gap is **absolute, blood-gas-referenced, deep-vein** venous sO₂ in humans — which no corpus method has yet closed.
