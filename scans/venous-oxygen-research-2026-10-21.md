### 1.1 — Non-invasive, Continuous Venous Oxygen Saturation and Oxygen Extraction Estimation from the Internal Jugular Vein *(previously surfaced — the flagship new-generation true-venous device)*
- **Authors:** ⚠ search-derived (bioengineering group associated with the jugular-optical-sensing line).
- **Year / Venue:** 2025 · **IEEE EMBC 2025** proceedings. IEEE Xplore doc 11254941; PubMed 41335695; DOI 10.1109/EMBC58623.2025.11254941. ⚠ egress-blocked; metadata cross-checked on ≥2 snippets (re-confirmed this run via PubMed snippet, which again states peripheral veins are non-pulsatile so standard pulse oximetry cannot read SvO₂ and the device estimates SvO₂ + O₂ extraction ratio within expected literature ranges).
- **Link:** https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/11254941/ · https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41335695/ · https://doi.org/10.1109/embc58623.2025.11254941
- **Relevance:** **8.5/10** — directly targets non-invasive, continuous **true venous** sO₂ at the IJV plus cerebral O₂ extraction.
- **Novelty:** Flexible/conformal neck sensor array reads the **IJV pulse + breathing-induced modulation** (peripheral veins being non-pulsatile) to estimate SvO₂ ≈ **65.7–80.9 %** (SD 1.67 % for IJV-pulse, 4.26 % for breathing modulation) and a per-subject **cerebral O₂-extraction ratio (O₂ER)** in 6 healthy adults; co-registers carotid artery and IJV at one neck site, working upright and supine. Authors flag artery–vein **crosstalk mitigation** and larger-cohort blood-gas validation as next steps.
- **Integration insights:** The extraction-aware wearable endpoint of the whole Topic-1 program; the artery serves as an in-situ reference (same logic as APM+, §2.2). The **CANVOS trial** (standing lead) is precisely the ICU blood-gas validation this device line said it needed. §1.3 (sternal-marrow reservoir) and §1.2 (jugular/cerebral NIRS) are the alternative-site and surface-NIRS counterparts. Sibling registry trial **NCT06004466** (National Taiwan University Hospital — plethysmographic device combining ultrasound imaging with optical tissue estimates for continuous non-invasive IJV saturation) re-surfaced this run as the independent clinical-translation counterpart; no published results yet. The UT-Austin **wearable optical e-tattoo** (2023 EMBC 10.1109/embc40787.2023.10340010; 2025 patent application US2025/0025074) is the conformal-array sibling pursuing the same neck/wrist artery–vein pair with spatial filtering for crosstalk.

### 1.2 — Near-Infrared Spectroscopy for Estimating Mixed Venous Oxygen Saturation and Cardiac Output During Off-Pump Coronary Artery Bypass Grafting: A Prospective Observational Study *(previously surfaced — first logged 2026-09-08)*
- **Authors:** ⚠ search-derived — not page-confirmed (PubMed/Springer egress-blocked).
- **Year / Venue:** 2026 · ***Journal of Clinical Monitoring and Computing*** (Springer); DOI **10.1007/s10877-026-01477-1**. PubMed 42606675. (Snippet dates publication to August 2026.)
- **Link / DOI:** https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10877-026-01477-1 · https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42606675/ ⚠ egress-blocked; metadata via index snippet.
- **Relevance:** **7.5/10** — non-invasive SvO₂ surrogate via **jugular + cerebral NIRS**, validated head-to-head against **pulmonary-artery-catheter SvO₂** in adults.
- **Novelty:** Tests two non-invasive NIRS surrogates in 25 OPCAB patients — (a) **rSjvO₂** over the left IJV (bias 2.0 %, wide LoA −14.9/+18.9 %) and (b) **baseline-calibrated cerebral NIRS (cal-rScO₂)** on the forehead (bias 0.8 %, LoA −7.8/+9.4 %); the calibrated cerebral measure reached **AUC 0.970 for detecting SvO₂ < 65 %** (sensitivity 95.9 %, specificity 89.8 % at optimal cutoff). **NIRS-derived cardiac output failed** (≈33 % error). The cerebral-NIRS-as-SvO₂-tracker idea is corroborated by the 2023 retrospective (PMC10709586, 176 h of OPCAB data, two NIRS devices).
- **Integration insights:** The most rigorous 2026 adult validation yet of jugular/cerebral NIRS as an SvO₂ *trend* monitor. Baseline-calibrated cerebral NIRS **out-tracked a sensor placed right over the IJV** — a strong argument for the vessel-*resolving* imaging of Topic 2 (PA, vis-OCT, RSOM) over surface averaging. Clean SvO₂ < 65 % discrimination is the clinically actionable low-SvO₂-alarm endpoint.

### 1.3 — Monitoring Changes in Mixed Venous Oxygen Saturation Using Photoplethysmography on the Sternum — A Proof of Concept Study *(previously surfaced — first logged 2026-09-08)*
- **Authors:** ⚠ search-derived — not page-confirmed. Likely the Scandinavian intraosseous-sternal-PPG group behind the earlier porcine sternal-oximetry work (*J. Clin. Monit. Comput.* 10.1007/s10877-023-00980-z).
- **Year / Venue:** 2026 · ***Medical & Biological Engineering & Computing*** (Springer); DOI **10.1007/s11517-026-03648-3**. PubMed 42527850.
- **Link / DOI:** https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11517-026-03648-3 · https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42527850/ ⚠ egress-blocked; metadata via index snippet.
- **Relevance:** **8/10** — non-invasive, continuous **true mixed-venous SvO₂** with a pulmonary-artery-catheter reference; a genuinely new anatomical site (sternum) for venous oximetry.
- **Novelty:** Exploits **sternal intramedullary (intraosseous) blood**, whose saturation tracks central/mixed-venous saturation, read **transcutaneously with a PPG probe** on the sternum. In anesthetized pigs, SvO₂ was driven down by stepwise hypoxia and staged hemorrhage while the non-invasive sternal signal (nSsO₂) was calibrated against pulmonary-artery SvO₂: **linear nSsO₂–SvO₂ correlation, ~87 % concordance** for tracking SvO₂ changes.
- **Integration insights:** A distinct physical route to non-invasive venous sO₂: the **bone marrow acts as a naturally venous-weighted optical reservoir**, sidestepping artery–vein disambiguation. Trend-grade (~87 %) performance sets a realistic accuracy bar; a natural fusion target for the pulse-modulation math of §1.5.

- **Year / Venue:** 2023 · *Journal of Cardiothoracic and Vascular Anesthesia* (S1053-0770(23)00782-6); PubMed 37827917.
- **Link:** https://www.jcvaonline.com/article/S1053-0770(23)00782-6/fulltext · https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1053077023007826 ⚠ egress-blocked; metadata via index snippet (re-confirmed this run: "central venous oxygen saturation can be estimated reasonably by the continuous noninvasive measurement of SjvO₂ using NIRS").

- **Novelty:** Clinical validation of the Mespere VO100 measuring SjvO₂ over the jugular as a non-invasive stand-in for catheter ScvO₂ in critically ill patients — strong linear correlation in both COVID-19 and post-transplant groups, Bland-Altman bias 0.57 %/0.00 % with percentage error 8.09 %/13.72 % respectively. ### 1.5 — Proof-of-Concept Non-Invasive Estimation of Peripheral Venous Oxygen Saturation (arterio-venous compliance pulse-modulation) *(previously surfaced — foundational method ancestor)*
- **Year / Venue:** 2017 · *BioMedical Engineering OnLine* 16:60; DOI 10.1186/s12938-017-0351-x; PMC5437414.
- **Link:** https://biomedical-engineering-online.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/s12938-017-0351-x · https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5437414/
- **Relevance:** **7/10** — the original non-invasive peripheral-venous (SpvO₂) optical concept.
- **Novelty:** Uses the **compliance difference between arteries and veins** to induce artificial respiration-like modulation that makes venous blood pulsatile, then reads the induced PPG to build a calibration model for regional venous sO₂; validated in 8 healthy volunteers with arm-lift/occlusion maneuvers against blood-gas references. Estimated SpvO₂ ran ≈3.1 ± 4.2 % below arterial saturation. (Siblings in corpus, re-confirmed this run: *A new method for noninvasive venous blood oxygen detection*, 10.1186/s12938-016-0208-8, 2016, PMC4952148/PubMed 27436186, RMSE 5.31 vs invasive reference; *Comparison of non-invasive peripheral venous saturations with venous blood co-oximetry*, PMC5655584, r = 0.81, mean difference ≈5.65 %; the 2007 artificial-finger-pulse during-bypass venous-oximetry paper, Echiadis et al., PubMed 17664681. Method basics corroborated this run by the NIRS venous-occlusion neonatal literature — r ≈ 0.96 after bias adjustment, better suited to relative-change tracking than absolute values.)
- **Integration insights:** The method ancestor of the §1.3 sternal-PPG and §1.1 IJV-pulse devices — every "make the vein pulsatile, then read it like a pulse oximeter" approach traces here. Its modest accuracy is exactly what the deep-diffuse-optics pulsation-decomposition ideas in Topic 2 aim to improve.

### 2.1 — Noninvasive In Vivo Photoacoustic Measurement of Internal Jugular Venous Oxygenation in Humans *(previously surfaced — the Topic-2 anchor)*
- **Year / Venue:** 2023 · preprint arXiv:2303.10775 (+ SPIE/Caltech-WUSTL lineage; precursor SPIE 8943E..1M, 2014, Garcia-Uribe et al., Wang lab). ⚠ arXiv egress-blocked; abstract re-confirmed on two index snippets this run (arXiv abs/PDF + Caltech authors.library records 5jp3j-jnz35 / 0pb76-cp433).
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.10775 · https://authors.library.caltech.edu/records/5jp3j-jnz35
- **Relevance:** **9/10** — human, in vivo, PA, *venous* (IJV).
- **Novelty:** Dual-wavelength photoacoustic tomography co-registered with ultrasound in 7 healthy volunteers; average internal-jugular sijvO₂ **72 ± 7 %** (range 62–79 %, re-confirmed this run). >90 % of IJV drainage comes from intracranial structures, so the IJV reading can track global brain oxygen consumption. Directly measures a named deep vein non-invasively — the result the whole Topic-2 program is built around, against a gold standard (catheterization) rarely used because it is invasive.
- **Integration insights:** The reference demonstration that deep-vein sO₂ is PA-measurable in humans. Everything else in Topic 2 either improves its quantification (fluence correction — GenMC standing lead; APM+ §2.2), extends the vessel-type/flow readout (PACT §2.3), moves to a different vascular bed (retina §2.4, skin microvasculature §2.5), or translates it to a wearable (LIVOx, Topic-1 standing context).

### 2.2 — Arterial Blood-Mediated Deep-Tissue Photoacoustic Oximetry (APM+): In-Vivo Human Radial-Vein sO₂ *(previously surfaced)*
- **Authors:** Sastry et al. (Wang lab, Caltech). ⚠ search-derived author short-form (re-confirmed this run via Caltech COILab PDF "Sastry-2026-AdvancedScience").
- **Year / Venue:** 2026 · *Advanced Science* (Wiley), 10.1002/advs.76366; PMC13334587; PubMed 42397001. Received Jan 2026, accepted Jun 2026, online early July 2026. ⚠ egress-blocked; abstract re-confirmed via Wiley + PubMed + PMC + COILab PDF snippets this run.
- **Link:** https://advanced.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/advs.76366 · https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13334587/
- **Relevance:** **8.5/10** — human, in vivo, PA, radial artery–vein pair with *venous* sO₂ reported.
- **Novelty:** Uses the arterial blood of an accompanying artery as an in-situ spectral reference to correct wavelength-dependent fluence for the adjacent deep vein — correcting fluence changes even *within* the vessel. In 8 healthy adults (dual-wavelength 3D PAT of the wrist), APM+ gave a **median venous sO₂ of 72.3 % (IQR 8.9 %)** (typical 60–80 % healthy range); the LUM comparator gave 75.2 % with much wider spread (IQR 34.4 %). Phantom (true 75 %): LUM 84 ± 2 %, **APM+ 72 ± 2 %** (ground-truth-closest); companion phantom figures LUM 98.5 %, APM 77.3 %, APM+ 69.2 %. Phantom median error **2.9 % (APM+) vs 9.8 % (LUM)**. Authors note the radial veins are superficial-to-moderate depth (not a true deep-vein study) and explicitly propose extending to the **carotid–IJV pair plus flow measurement to estimate oxygen consumption** — the same O₂ER endpoint as §1.1 — noting "most major arteries have accompanying veins."
- **Integration insights:** The artery-as-reference trick is the PA analogue of the arterio-venous compliance idea (§1.5) and the co-registered artery of §1.1; "most major arteries have an accompanying vein" is the generalization argument that makes this broadly applicable to deep veins.

### 2.3 — Oxygenation Heterogeneity Facilitates Spatiotemporal Flow-Pattern Visualization Inside Human Blood Vessels Using PACT *(previously surfaced)*
- **Year / Venue:** 2024 · PACT venue; PMC11161372; PubMed 38855671. ⚠ egress-blocked; abstract re-confirmed via snippet.
- **Link:** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11161372/ · https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38855671/
- **Relevance:** **8/10** — human, intravascular sO₂ heterogeneity and flow, PACT; recovers the parabolic laminar-flow wavefront inside a deep vein in vivo.

### 2.4 — Adaptive Spectroscopic Visible-Light OCT for Clinical Retinal Oximetry (ADS-vis-OCT) *(previously surfaced)*
- **Year / Venue:** 2023 · *Communications Medicine* (Nature), s43856-023-00288-8; PubMed 37095177; PMC10126115.
- **Link:** https://www.nature.com/articles/s43856-023-00288-8 · https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37095177/
- **Relevance:** **8/10** — resolves *per-vessel arterial and venous* retinal sO₂ non-invasively.
- **Novelty:** Adds an adaptive spectral-contamination-removal step to vis-OCT, giving accurate, repeatable sO₂ in retinal arteries and veins across vessel diameters in 18 volunteers (RMSE vs pulse oximeter 2.1 % in major arteries; repeat SD 2.5 % small arteries / 2.3 % small veins). Clinical siblings in corpus: retinal-vein-occlusion vis-OCT (venous sO₂ ~6.3 % lower in ischemic areas) and the 2025 glaucoma macular-AV-difference pilot (macular venous sO₂ rises, A–V difference falls with severity; TVST article 2802559). Standing modeling/technique upgrades: **dcBLL** (diffusion-corrected Beer-Lambert, *Med. Biol. Eng. Comput.* 64:2177, 2026; PubMed 42008039) and **DGFT** depth-gated Fourier transform (Nolen et al., *Opt. Lett.* 50(4):1061, 2025; PubMed 39951728).
- **Integration insights:** The retina is the one bed where per-vessel *venous* sO₂ is already clinically mapped non-invasively; its adaptive spectral-unmixing is directly transferable to the PA/RSOM vessel-resolving problem elsewhere in the body.

### 2.5 — Recording Skin Oxygenation by Dual-Wavelength Ultra-Wideband Optoacoustic Mesoscopy (DW-RSOM) *(previously surfaced — first surfaced 2026-10-15)*
- **Authors:** ⚠ search-derived — Aguirre / Berezhnoi / Ntziachristos and colleagues (TUM / Helmholtz Munich raster-scan optoacoustic mesoscopy group).
- **Year / Venue:** 2026 · ***npj Imaging*** (Nature portfolio); DOI **10.1038/s44303-026-00184-5**; PubMed **42432228** (listed 2026 Jul 10;4(1):51); PMC **13415847**. ⚠ egress-blocked; metadata cross-checked on ≥3 snippets (nature.com + PubMed + TUM FIS portal). *Note:* TUM FIS portal shows a later "Published - Dec 2026" state; the PubMed article date (Jul 2026) is taken as authoritative.
- **Link:** https://www.nature.com/articles/s44303-026-00184-5 · https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42432228/ · https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13415847/
- **Relevance:** **7/10** — human, in-vivo, non-invasive optoacoustic imaging that **explicitly resolves venule-compartment oxygenation**. *Flagged:* this is **microvascular / skin venule-level sO₂**, not a named-vein SvO₂/ScvO₂ — closer to a vessel-resolved StO₂ than to true venous saturation.
- **Novelty:** First dual-wavelength (**515 nm + 532 nm**) ultra-wideband raster-scan optoacoustic mesoscopy — prior RSOM was single-wavelength, so oxygenation mapping was not possible. In 6 healthy volunteers during **post-occlusive reactive hyperemia** (forearm) and a **vascular occlusion test** (nailfold), the system maps microvascular sO₂ at single-microvessel level, **thresholding the 515/532 nm absorption ratio at 0.9 to label vessels below as venules and above as arterioles**. Imaging-based mean sO₂ **80 ± 10 %** vs **87.5 ± 2.5 %** by blood-gas (difference n.s., p = 0.097); saturation rose to **88 ± 7 %** after cuff release. Prototype, small cohort. (Lineage: the 2021 *Photoacoustics* multispectral-RSOM venous/arterial-occlusion study, Attia et al., PMC8122174, is the single-wavelength-era precursor.)
- **Integration insights:** The first corpus entry to perform **arteriole-vs-venule spectral separation at the microvascular scale** non-invasively — the fine-grained complement to the deep-vein PA anchors (§2.1–§2.3) and per-vessel retinal vis-OCT (§2.4). The 515/532 nm ratio rule is a concrete, transferable rule for the artery–vein disambiguation that §1.1 and §2.2 call their key unsolved problem; its occlusion/hyperemia protocol is the same physiological maneuver used to make veins readable in §1.5.

## Cross-topic synthesis (2026-10-21)

**State of the field.** After a broad 10-query sweep across arXiv/PubMed/PMC, Springer, Wiley, Optica/SPIE, De Gruyter and clinical registries, the corpus is strongly **converged**: non-invasive *venous* (SvO₂/ScvO₂) optical measurement is a genuine low-publication-volume niche, and today's window added exactly **one** new-to-corpus primary paper — the **Khansari multispectral LS-PAM** methods paper — which is **tissue-level mouse-skin sO₂ mapping, not true venous**. No new Topic-1 (true-venous) paper and no new vessel-resolving *venous* optical paper appeared. This is reported honestly rather than padded with arterial-only SpO₂ work.

**The one structural problem the whole field shares.** Both topics are converging on the same core obstacle — **separating the venous signal from the overwhelming arterial/tissue background**, and **correcting wavelength-dependent optical fluence with depth** so that an sO₂ number is quantitative, not just a trend. Three independent strategies are now visible across the corpus:
1. **Make the vein pulsatile, then read it like a pulse oximeter** (Topic 1: §1.5 compliance-modulation → §1.1 IJV-pulse/breathing modulation, §1.3 sternal-marrow reservoir). Accuracy is trend-grade (~87 % concordance; LoA still wide).
2. **Use an accompanying artery as an in-situ spectral/fluence reference** (§2.2 APM+, and §1.1's co-registered carotid). APM+'s "most major arteries have an accompanying vein" argument is the clearest route from the radial wrist to the carotid–IJV neck pair — and it points at the same O₂-extraction endpoint the Topic-1 devices target.
3. **Resolve the vein spatially and unmix spectrally** (§2.1 PA-IJV, §2.3 intravascular PACT, §2.4 per-vessel vis-OCT, §2.5 arteriole/venule RSOM; plus GenMC, Hybrid-Net and today's Khansari paper on the fluence/unmixing side).

**Where today's new paper fits.** Khansari's melanin-aware, SRS-tunable multispectral LS-PAM is a strategy-3 **enabler**: label-free unmixing of oxy-/deoxy-Hb against melanin and collagen is exactly the pipeline that **skin-tone-robust** deep-vein PA oximetry needs (cf. GenMC's explicit Fitzpatrick III–V validation, and the melanin-corrected-StO₂ standing lead). It is mouse-skin and tissue-level today, but the source/unmixing advance is transferable once pushed to vessel-resolved, deeper, human imaging.

**Most actionable convergence.** The retina (§2.4) remains the one bed where **per-vessel venous sO₂ is already clinically mapped non-invasively**, and §2.5's **515/532 nm ratio rule** is the most concrete transferable recipe for the arteriole-vs-venule disambiguation that §1.1 and §2.2 both call their key unsolved problem. The near-term clinical signal to watch is validation volume, not new methods: the **CANVOS ICU trial** (ACTRN12625000866404) and **NCT06004466** (jugular, Taiwan) are the blood-gas validations the §1.1 device line said it needed, and **Noninvasix LIVOx** is the commercial optoacoustic ScvO₂ endpoint — none has reported results yet. **Watch list:** first CANVOS/LIVOx/NCT06004466 results; a journal version of GenMC; and any vessel-resolved or human extension of the Khansari / TD-HSI / DW-RSOM microvascular-unmixing line.
