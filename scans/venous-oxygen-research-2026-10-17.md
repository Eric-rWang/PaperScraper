- **Year / Venue:** 2025 · **IEEE EMBC 2025** proceedings. - **Link:** https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/11254941/ · https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41335695/ · https://doi.org/10.1109/embc58623.2025.11254941
- **Relevance:** **8.5/10** — directly targets non-invasive, continuous **true venous** sO₂ at the IJV plus cerebral O₂ extraction.
- **Novelty:** Flexible/conformal neck sensor array reads the **IJV pulse + breathing-induced modulation** (peripheral veins being non-pulsatile) to estimate SvO₂ ≈ **65.7–80.9 %** (SD 1.67 % for IJV-pulse, 4.26 % for breathing modulation) and a per-subject **cerebral O₂-extraction ratio (O₂ER)** in 6 healthy adults; co-registers carotid artery and IJV at one neck site, working upright and supine. ### 1.2 — Near-Infrared Spectroscopy for Estimating Mixed Venous Oxygen Saturation and Cardiac Output During Off-Pump Coronary Artery Bypass Grafting: A Prospective Observational Study *(previously surfaced — first logged 2026-09-08)*
- **Authors:** ⚠ search-derived — not page-confirmed (PubMed/Springer egress-blocked).
- **Year / Venue:** 2026 · ***Journal of Clinical Monitoring and Computing*** (Springer); DOI **10.1007/s10877-026-01477-1**. PubMed 42606675. (Snippet dates publication to August 2026.)
- **Link / DOI:** https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10877-026-01477-1 · https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42606675/ ⚠ egress-blocked; metadata via index snippet.
- **Relevance:** **7.5/10** — non-invasive SvO₂ surrogate via **jugular + cerebral NIRS**, validated head-to-head against **pulmonary-artery-catheter SvO₂** in adults.
- **Novelty:** Tests two non-invasive NIRS surrogates in 25 OPCAB patients — (a) **rSjvO₂** over the left IJV (bias 2.0 %, wide LoA −14.9/+18.9 %) and (b) **baseline-calibrated cerebral NIRS (cal-rScO₂)** on the forehead (bias 0.8 %, LoA −7.8/+9.4 %); the calibrated cerebral measure reached **AUC 0.970 for detecting SvO₂ < 65 %** (sensitivity 95.9 %, specificity 89.8 % at optimal cutoff). **NIRS-derived cardiac output failed** (≈33 % error). The cerebral-NIRS-as-SvO₂-tracker idea is corroborated by the 2023 retrospective (PMC10709586, 176 h of OPCAB data, two NIRS devices).

Note the symmetry with the retinal line (§1.4/§1.5): both ask whether a non-invasive optical read can substitute for catheter SvO₂ *and* track cardiac output; NIRS-CO failed here, retinal A–V-difference-vs-CO succeeded as a *trend* (§1.5) — the retinal bed's per-vessel resolution is the plausible reason.

### 1.3 — Monitoring Changes in Mixed Venous Oxygen Saturation Using Photoplethysmography on the Sternum — A Proof of Concept Study *(previously surfaced — first logged 2026-09-08)*
- **Authors:** ⚠ search-derived — not page-confirmed. Likely the Scandinavian intraosseous-sternal-PPG group behind the earlier porcine sternal-oximetry work (*J. Clin. Monit. Comput.* 10.1007/s10877-023-00980-z).
- **Year / Venue:** 2026 · ***Medical & Biological Engineering & Computing*** (Springer); DOI **10.1007/s11517-026-03648-3**. PubMed 42527850.
- **Link / DOI:** https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11517-026-03648-3 · https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42527850/ ⚠ egress-blocked; metadata via index snippet (re-confirmed this run).
- **Relevance:** **8/10** — non-invasive, continuous **true mixed-venous SvO₂** with a pulmonary-artery-catheter reference; a genuinely new anatomical site (sternum) for venous oximetry.
- **Novelty:** Exploits **sternal intramedullary (intraosseous) blood**, whose saturation tracks central/mixed-venous saturation, read **transcutaneously with a PPG probe** on the sternum. Trend-grade (~87 %) performance sets a realistic accuracy bar; a natural fusion target for the pulse-modulation math of the arterio-venous-compliance ancestor (standing lead).

### 1.4 — Retinal Oxygen Saturation as a Non-Invasive Estimate for Mixed Venous Oxygen Saturation and Cardiac Output *(**NEW TO CORPUS — first surfaced 2026-10-17; landmark true-venous validation**)*
- **Authors:** Van Keer K. et al. ⚠ author short-form search-derived.
- **Year / Venue:** 2019 · ***Acta Ophthalmologica*** (Wiley); DOI **10.1111/aos.13890**; PubMed **30280510**. ⚠ egress-blocked; metadata cross-checked on ≥3 snippets (PubMed + Wiley abstract + Wiley PDF listing).
- **Link / DOI:** https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/aos.13890 · https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30280510/
- **Relevance:** **8/10** — non-invasive **retinal venous sO₂** validated directly against **invasively catheter-measured mixed-venous saturation**; one of the very few true non-invasive-venous-vs-gold-standard-SvO₂ papers in existence.
- **Novelty:** Dual-wavelength retinal (fundus) oximetry in **14 patients who also underwent right-heart catheterisation**. Retinal **venous** saturation correlated with invasively measured **mixed-venous** saturation after age adjustment (**β = 0.80, p = 0.003**); the **retinal arteriovenous saturation difference** correlated with the **inverse of cardiac output** (Spearman ρ = 0.59, p = 0.026). Explicitly framed as a proof-of-concept that the eye's accessible vasculature can stand in for a pulmonary-artery catheter.
- **Integration insights:** This is the retinal line's *true-venous anchor* and the reason it belongs in Topic 1, not merely Topic 2: unlike tissue-StO₂ surrogates, retinal fundus oximetry resolves a named **retinal vein** and the paper ties it to the single most clinically important venous number (mixed-venous SvO₂) against the invasive gold standard. It is the direct predecessor of the 2026 echocardiography pilot (§1.5) and conceptually parallel to the neck artery–vein O₂ER device (§1.1): both exploit the **A–V saturation gap as a non-invasive oxygen-extraction readout**. Its small n (14) and proof-of-concept status set the honest accuracy expectation.

### 1.5 — Retinal Arteriovenous Oxygen Saturation Difference Is Inversely Associated with Cardiac Output Measured by Echocardiography: A Non-Invasive Pilot Study *(**NEW TO CORPUS — first surfaced 2026-10-17**)*

- **Year / Venue:** 2026 · ***Scientific Reports*** (Nature portfolio); DOI **10.1038/s41598-026-56918-3**; PubMed **42265462**; PMC **13500711**. ⚠ egress-blocked (direct `WebFetch` of nature.com DNS-blocked this run); metadata cross-checked on ≥3 snippets (nature.com + PMC + PubMed). **Publication date ambiguous** in index snippets (one gives 2026-06-09); flagged ⚠ pending page-verification.
- **Link / DOI:** https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-026-56918-3 · https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13500711/ · https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42265462/
- **Relevance:** **7.5/10** — non-invasive **retinal venous sO₂** and A–V difference as a systemic-oxygen-extraction / cardiac-output proxy; updates the §1.4 line with a modern, catheter-free reference.
- **Novelty:** Dual-wavelength retinal oximetry in both eyes plus same-day **transthoracic echocardiography** (non-invasive CO reference, replacing §1.4's catheter) in **14 participants (5 clinically stable cardiac patients + healthy volunteers)**. ## Cross-topic synthesis (2026-10-17)

1. **The retinal vasculature finally enters the corpus as a true-venous route, not just a Topic-2 modality.** The two new-to-corpus items — Van Keer et al. 2019 (§1.4) and the 2026 *Sci Rep* echocardiography pilot (§1.5) — matter because retinal fundus oximetry is the one accessible human bed where a **named vein** is optically resolved *and* its saturation has been tied to the single most clinically important venous number, **mixed-venous SvO₂**, against the invasive gold standard (β = 0.80 vs right-heart catheter). That makes the retinal line a genuine non-invasive-venous validation, not an arterial-only transfer — which is why it belongs in Topic 1. It had simply been missing from 76 prior scans; surfacing it is the substantive addition this run.

2. **Three independent methods now converge on the same physiological target: the arterio-venous O₂ gap as a non-invasive extraction / cardiac-output readout.** The neck IJV-pulse O₂ER device (§1.1), the PA radial artery–vein APM+ method (§2.2, which explicitly proposes a carotid–IJV + flow extension), and the retinal A–V-saturation-difference-vs-CO pilots (§1.4/§1.5) are three realizations of one idea — measure artery and vein at one site and read the difference. The retina's advantage is that artery and vein are *already* optically separated; the neck and radial sites must still solve the artery–vein disambiguation that §2.5's 515/532 nm ratio rule attacks at the microvascular scale.

3. **The field's shape is otherwise unchanged: deep-vein SvO₂ remains a low-publication-volume niche.** No new deep-vein/intravascular PA or NIRS paper published in the two-day window; Topic 2's §2.1–§2.5 anchors are stable. The honest picture is now four converging fronts — (a) **deep named-vein PA** (IJV §2.1, radial-vein APM+ §2.2, intravascular-flow PACT §2.3); (b) **surface/wearable surrogates** (jugular/cerebral NIRS §1.2 and Mespere standing lead, sternal-marrow PPG §1.3, IJV-pulse array §1.1); (c) **microvascular vessel-resolving** (skin RSOM §2.5, SFDI standing lead); and (d) **retinal per-vessel** (vis-OCT §2.4, fundus-oximetry §1.4/§1.5). Each trades among depth, resolution, and clinical accessibility.

4. **Fluence and artery–vein crosstalk remain the two recurring bottlenecks**, attacked from complementary directions: physics-based reference correction (APM+ arterial reference §2.2; dcBLL diffusion term; GenMC generative Monte-Carlo), learned inversion (Hybrid-Net, LSD-qPAI family), an empirical two-wavelength ratio rule for compartment labeling (§2.5), and — uniquely — the retina, where the anatomy pre-solves artery–vein separation and the remaining problem is absolute calibration (§1.4/§1.5 are trend/association-grade at n = 14).
