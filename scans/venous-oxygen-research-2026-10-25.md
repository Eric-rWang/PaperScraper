### 1.1 — Non-invasive, Continuous Venous Oxygen Saturation and Oxygen Extraction Estimation from the Internal Jugular Vein *(previously surfaced — the flagship new-generation true-venous device)*
- **Authors:** ⚠ search-derived (bioengineering group associated with the jugular-optical-sensing line).
- **Year / Venue:** 2025 · **IEEE EMBC 2025** proceedings. IEEE Xplore doc 11254941; PubMed 41335695; DOI 10.1109/EMBC58623.2025.11254941. ⚠ egress-blocked; `WebFetch` on PubMed 41335695 failed this run (`ENOTFOUND`), so metadata is from prior-run PubMed snippets (peripheral veins are non-pulsatile so standard pulse oximetry cannot read SvO₂; the device estimates SvO₂ + O₂-extraction ratio within expected literature ranges).
- **Link:** https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/11254941/ · https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41335695/ · https://doi.org/10.1109/embc58623.2025.11254941
- **Relevance:** **8.5/10** — directly targets non-invasive, continuous **true venous** sO₂ at the IJV plus cerebral O₂ extraction.
- **Novelty:** Flexible/conformal neck sensor array reads the **IJV pulse + breathing-induced modulation** (peripheral veins being non-pulsatile) to estimate SvO₂ ≈ **65.7–80.9 %** (SD 1.67 % for IJV-pulse, 4.26 % for breathing modulation) and a per-subject **cerebral O₂-extraction ratio (O₂ER)** in 6 healthy adults; co-registers carotid artery and IJV at one neck site, working upright and supine. Authors flag artery–vein **crosstalk mitigation** and larger-cohort blood-gas validation as next steps.
- **Integration insights:** The extraction-aware wearable endpoint of the whole Topic-1 program; the artery serves as an in-situ reference (same logic as APM+, §2.2). Sibling registry trial **NCT06004466** (National Taiwan University Hospital — plethysmographic device combining ultrasound imaging with optical tissue estimates for continuous non-invasive IJV saturation) remains the independent clinical-translation counterpart; no published results yet. The UT-Austin **wearable optical e-tattoo** (2023 EMBC 10.1109/embc40787.2023.10340010; 2025 patent application US2025/0025074; re-surfaced this run) is the conformal-array sibling pursuing the same neck/wrist artery–vein pair with spatial filtering for crosstalk.

### 1.2 — Near-Infrared Spectroscopy for Estimating Mixed Venous Oxygen Saturation and Cardiac Output During Off-Pump Coronary Artery Bypass Grafting: A Prospective Observational Study *(previously surfaced — first logged 2026-09-08)*
- **Authors:** ⚠ search-derived — not page-confirmed (PubMed/Springer egress-blocked).
- **Year / Venue:** 2026 · ***Journal of Clinical Monitoring and Computing*** (Springer); DOI **10.1007/s10877-026-01477-1**. PubMed 42606675.
- **Link / DOI:** https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10877-026-01477-1 · https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42606675/ ⚠ egress-blocked; metadata via index snippet.
- **Relevance:** **7.5/10** — non-invasive SvO₂ surrogate via **jugular + cerebral NIRS**, validated head-to-head against **pulmonary-artery-catheter SvO₂** in adults.
- **Novelty:** Tests two non-invasive NIRS surrogates in 25 OPCAB patients — (a) **rSjvO₂** over the left IJV (bias 2.0 %, wide LoA −14.9/+18.9 %) and (b) **baseline-calibrated cerebral NIRS (cal-rScO₂)** on the forehead (bias 0.8 %, LoA −7.8/+9.4 %); the calibrated cerebral measure reached **AUC 0.970 for detecting SvO₂ < 65 %** (sensitivity 95.9 %, specificity 89.8 %). **NIRS-derived cardiac output failed** (≈33 % error). Corroborated by the 2023 retrospective (PMC10709586) and by the Geneva external-jugular-vs-ScvO₂ validation re-surfaced this run.
- **Integration insights:** The most rigorous 2026 adult validation yet of jugular/cerebral NIRS as an SvO₂ *trend* monitor. Baseline-calibrated cerebral NIRS **out-tracked a sensor placed right over the IJV** — a strong argument for the vessel-*resolving* imaging of Topic 2 over surface averaging. Clean SvO₂ < 65 % discrimination is the clinically actionable low-SvO₂-alarm endpoint.

### 1.3 — Monitoring Changes in Mixed Venous Oxygen Saturation Using Photoplethysmography on the Sternum — A Proof of Concept Study *(previously surfaced — first logged 2026-09-08)*
- **Authors:** ⚠ search-derived — not page-confirmed. Likely the Scandinavian intraosseous-sternal-PPG group behind the earlier porcine sternal-oximetry work (*J. Clin. Monit. Comput.* 10.1007/s10877-023-00980-z).
- **Year / Venue:** 2026 · ***Medical & Biological Engineering & Computing*** (Springer); DOI **10.1007/s11517-026-03648-3**. PubMed 42527850. (Linear nSsO₂–SvO₂ correlation with ~87 % concordance, per prior-run PubMed snippet.)
- **Link / DOI:** https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11517-026-03648-3 · https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42527850/ ⚠ egress-blocked; metadata via index snippet.
- **Relevance:** **8/10** — non-invasive, continuous **true mixed-venous SvO₂** with a pulmonary-artery-catheter reference; a genuinely new anatomical site (sternum) for venous oximetry.
- **Novelty:** Exploits **sternal intramedullary (intraosseous) blood**, whose saturation tracks central/mixed-venous saturation, read **transcutaneously with a PPG probe** on the sternum. In anesthetized pigs, SvO₂ was driven down by stepwise hypoxia and staged hemorrhage while the non-invasive sternal signal (nSsO₂) was calibrated against pulmonary-artery SvO₂: **linear nSsO₂–SvO₂ correlation, ~87 % concordance** for tracking SvO₂ changes.
- **Integration insights:** A distinct physical route to non-invasive venous sO₂: the **bone marrow acts as a naturally venous-weighted optical reservoir**, sidestepping artery–vein disambiguation — the surface, non-invasive analogue of the deep mucosal reservoirs used by the esophageal-RRS and PA-TEE probes. Trend-grade (~87 %) performance sets a realistic accuracy bar; a natural fusion target for the pulse-modulation math of §1.5.

### 1.5 — Proof-of-Concept Non-Invasive Estimation of Peripheral Venous Oxygen Saturation (arterio-venous compliance pulse-modulation) *(previously surfaced — foundational method ancestor)*
- **Year / Venue:** 2017 · *BioMedical Engineering OnLine* 16:60; DOI 10.1186/s12938-017-0351-x; PMC5437414.
- **Link:** https://biomedical-engineering-online.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/s12938-017-0351-x · https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5437414/
- **Relevance:** **7/10** — the original non-invasive peripheral-venous (SpvO₂) optical concept.
- **Novelty:** Uses the **compliance difference between arteries and veins** to induce artificial respiration-like modulation that makes venous blood pulsatile, then reads the induced PPG to build a calibration model for regional venous sO₂; validated in 8 healthy volunteers with arm-lift/occlusion maneuvers against blood-gas references. Estimated SpvO₂ ran ≈3.1 ± 4.2 % below arterial saturation. (Siblings re-confirmed this run via index snippets: *A new method for noninvasive venous blood oxygen detection*, 10.1186/s12938-016-0208-8, 2016, PMC4952148/PubMed 27436186, RMSE 5.31, r = 0.72 vs invasive; *Comparison of non-invasive peripheral venous saturations with venous blood co-oximetry* by Kyriacou's group, PMC5655584, r = 0.81 with a wide mean difference; the 2007 artificial-finger-pulse during-bypass venous-oximetry paper, Echiadis et al., PubMed 17664681.)
- **Integration insights:** The method ancestor of the §1.3 sternal-PPG and §1.1 IJV-pulse devices — every "make the vein pulsatile, then read it like a pulse oximeter" approach traces here. The artery–vein crosstalk problem it first exposed (artery and vein sit close, so their signals mix) is the same one the §1.1 spatial-filtering e-tattoo and the §2.5 spectral-ratio rule now attack. Its modest accuracy is exactly what the deep-diffuse-optics pulsation-decomposition ideas in Topic 2 aim to improve.

> **Standing context — non-invasive external-jugular reflectance probe + its ICU trial (previously surfaced, De Gruyter + ANZCTR):** *Estimation of Venous Oxygen Saturation Through Non-Invasive Optical Sensing at the Jugular Veins* (cdbme-2024-2072, 10.1515/cdbme-2024-2072). Hill, Campbell, Chase, Pretty. Pulse-oximeter-like optical sensor on the external jugular vein; 3 subjects, SvO₂ 70.4/71.1/72.2 %. The **CANVOS** reflectance-over-the-IJV ICU trial (ANZCTR **ACTRN12625000866404**, re-surfaced this run; first logged 2026-10-01) is the same Christchurch group's clinical-translation step, comparing the optical estimate against blood-gas analysis in ICU patients. Authors note the 70–75 % estimates still need calibration against blood-gas analysers — the surface-reflectance EJV sibling of the §1.1 IJV-pulse device.
> **Standing context — minimally-invasive esophageal resonance-Raman SvO₂ probe (previously surfaced, first logged 2026-10-23):** *Esophageal Resonance Raman Spectroscopy Tracks Mixed Venous Oxygenation ...* (Kheir/Romfh line; *Intensive Care Medicine Experimental* 10.1186/s40635-026-00989-z, online 2026-10-15). Esophageal RRS discriminated SvO₂ < 40 % with **AUC 0.98 vs transabdominal NIRS 0.82** in rats. Minimally-invasive/animal; the Raman twin of the PA-TEE probe below.
> **Standing context — minimally-invasive PA-TEE SvO₂ probe (previously surfaced, Wang lab, SPIE 2016):** esophageal photoacoustic probe reading mixed-venous SvO₂ against the PA-catheter gold standard in swine, framed as a way to avoid the ≈10 % severe-complication risk of pulmonary-artery catheters — the photoacoustic twin of the esophageal-RRS paper.

### 2.1 — Noninvasive In Vivo Photoacoustic Measurement of Internal Jugular Venous Oxygenation in Humans *(previously surfaced — the Topic-2 anchor)*
- **Authors:** Garcia-Uribe et al. (Wang lab). ⚠ arXiv egress-blocked; abstract re-confirmed this run on index snippets (arXiv abs/PDF + Caltech authors.library records 5jp3j-jnz35 and 0pb76-cp433 + WUSTL profiles).
- **Year / Venue:** 2023 · preprint arXiv:2303.10775 (+ SPIE/Caltech-WUSTL lineage; precursor SPIE 8943E..1M, 2014).
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.10775 · https://authors.library.caltech.edu/records/5jp3j-jnz35
- **Relevance:** **9/10** — human, in vivo, PA, *venous* (IJV).
- **Novelty:** Dual-wavelength photoacoustic tomography co-registered with ultrasound (5 fps) in 7 healthy volunteers; average internal-jugular sijvO₂ **72 ± 7 %** (re-confirmed this run). >90 % of IJV drainage comes from intracranial structures, so the IJV reading can track global brain oxygen consumption; the authors frame it as a non-invasive alternative to fluoroscopy-guided jugular catheterization. A larger PA/US cohort (SKKU; 165 carotids in 117 volunteers) reports IJV **71.3 ± 6.4 %** vs carotid **93.0 ± 3.5 %** — corroborating the ≈72 % IJV figure.
- **Integration insights:** The reference demonstration that deep-vein sO₂ is PA-measurable in humans. Everything else in Topic 2 either improves its quantification (GenMC standing lead; APM+ §2.2), extends the flow/vessel-type readout (PACT §2.3), moves to another bed (retina §2.4, skin microvasculature §2.5), or translates it to a wearable (LIVOx / e-tattoo, Topic-1 standing context).

### 2.2 — Arterial Blood-Mediated Deep-Tissue Photoacoustic Oximetry (APM+): In-Vivo Human Radial-Vein sO₂ *(previously surfaced)*
- **Authors:** Sastry et al. (Wang lab, Caltech). ⚠ re-confirmed this run via Caltech COILab PDF snippet ("Sastry-2026-AdvancedScience"; Received 5 Jan 2026, Revised 5 Jun 2026, Accepted 8 Jun 2026).
- **Year / Venue:** 2026 · *Advanced Science* (Wiley), 10.1002/advs.76366; PMC13334587; PubMed 42397001. Online early July 2026.
- **Link:** https://advanced.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/advs.76366 · https://coilab.caltech.edu/documents/34878/Sastry-2026-AdvancedScience.pdf · https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13334587/
- **Relevance:** **8.5/10** — human, in vivo, PA, radial artery–vein pair with *venous* sO₂ reported.
- **Novelty:** Uses the arterial blood of an accompanying artery (high, known saturation) as an in-situ spectral reference to correct wavelength-dependent fluence for the adjacent deep vein. In 8 healthy adults (dual-wavelength 3D PAT of the wrist), APM+ gave a **median venous sO₂ of 72.3 % (IQR 8.9 %)**; the LUM comparator gave 75.2 % with much wider spread (IQR 34.4 %). Phantom (true 75 %): **APM+ 72 ± 2 %**; phantom median error **2.9 % (APM+) vs 9.8 % (LUM)**; validated through up to 1.5 cm ex vivo tissue. Authors explicitly propose extending to the **carotid–IJV pair plus flow to estimate oxygen consumption** — the same O₂ER endpoint as §1.1 — noting "most major arteries have accompanying veins."
- **Integration insights:** The artery-as-reference trick is the PA analogue of the arterio-venous compliance idea (§1.5) and the co-registered artery of §1.1; "most major arteries have an accompanying vein" is the generalization argument that makes this broadly applicable to deep veins. Its phantom-error figures remain the best in-corpus demonstration that per-vessel fluence correction beats linear unmixing for the venous compartment.

### 2.3 — Oxygenation Heterogeneity Facilitates Spatiotemporal Flow-Pattern Visualization Inside Human Blood Vessels Using PACT *(previously surfaced)*
- **Year / Venue:** 2024 · PACT venue; PMC11161372; PubMed 38855671. ⚠ egress-blocked; re-confirmed via snippet this run.
- **Link:** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11161372/ · https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38855671/
- **Relevance:** **8/10** — human, intravascular sO₂ heterogeneity and flow, PACT; recovers the parabolic laminar-flow wavefront inside a deep vein in vivo.
- **Novelty:** Shows intravascular oxygenation heterogeneity can be used as endogenous contrast to visualize the spatiotemporal flow pattern inside human vessels — resolving sO₂ *within* a single deep vessel lumen rather than a single vessel-average value.
- **Integration insights:** Pushes PA venous oximetry from "one number per vein" toward spatially-resolved intravascular sO₂ + flow — the data a true O₂-consumption / extraction estimate (§1.1, §2.2) ultimately needs.

### 2.4 — Adaptive Spectroscopic Visible-Light OCT for Clinical Retinal Oximetry (ADS-vis-OCT) *(previously surfaced)*
- **Year / Venue:** 2023 · *Communications Medicine* (Nature), s43856-023-00288-8; PubMed 37095177; PMC10126115.
- **Link:** https://www.nature.com/articles/s43856-023-00288-8 · https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37095177/
- **Relevance:** **8/10** — resolves *per-vessel arterial and venous* retinal sO₂ non-invasively.
- **Novelty:** Adds an adaptive spectral-contamination-removal step to vis-OCT, giving accurate, repeatable sO₂ in retinal arteries and veins across vessel diameters in 18 volunteers (RMSE vs pulse oximeter 2.1 % major arteries; repeat SD 2.5 % small arteries / 2.3 % small veins). Typical retinal readings place venous sO₂ ≈ 72 % vs arterial ≈ 95 %. Clinical siblings in corpus: retinal-vein-occlusion vis-OCT (venous sO₂ ~6.3 % lower in ischemic areas) and the 2025 glaucoma macular-AV-difference pilot (TVST 2802559; Johns Hopkins, 48 eyes/35 subjects). Lett.* 50(4):1061, 2025; PubMed 39951728).
- **Integration insights:** The retina is the one bed where per-vessel *venous* sO₂ is already clinically mapped non-invasively; its adaptive spectral-unmixing is directly transferable to the PA/RSOM vessel-resolving problem elsewhere.

### 2.5 — Recording Skin Oxygenation by Dual-Wavelength Ultra-Wideband Optoacoustic Mesoscopy (DW-RSOM) *(previously surfaced — first surfaced 2026-10-15)*
- **Authors:** ⚠ search-derived — Aguirre / Berezhnoi / Ntziachristos and colleagues (TUM / Helmholtz Munich RSOM group).
- **Year / Venue:** 2026 · ***npj Imaging*** (Nature portfolio); DOI **10.1038/s44303-026-00184-5**; PubMed **42432228** (2026 Jul 10;4(1):51); PMC **13415847**. ⚠ egress-blocked; metadata cross-checked on prior-run snippets.
- **Link:** https://www.nature.com/articles/s44303-026-00184-5 · https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42432228/ · https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13415847/
- **Relevance:** **7/10** — human, in-vivo, non-invasive optoacoustic imaging that **explicitly resolves venule-compartment oxygenation**. *Flagged:* **microvascular / skin venule-level sO₂**, not a named-vein SvO₂/ScvO₂ — closer to a vessel-resolved StO₂.
- **Novelty:** First dual-wavelength (**515 nm + 532 nm**) ultra-wideband raster-scan optoacoustic mesoscopy — prior RSOM was single-wavelength. In 6 healthy volunteers during post-occlusive reactive hyperemia and a vascular occlusion test, maps microvascular sO₂ at single-microvessel level, **thresholding the 515/532 nm absorption ratio at 0.9 to label vessels below as venules, above as arterioles**. Imaging-based mean sO₂ **80 ± 10 %** vs **87.5 ± 2.5 %** by blood-gas (p = 0.097); rose to **88 ± 7 %** after cuff release.
- **Integration insights:** The first corpus entry to perform **arteriole-vs-venule spectral separation at the microvascular scale** non-invasively — the fine-grained complement to the deep-vein PA anchors (§2.1–§2.3) and per-vessel retinal vis-OCT (§2.4). The 515/532 nm ratio rule is a concrete, transferable artery–vein disambiguation rule; its occlusion/hyperemia protocol is the same maneuver used to make veins readable in §1.5.

> **Standing lead — GenMC: Real-Time Generative Monte Carlo Surrogate for Quantitative Photoacoustic Imaging** *(first logged 09-27; re-confirmed this run via arXiv PDF snippet).* Shi, He, Vercauteren, Xia (King's College London). 2026 · preprint **arXiv:2609.28261**. A PA fluence-quantification method enabling quantitative venous sO₂ at depth; with fluence correction yields in-vivo **venous sO₂ ≈ 78.05 %** (vs Monte-Carlo baseline 78.36 %; uncorrected linear-unmixing overestimate ≈99.98 %); PSNR 26.16 dB for predicted fluence; validated in phantoms and **37 human volunteers spanning Fitzpatrick III–V**. Merit ~7.5/10; a strong candidate to re-enter the ranked five on journal publication.
> **Standing lead — "Maximum fluence for accurate functional photoacoustic microscopy"** *(already in corpus, first logged September; re-surfaced in search this run — NOT new).* *Photoacoustics* (ScienceDirect S2213597926000571). Reports that, versus saO₂, **svO₂ shows a larger increase with increasing laser fluence** — a direct quantification of the fluence-dependent bias that most inflates *venous* PA estimates, reinforcing the fluence-correction thesis of GenMC and APM+ (§2.2). Related classic fluence-correction work re-surfaced this run: Naser et al. local fluence correction (*IEEE TMI* 2019, SO₂ error 10.1 %→2.8 %) and Zhou et al. diffusion-dipole vs Monte-Carlo comparison (PMC7210453). Methods line, ~6/10.
