### 1.1 — Non-invasive, Continuous Venous Oxygen Saturation and Oxygen Extraction Estimation from the Internal Jugular Vein *(previously surfaced — the flagship new-generation true-venous device; re-confirmed this run)*
- **Authors:** ⚠ search-derived (bioengineering group associated with the jugular-optical-sensing line).
- **Year / Venue:** 2025 · **IEEE EMBC 2025** proceedings. IEEE Xplore doc 11254941; PubMed 41335695; DOI 10.1109/EMBC58623.2025.11254941.
- **Link:** https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/11254941/ · https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41335695/ · https://doi.org/10.1109/embc58623.2025.11254941
- **Relevance:** **8.5/10** — directly targets non-invasive, continuous **true venous** sO₂ at the IJV plus cerebral O₂ extraction.
- **Novelty:** Flexible/conformal neck sensor array reads the **IJV pulse + breathing-induced modulation** (peripheral veins being non-pulsatile) to estimate SvO₂ ≈ **65.7–80.9 %** (SD 1.67 % for IJV-pulse, 4.26 % for breathing modulation) and a per-subject **cerebral O₂-extraction ratio (O₂ER)** in 6 healthy adults; co-registers carotid artery and IJV at one neck site. Authors flag artery–vein **crosstalk mitigation** and larger-cohort blood-gas validation as next steps.
- **Integration insights:** The extraction-aware wearable endpoint of the whole Topic-1 program; the artery serves as an in-situ reference (same logic as APM+, §2.2). Sibling registry trials remain the independent clinical-translation counterparts: **NCT06004466** (National Taiwan University Hospital, plethysmographic IJV device, enrolling) and **ACTRN12625000866404** (Australian flexible optical IJV sensor vs venous blood gas, with oxygen-extraction-ratio agreement as an outcome) — no published results yet. The UT-Austin wearable optical e-tattoo and the Vanderbilt 48-channel PPG e-tattoo are conformal-array siblings pursuing the same neck artery–vein pair. The De Gruyter jugular-optical probe (Hill/Campbell/Chase/Pretty, cdbme-2024-2072) is the proof-of-concept external-jugular analogue.

### 1.2 — Near-Infrared Spectroscopy for Estimating Mixed Venous Oxygen Saturation and Cardiac Output During Off-Pump Coronary Artery Bypass Grafting: A Prospective Observational Study *(previously surfaced — first logged 2026-09-08; re-confirmed this run)*
- **Authors:** Oh et al. ⚠ author line search-derived — not page-confirmed (PubMed/Springer egress-blocked).
- **Year / Venue:** 2026 · ***Journal of Clinical Monitoring and Computing*** (Springer); DOI **10.1007/s10877-026-01477-1**. PubMed 42606675.
- **Link / DOI:** https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10877-026-01477-1 · https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42606675/
- **Relevance:** **7.5/10** — non-invasive SvO₂ surrogate via **jugular + cerebral NIRS**, validated head-to-head against **pulmonary-artery-catheter SvO₂** in adults.
- **Novelty:** Tests two non-invasive NIRS surrogates in 25 OPCAB patients — (a) **rSjvO₂** over the left IJV (bias 2.0 %, wide LoA −14.9/+18.9 %) and (b) **baseline-calibrated cerebral NIRS (cal-rScO₂)** on the forehead (bias 0.8 %, LoA −7.8/+9.4 %); the calibrated cerebral measure reached **AUC 0.970 for detecting SvO₂ < 65 %** (sensitivity 95.9 %, specificity 89.8 %). **NIRS-derived cardiac output failed** (≈33 % error).
- **Integration insights:** The most rigorous 2026 adult validation yet of jugular/cerebral NIRS as an SvO₂ *trend* monitor. Baseline-calibrated cerebral NIRS **out-tracked a sensor placed right over the IJV** — a strong argument for the vessel-*resolving* imaging of Topic 2 over surface averaging. A 2023 retrospective (*Sci. Rep.* s41598-023-49078-1 / PMC10709586) independently reports cerebral oximetry tracking SvO₂ with ~91 % concordance. Clean SvO₂ < 65 % discrimination is the clinically actionable low-SvO₂-alarm endpoint.

### 1.3 — Monitoring Changes in Mixed Venous Oxygen Saturation Using Photoplethysmography on the Sternum — A Proof of Concept Study *(previously surfaced — first logged 2026-09-08; re-confirmed this run)*
- **Authors:** ⚠ search-derived — likely the Scandinavian intraosseous-sternal-PPG group (prior porcine work *J. Clin. Monit. Comput.* 10.1007/s10877-023-00980-z).
- **Year / Venue:** 2026 (online 30 Jul 2026) · ***Medical & Biological Engineering & Computing*** (Springer); DOI **10.1007/s11517-026-03648-3**. PubMed 42527850.
- **Link / DOI:** https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11517-026-03648-3 · https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42527850/
- **Relevance:** **8/10** — non-invasive, continuous **true mixed-venous SvO₂** with a pulmonary-artery-catheter reference; a genuinely new anatomical site (sternum) for venous oximetry.
- **Novelty:** Exploits **sternal intramedullary (intraosseous) blood**, whose saturation tracks central/mixed-venous saturation, read **transcutaneously with a PPG probe** on the sternum. In anesthetized pigs, SvO₂ was driven down by stepwise hypoxia and staged hemorrhage while the non-invasive sternal signal (nSsO₂) was calibrated against pulmonary-artery SvO₂: **linear nSsO₂–SvO₂ correlation, ~87 % concordance**.
- **Integration insights:** A distinct physical route to non-invasive venous sO₂: the **bone marrow acts as a naturally venous-weighted optical reservoir**, sidestepping artery–vein disambiguation. The 2025 bone-marrow MSOT paper (Nano Letters, PMC12893716; Topic-2 standing lead) independently demonstrated bone-marrow sO₂ is non-invasively optoacoustically measurable. The Noninvasix **LIVOx** chest-worn probe and the NIRSense CHF sternal wearable (NCT07874386) are further independent bets on the chest/sternal site.

### 1.5 — Proof-of-Concept Non-Invasive Estimation of Peripheral Venous Oxygen Saturation (arterio-venous compliance pulse-modulation) *(previously surfaced — foundational method ancestor; re-confirmed this run)*
- **Year / Venue:** 2017 · *BioMedical Engineering OnLine* 16:60; DOI 10.1186/s12938-017-0351-x; PMC5437414.
- **Link:** https://biomedical-engineering-online.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/s12938-017-0351-x · https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5437414/
- **Relevance:** **7/10** — the original non-invasive peripheral-venous (SpvO₂) optical concept.
- **Novelty:** Uses the **compliance difference between arteries and veins** to induce artificial respiration-like modulation that makes venous blood pulsatile, then reads the induced PPG to build a calibration model for regional venous sO₂; validated in 8 healthy volunteers with arm-lift/occlusion maneuvers against blood-gas references. Estimated SpvO₂ ran ≈3.1 ± 4.2 % below arterial saturation. (Siblings re-hit this run: 10.1186/s12938-016-0208-8 / PMC4952148, RMSE 5.31, r = 0.72; the 2007 Echiadis finger-pulse paper, PubMed 17664681 / IOPscience 10.1088/0967-3334/28/8/012.)
- **Integration insights:** The method ancestor of the §1.3 sternal-PPG, §1.1 IJV-pulse devices, and the newly-surfaced §1.NEW-a MESA-2024 finger sensor — every "make the vein pulsatile, then read it like a pulse oximeter" approach traces here. The artery–vein crosstalk problem it first exposed is the same one the §1.1 e-tattoo and the Topic-2 spectral-ratio methods now attack.

### 2.2 — Arterial Blood-Mediated Deep-Tissue Photoacoustic Oximetry (APM+): In-Vivo Human Radial-Vein sO₂ *(previously surfaced; re-confirmed this run via the Caltech coilab PDF)*
- **Authors:** Sastry et al. (Caltech). ⚠ full author line not page-confirmed this run.
- **Year / Venue:** 2026 · ***Advanced Science*** (Wiley); DOI **10.1002/advs.76366**; PubMed 42397001; PMC13334587.
- **Link:** https://advanced.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/advs.76366 · https://doi.org/10.1002/advs.76366 · https://coilab.caltech.edu/documents/34878/Sastry-2026-AdvancedScience.pdf
- **Relevance:** **8.5/10** — human, in vivo, PA, radial artery–vein pair with *venous* sO₂ reported.
- **Novelty:** Uses the **known high oxygenation of arterial blood as an in-situ fluence reference** (APM+) to correct deep-tissue PA oximetry. In 8 volunteers at the radial artery–vein pair, APM+ gave median **venous sO₂ 69.2 %** (vs 77.3 % APM, 98.5 % uncorrected linear-unmixing — a vivid illustration of how strongly fluence correction matters); phantom median estimation error ≈2.9 %. - **Integration insights:** Directly attacks the fluence-unmixing overestimation bottleneck that plagues every PA oximetry line (the same bottleneck MOBILE's depth-resolved readout and the GenMC/Hybrid-Net learned-fluence methods target). The arterial-reference logic mirrors the §1.1 artery-as-reference neck sensor, and the named carotid–IJV + flow endpoint is the direct bridge to §1.1's O₂ER goal.

### 2.5 — Harnessing Dynamic Multiple-Scattering for Pixel-Wise Retinal Oximetry in Visible-Light OCT *(previously surfaced — first logged 2026-11-14)*
- **Authors:** ⚠ search-derived, UNVERIFIED — a Science.org citation listing gives *Hui Yang, Chuanqing Zhou* as the likely author pair; not page-confirmed (ScienceDirect egress-blocked).
- **Year / Venue:** 2026 · ***Optics & Laser Technology*** **204**:116070; DOI **10.1016/j.optlastec.2026.116070**; ScienceDirect pii S0030399226014210.
- **Link / DOI:** https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0030399226014210 · https://doi.org/10.1016/j.optlastec.2026.116070
- **Relevance:** **7/10** — resolves **per-vessel arterial AND venous** retinal sO₂ and extends it to **pixel-wise maps across vascular networks**, non-invasively (vis-OCT). ⚠ retinal-vessel-level, not a named deep-vein SvO₂.
- **Novelty:** A **spatio-temporal statistical gating** method isolates the *multiple-scattering* (dynamic) component of turbid-medium light by mapping the internal light field onto a **random matrix** (Tracy-Widom noise-floor estimation, Marchenko-Pastur compensation), separating the dynamic blood signal from the static tissue background. This suppresses the static-background contamination that has blocked **pixel-wise** vis-OCT oximetry, extending sO₂ estimation beyond large vessels into the capillary/vascular network.
- **Integration insights:** A genuine methods advance on the §2.4 vis-OCT venous-oximetry line — moving from **vessel-averaged** to **pixel-wise** per-vessel sO₂ is the step needed to read small veins and the venular network. Because it attacks multiple-scattering contamination (the same physics dcBLL models), it is directly complementary to the modeling-side corpus. **Watch for a journal-confirmed author list and in-vivo venous validation figures; metadata is still snippet-only and flagged.**

- **A sparse day with two modest Topic-1 finds, no Topic-2 technique advance.** This run's two new-to-corpus items are both surface/perfusion peripheral-venous, not vessel-resolving imaging: a **2024 low-cost finger arterio-venous sensor** (§1.NEW-a) that is a cheap instantiation of the §1.5 compliance-pulsation principle, and a **new-but-extracorporeal tubing SvO₂ sensor** (§1.NEW-b) that corpus policy excludes from the ranked set. - **Two converging "make the vein readable" strategies are now visible.** Topic 1's cheap, surface route **induces venous pulsatility** (cuff-driven: §1.NEW-a finger, §1.5 compliance ancestor, §1.1 IJV-pulse, §1.3 sternal/intraosseous reservoir) and then unmixes arterial vs venous. Topic 2's imaging route **resolves the vein spatially** (PA/vis-OCT: §2.1–§2.5) and corrects fluence/scattering. Both hit the **same artery–vein crosstalk / fluence-contamination wall** — the §1.NEW-a sensor's signal-separation problem is the surface analogue of APM+'s arterial-reference fluence correction (§2.2) and GenMC's learned fluence maps. The crosstalk/fluence problem is the single methodological throughline across both topics.
- **Topic 1 remains the true bottleneck — but it is where this run's movement was.** No new **vessel-resolving in-vivo** technique appeared (Topic 2), while both new items landed in Topic 1's **surface/peripheral** space. The honest state is unchanged: **vessel-resolving imaging (Topic 2) is more mature**, but the **clinically decisive, cheap, wearable surface-SvO₂ niche (Topic 1)** is where incremental new hardware keeps appearing — all still trend-monitors needing larger blood-gas-referenced validation.
- **Convergent endpoint — oxygen extraction.** The §1.1 neck O₂ER device, the §2.2 artery-referenced APM+ (which explicitly names the carotid–IJV + flow route), the §2.3 PACT flow+sO₂ work, the MOBILE depth-resolved occlusion readout, and the ULM+PA co-registration lead all point at the same destination: **co-registered per-vessel flow and oxygenation → regional O₂ extraction / CMRO₂**, non-invasively — the field's real goal, beyond a single SvO₂ number.
