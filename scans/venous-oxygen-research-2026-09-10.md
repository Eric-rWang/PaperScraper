### 2.5 — Improved Sensitivity in Large Field-of-View Multispectral Laser-Scanning Photoacoustic Microscopy for Measuring Oxygen Saturation In Vivo *(⭐ NEW to corpus this scan — first logged 2026-09-10 · PA-microscopy instrumentation, venous-transferable)*
- **Authors:** Zafar, Khansari, Manwar, Avanaki (Kamran Avanaki group). ⚠ full author list snippet-confirmed on Wiley + PMC index; not page-verified (egress-blocked).
- **Year / Venue:** 2026 · ***Journal of Biophotonics*** 19(3):e202500378. **DOI 10.1002/jbio.202500378**; PMC12980567; PubMed 41213654. ⚠ pages egress-blocked; metadata cross-checked on ≥3 snippets (Wiley + PMC + PubMed).
- **Link / DOI:** https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/jbio.202500378 · https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41213654/
- **Relevance:** **6.5/10** — not a venous *measurement* paper, but the sensitivity gain it delivers is concentrated in the **deoxyhemoglobin-dominant (low-sO₂ / venous) regime**, making it directly transferable to venous PA oximetry; included per the task's "technique directly transferable to venous" rule.
- **Novelty:** Addresses the chronic weakness of laser-scanning PAM — low detection sensitivity from a flat transducer with non-coaxial alignment, worsened at wide field-of-view (coarser lateral resolution). The system splits the sO₂ measurement into **two wavelength groups: an isosbestic pair (532/545 nm)** for total-hemoglobin normalization and a **deoxyhemoglobin-dominant pair (545/558 nm)** to maximize contrast where deoxyHb absorption diverges from oxyHb — i.e., precisely the spectral region that carries the venous (low-saturation) signal. Reported to improve detected-signal SNR and sO₂ accuracy, validated in phantom and in vivo.
- **Integration insights:** Venous PA oximetry is *doubly* disadvantaged — weak absorption contrast in the deoxygenated compartment (§2.2's spectral-coloring problem) **and** a tighter thermal-fluence budget (09-09's §2.5 maximum-fluence result, where thermal bias appears first in veins). This paper attacks the first half from the front end: rather than pushing fluence (which the fluence-ceiling paper forbids in the venous compartment), it recovers SNR by **choosing wavelength pairs that maximize deoxyHb contrast**. That is exactly the right lever for the venous regime — buy sensitivity spectrally, not thermally. Pairs naturally with APM+'s fluence-reference correction (§2.2) and the FD-PAM resonant-transducer reliability result (standing lead): three independent routes to trustworthy low-sO₂ PA readout.

## Cross-topic synthesis

1. **An all-Topic-2, all-enabling-technology day: three new instrumentation/metrology papers, zero new venous measurements.** After the modest 09-09 cycle (two new *Photoacoustics* accuracy papers), today continued the field's baseline texture — the frontier moved on the *enabling* side (front-end sensitivity, phantom ground-truth) rather than on any new non-invasive *venous* measurement. This is the honest shape of the niche: true venous work arrives in bursts; most days the supporting toolchain advances.

2. **The 2026 literature is now assembling a full *validation stack* for low-sO₂/venous optical oximetry.** Today's two phantom papers slot into complementary layers: the **dye phantom** (§2.5 box) gives PA a stable, tunable, non-clotting *test object* across the venous band, while **IPCR** gives NIRS a *continuous ground-truth SO₂* (2.1 % uncertainty) that tracks dynamic desaturation into the venous range. Together with the earlier sulfate-phantom (arXiv:2512.01458) and blood-mimicking anchors, the community now has PA *and* NIRS metrology substrates specifically capable of the low-saturation regime venous oximetry lives in — the precondition for any regulatory-grade venous claim.

3. **Front-end sensitivity is the newly-active lever, and it is the *right* lever for veins.** The LS-PAM paper (§2.5) buys SNR by **choosing deoxyHb-dominant wavelength pairs (545/558 nm)** rather than by raising fluence.
