### 1.1 — Non-invasive, Continuous Venous Oxygen Saturation and Oxygen Extraction Estimation from the Internal Jugular Vein *(previously surfaced — the flagship new-generation true-venous device)*
- **Authors:** ⚠ search-derived (bioengineering group associated with the jugular-optical-sensing line).
- **Year / Venue:** 2025 · **IEEE EMBC 2025** proceedings. IEEE Xplore doc 11254941; PubMed 41335695; DOI 10.1109/EMBC58623.2025.11254941. ⚠ egress-blocked; metadata cross-checked on ≥2 snippets this run.
- **Link:** https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/11254941/ · https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41335695/ · https://doi.org/10.1109/embc58623.2025.11254941
- **Relevance:** **8.5/10** — directly targets non-invasive, continuous **true venous** sO₂ at the IJV plus cerebral O₂ extraction.
- **Novelty:** Flexible/conformal neck sensor array reads the **IJV pulse + breathing-induced modulation** (peripheral veins being non-pulsatile) to estimate SvO₂ ≈ **65.7–80.9 %** (SD 1.67 % for IJV-pulse, 4.26 % for breathing modulation) and a per-subject **cerebral O₂-extraction ratio (O₂ER)** in 6 healthy adults; co-registers carotid artery and IJV at one neck site, working in both upright and supine positions. Authors explicitly flag artery–vein **crosstalk mitigation** and larger-cohort blood-gas validation as next steps.
- **Integration insights:** The extraction-aware wearable endpoint of the whole Topic-1 program; the artery serves as an in-situ reference (same logic as APM+, §2.2). §1.3 (sternal-marrow reservoir) and §1.2 (jugular/cerebral NIRS) are the alternative-site and surface-NIRS counterparts to this vessel-targeted pulse device. Today's new retinal A-V paper (§2.5) is the ocular analogue of its cerebral-O₂ER endpoint — extraction read at a different accessible vascular bed.

### 1.2 — Near-Infrared Spectroscopy for Estimating Mixed Venous Oxygen Saturation and Cardiac Output During Off-Pump Coronary Artery Bypass Grafting: A Prospective Observational Study *(previously surfaced — first logged 2026-09-08)*
- **Authors:** ⚠ search-derived — not page-confirmed (PubMed egress-blocked).
- **Year / Venue:** 2026 · venue **not page-confirmed** (⚠ search-derived; anesthesia/monitoring journal). PubMed 42606675. (Search snippet dates the publication to August 2026.)
- **Link / DOI:** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42606675/ ⚠ egress-blocked; DOI not captured.
- **Relevance:** **7.5/10** — non-invasive SvO₂ surrogate via **jugular + cerebral NIRS**, validated head-to-head against **pulmonary-artery-catheter SvO₂** in adults.
- **Novelty:** Tests two non-invasive NIRS surrogates in 25 OPCAB patients — (a) **rSjvO₂** over the left IJV (bias 2.0 %, wide LoA −14.9/+18.9 %) and (b) **baseline-calibrated cerebral NIRS (cal-rScO₂)** on the forehead (bias 0.8 %, LoA −7.8/+9.4 %, good SvO₂ < 65 % discrimination and trending). **NIRS-derived cardiac output failed** (≈33 % error).
- **Integration insights:** The most rigorous 2026 adult validation yet of jugular/cerebral NIRS as an SvO₂ *trend* monitor. Tellingly, **baseline-calibrated cerebral NIRS out-tracked a sensor placed right over the IJV** — a strong argument for the vessel-*resolving* imaging of Topic 2 (PA, vis-OCT) over surface averaging. Clean SvO₂ < 65 % discrimination is the clinically actionable low-SvO₂-alarm endpoint. Note the contrast with today's retinal paper (§2.5): NIRS-derived *cardiac output* failed here, whereas the retinal A-V *difference* tracked cardiac output — the extraction ratio may carry the hemodynamic signal that absolute NIRS StO₂ does not.

### 1.3 — Monitoring Changes in Mixed Venous Oxygen Saturation Using Photoplethysmography on the Sternum — A Proof of Concept Study *(previously surfaced — first logged 2026-09-08)*
- **Authors:** ⚠ search-derived — not page-confirmed (PubMed egress-blocked). Likely the Scandinavian intraosseous-sternal-PPG group behind the earlier porcine sternal-oximetry work (*J. Clin. Monit. Comput.* 10.1007/s10877-023-00980-z).
- **Year / Venue:** 2026 · venue **not page-confirmed** (⚠ search-derived). PubMed 42527850.
- **Link / DOI:** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42527850/ ⚠ egress-blocked; DOI not captured.
- **Relevance:** **8/10** — non-invasive, continuous **true mixed-venous SvO₂** with a pulmonary-artery-catheter reference; a genuinely new anatomical site (sternum) for venous oximetry.
- **Novelty:** Exploits **sternal intramedullary (intraosseous) blood**, whose saturation tracks central/mixed-venous saturation, read **transcutaneously with a PPG probe** on the sternum. In anesthetized pigs, SvO₂ was driven down by stepwise hypoxia and staged hemorrhage while the non-invasive sternal signal (nSsO₂) was calibrated against pulmonary-artery SvO₂: **linear nSsO₂–SvO₂ correlation, ~87 % concordance** for tracking SvO₂ changes.
- **Integration insights:** A distinct physical route to non-invasive venous sO₂: the **bone marrow acts as a naturally venous-weighted optical reservoir**, sidestepping the artery–vein disambiguation problem. Trend-grade (~87 %) performance sets a realistic accuracy bar; a natural fusion target for the pulse-modulation math of §1.5.

### 1.5 — Proof-of-Concept Non-Invasive Estimation of Peripheral Venous Oxygen Saturation (arterio-venous compliance pulse-modulation) *(previously surfaced — foundational method ancestor)*
- **Year / Venue:** 2017 · *BioMedical Engineering OnLine* 16:60; DOI 10.1186/s12938-017-0351-x; PMC5437414.
- **Link:** https://biomedical-engineering-online.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/s12938-017-0351-x
- **Relevance:** **7/10** — the original non-invasive peripheral-venous (SpvO₂) optical concept.
- **Novelty:** Uses the **compliance difference between arteries and veins** to induce artificial respiration-like modulation that makes venous blood pulsatile, then reads the induced PPG to build a calibration model for regional venous sO₂; validated in 8 healthy volunteers with arm-lift/occlusion maneuvers against blood-gas references. Estimated SpvO₂ ran ≈3.1 ± 4.2 % below arterial saturation. (Its earlier sibling — *A new method for noninvasive venous blood oxygen detection*, 10.1186/s12938-016-0208-8, 2016 — and the 2007 during-bypass venous-oximetry paper, PubMed 17664681, both in-corpus.)
- **Integration insights:** The method ancestor of the §1.3 sternal-PPG and §1.1 IJV-pulse devices — every "make the vein pulsatile, then read it like a pulse oximeter" approach traces here. Its modest accuracy is exactly what the deep-diffuse-optics pulsation-decomposition ideas in Topic 2 aim to improve.

> **Non-optical non-invasive venous anchor (not ranked, in corpus):** *Development and Validation of a Noninvasive Model of Mixed Venous Oxygen Saturation in Heart Failure* (McDiarmid et al.; *JACC: Advances* 5(1):102484, Jan 2026; 10.1016/j.jacadv.2025.102484; PMC12869880; PubMed 41609283). **Cardiac-MRI T₂ mapping** yields imaging-derived SvO₂ (iSvO₂) validated in a 628-patient HF cohort — non-invasive and *true mixed-venous*, but MRI-based, not optical. Pulse-oximeter-like probe over the **external jugular vein**; correctly identified the EJV waveform and returned SvO₂ ≈ 70.4–75.3 % in 3 subjects — the earlier sibling of §1.1.
> **DNN + Monte-Carlo IJV NIRS (not ranked, in corpus):** *Quantifying Changes in Oxygen Saturation of the Internal Jugular Vein In Vivo Using Deep Neural Networks and Subject-Specific 3D Monte Carlo Models* (*Optics Letters* 49(10):2669, 2024). ### 2.2 — Arterial Blood-Mediated Deep-Tissue Photoacoustic Oximetry (APM+): In-Vivo Human Radial-Vein sO₂ *(previously surfaced)*
- **Authors:** Sastry, Olick-Gibson et al.
- **Year / Venue:** 2026 · *Advanced Science* (Wiley), 10.1002/advs.76366; PMC13334587; PubMed 42397001. ⚠ egress-blocked; abstract re-confirmed via index snippet.
- **Link:** https://advanced.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/advs.76366 · https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13334587/
- **Relevance:** **9/10** — solves the fluence problem for deep-tissue PA sO₂; reports in-vivo human values on the radial artery–vein pair (n = 8).
- **Novelty:** Uses high, known arterial oxygenation as a local in-situ **fluence reference** to correct spectral coloring, cutting median sO₂ error to **2.9 %** (vs 9.8 % for linear unmixing); human **venous sO₂ median 72.3 %** (IQR 8.9 %, vs 34.4 % for linear unmixing) on the radial vein, robust through ex-vivo tissue up to 1.5 cm. Re-confirmed this run: recovered the parabolic laminar-flow wavefront inside a deep vein in vivo in real time.
- **Integration insights:** Directly attacks the single biggest barrier to quantitative deep venous PA — unknown local fluence — using the artery as a self-calibrator, the same artery-as-reference logic §1.1 applies in the pulse domain.

### 2.4 — Adaptive Spectroscopic Visible-Light OCT for Clinical Retinal Oximetry (ADS-vis-OCT) *(previously surfaced)*
- **Authors:** Chen Q., Nolen S.L., Spencer S.A., Yi J. (Johns Hopkins).
- **Year / Venue:** 2023 · *Communications Medicine* (Nature), s43856-023-00288-8; PubMed 37095177; PMC10126115.
- **Link:** https://www.nature.com/articles/s43856-023-00288-8 ⚠ egress-blocked; metadata via snippet.
- **Relevance:** **8/10** — resolves *per-vessel arterial and venous* retinal sO₂ non-invasively.
- **Novelty:** Adaptively removes vessel-specific spectral contaminants; RMSE ≈ 2.1 % vs pulse oximeter in major arteries, repeatability SD ≈ 2.3 % in *veins*; ex-vivo phantom agreement within 1 % bias across 0–100 % sO₂. Demonstrated across glaucoma, ischemia, DR, CRVO, sickle-cell retinopathy.
- **Integration insights:** Sets the accuracy ceiling for optical venous oximetry in an accessible vascular bed; the adaptive spectral-correction idea is transferable to PA/DOT venous inversion where spectral coloring dominates error (cf. §2.2). Its compute cost is directly addressed by the DGFT acceleration paper (standing lead below, same group). Today's retinal A-V paper (§2.5) is the clinical-application counterpart in the same vascular bed — a lower-tech (dual-wavelength fundus) route to the same per-vessel arterial+venous readout.

### 2.5 — Retinal Arteriovenous Oxygen Saturation Difference Is Inversely Associated with Cardiac Output Measured by Echocardiography: A Non-Invasive Pilot Study *(NEW to corpus — first surfaced 2026-09-23 · per-vessel retinal venous oximetry, clinical-correlation application)*
- **Authors:** Křivohlavý T., Lada M., Malý M., et al. ⚠ search-derived (author list from index snippet; full list not page-confirmed — Military University Hospital Prague, Dept. of Internal Medicine + Dept. of Ophthalmology).
- **Year / Venue:** 2026 · ***Scientific Reports*** 16:26403; DOI **10.1038/s41598-026-56918-3**; single-centre prospective observational pilot. ⚠ nature.com egress-blocked this run; metadata cross-checked on ≥2 index snippets.
- **Link / DOI:** https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-026-56918-3 · https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-026-56918-3
- **Relevance:** **6.5/10** — measures **true per-vessel retinal venous sO₂** non-invasively (not tissue-level StO₂), and its **arteriovenous saturation difference is an oxygen-extraction proxy** tied to systemic hemodynamics. Scored below the four PA/vis-OCT anchors because the imaging *technique* (dual-wavelength fundus oximetry) is well-established and the study is a clinical-correlation pilot rather than a new measurement modality; scored above the SFDI VOT paper on venous specificity because it reads intravascular venous sO₂ directly.

New here: contemporary echocardiographic cardiac-output reference and confirmation of the inverse A-V-difference↔CO relationship in a fresh cohort.
- **Integration insights:** (1) **The A-V difference is the ocular analogue of the cerebral O₂-extraction ratio** that the §1.1 IJV device targets and of the microvascular-vs-macrovascular extraction gap that SFDI (standing lead) maps — extraction, not absolute SvO₂, is emerging as the shared clinically-actionable readout across accessible vascular beds. (2) It is a **low-cost, widely-deployable counterweight to the high-end §2.4 vis-OCT** in the same retinal bed: fundus oximetry trades vis-OCT's per-vessel accuracy for clinical accessibility, and this paper shows the cruder readout still carries systemic-hemodynamic signal. (3) Directly contrasts with §1.2, where NIRS-derived *cardiac output* failed while NIRS SvO₂-trend held — here the venous-weighted *extraction* metric is what tracks cardiac output, suggesting the extraction ratio is the more robust hemodynamic surrogate. Caveat: retinal saturation is a regional CNS-bed measure, a proxy for — not a direct read of — central/mixed venous SvO₂.

## Cross-topic synthesis

**Headline (2026-09-23):** After eight runs without a new in-scope paper, **one genuinely new-to-corpus in-scope item surfaced** — Křivohlavý et al.'s *Scientific Reports* 2026 pilot showing the **retinal arteriovenous oxygen-saturation difference is inversely associated with echocardiographic cardiac output** (§2.5). It is a *clinical-correlation application* of an established per-vessel retinal venous-oximetry technique (dual-wavelength fundus oximetry), not a new imaging modality, so it enters ranked at a candid 6.5/10 — above the SFDI VOT paper on venous specificity (it reads true intravascular venous sO₂), below the four PA/vis-OCT anchors on methodological novelty. No new *true-venous* Topic-1 device paper and no new PA/vis-OCT venous *method* surfaced; twelve searches otherwise returned only papers already logged across the prior 62 scans.

**What today's item shifts.** The retinal A-V paper sharpens a theme the corpus has been circling: **oxygen *extraction* (the A-V difference), not absolute SvO₂, is emerging as the shared, clinically-actionable readout** across accessible vascular beds. The §1.1 IJV device reports a cerebral O₂-extraction ratio; SFDI's VOT reads a microvascular-vs-macrovascular extraction gap; and now retinal A-V difference tracks systemic cardiac output where NIRS-derived cardiac output failed outright (§1.2). Three different beds (neck vein, forearm skin, retina), three different modalities (pulse-optics, SFDI, fundus oximetry), converging on extraction as the robust hemodynamic signal.

**State of the field, restated.** The corpus still clusters around one structural problem — **compartment disambiguation**, separating the venous signal from arterial and tissue background — attacked at four scales that remain the live frontier: (1) **single-vessel resolution** (vis-OCT §2.4, PACT §2.3, TD-HSI, fundus-camera and retinal-HSI methods, and now the §2.5 retinal A-V application) resolves the vein directly; (2) **temporal isolation** (respiration-modulated NIRS §1.2, the IJV-pulse device §1.1, the 2017 compliance-modulation ancestor §1.5) separates the venous pulsation in time; (3) **an anatomical venous reservoir** (sternal marrow §1.3; portal-venous liver) sidesteps disambiguation by reading a naturally venous-weighted source; and (4) **spatial-heterogeneity mapping** (SFDI VOT, standing lead) shows tissue-scale micro- vs macrovascular ROIs desaturate differently. APM+ (§2.2) cuts across all four by using the artery as an in-situ fluence anchor; the DGFT lead sits orthogonal on the *speed* axis every deployable venous optical monitor will eventually need.

**Bottom line for the program.** Today delivered a real but modest addition: a clinical validation that per-vessel retinal venous oximetry carries systemic-hemodynamic signal via the extraction ratio, deployable on stock fundus hardware (cf. the low-cost fundus-oximetry instrument lead). The enabling-technology layer — throughput (DGFT), instruments (fiber-array PACT, wearable FD-NIRS, fundus oximetry), ground-truth (dye/sulfate phantoms, IPCR), learned inversion (Hybrid-Net, PINN), and fluence/depth correction (ISDC, maximum-fluence, FD-PAM) — is now well-populated. The scarce, still-awaited item remains a genuinely new venous *measurement modality* — a new way to read the vein, not another enabler, phantom, or clinical correlation of an existing readout.
