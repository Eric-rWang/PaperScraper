### 1.1 — Non-invasive, Continuous Venous Oxygen Saturation and Oxygen Extraction Estimation from the Internal Jugular Vein *(previously surfaced — the flagship new-generation true-venous device)*
- **Authors:** ⚠ search-derived (bioengineering group associated with the jugular-optical-sensing line).
- **Year / Venue:** 2025 · **IEEE EMBC 2025** proceedings. IEEE Xplore doc 11254941; PubMed 41335695; DOI 10.1109/EMBC58623.2025.11254941. ⚠ egress-blocked; metadata cross-checked on ≥2 snippets this run.
- **Link:** https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/11254941/ · https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41335695/ · https://doi.org/10.1109/embc58623.2025.11254941
- **Relevance:** **8.5/10** — directly targets non-invasive, continuous **true venous** sO₂ at the IJV plus cerebral O₂ extraction.
- **Novelty:** Flexible/conformal neck sensor array reads the **IJV pulse + breathing-induced modulation** (peripheral veins being non-pulsatile) to estimate SvO₂ ≈ **65.7–80.9 %** (SD 1.67 % for IJV-pulse, 4.26 % for breathing modulation) and a per-subject **cerebral O₂-extraction ratio (O₂ER)** in 6 healthy adults; co-registers carotid artery and IJV at one neck site, working in both upright and supine positions. Authors explicitly flag artery–vein **crosstalk mitigation** and larger-cohort blood-gas validation as next steps.
- **Integration insights:** The extraction-aware wearable endpoint of the whole Topic-1 program; the artery serves as an in-situ reference (same logic as APM+, §2.2). §1.3 (sternal-marrow reservoir) and §1.2 (jugular/cerebral NIRS) are the alternative-site and surface-NIRS counterparts to this vessel-targeted pulse device.

### 1.2 — Near-Infrared Spectroscopy for Estimating Mixed Venous Oxygen Saturation and Cardiac Output During Off-Pump Coronary Artery Bypass Grafting: A Prospective Observational Study *(previously surfaced — first logged 2026-09-08)*
- **Authors:** ⚠ search-derived — not page-confirmed (PubMed egress-blocked).
- **Year / Venue:** 2026 · venue **not page-confirmed** (⚠ search-derived; anesthesia/monitoring journal). PubMed 42606675. (Search snippet dates the publication to August 2026.)
- **Link / DOI:** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42606675/ ⚠ egress-blocked; DOI not captured.
- **Relevance:** **7.5/10** — non-invasive SvO₂ surrogate via **jugular + cerebral NIRS**, validated head-to-head against **pulmonary-artery-catheter SvO₂** in adults.
- **Novelty:** Tests two non-invasive NIRS surrogates in 25 OPCAB patients — (a) **rSjvO₂** over the left IJV (bias 2.0 %, wide LoA −14.9/+18.9 %) and (b) **baseline-calibrated cerebral NIRS (cal-rScO₂)** on the forehead (bias 0.8 %, LoA −7.8/+9.4 %, good SvO₂ < 65 % discrimination and trending). **NIRS-derived cardiac output failed** (≈33 % error).
- **Integration insights:** The most rigorous 2026 adult validation yet of jugular/cerebral NIRS as an SvO₂ *trend* monitor. Tellingly, **baseline-calibrated cerebral NIRS out-tracked a sensor placed right over the IJV** — a strong argument for the vessel-*resolving* imaging of Topic 2 (PA, vis-OCT) over surface averaging. Clean SvO₂ < 65 % discrimination is the clinically actionable low-SvO₂-alarm endpoint.

### 1.3 — Monitoring Changes in Mixed Venous Oxygen Saturation Using Photoplethysmography on the Sternum — A Proof of Concept Study *(previously surfaced — first logged 2026-09-08)*
- **Authors:** ⚠ search-derived — not page-confirmed (PubMed egress-blocked). Likely the Scandinavian intraosseous-sternal-PPG group behind the earlier porcine sternal-oximetry work (*J. Clin. Monit. Comput.* 10.1007/s10877-023-00980-z).
- **Year / Venue:** 2026 · venue **not page-confirmed** (⚠ search-derived). PubMed 42527850.
- **Link / DOI:** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42527850/ ⚠ egress-blocked; DOI not captured.
- **Relevance:** **8/10** — non-invasive, continuous **true mixed-venous SvO₂** with a pulmonary-artery-catheter reference; a genuinely new anatomical site (sternum) for venous oximetry.
- **Novelty:** Exploits **sternal intramedullary (intraosseous) blood**, whose saturation tracks central/mixed-venous saturation, read **transcutaneously with a PPG probe** on the sternum. In anesthetized pigs, SvO₂ was driven down by stepwise hypoxia and staged hemorrhage while the non-invasive sternal signal (nSsO₂) was calibrated against pulmonary-artery SvO₂: **linear nSsO₂–SvO₂ correlation, ~87 % concordance** for tracking SvO₂ changes.
- **Integration insights:** A distinct physical route to non-invasive venous sO₂: the **bone marrow acts as a naturally venous-weighted optical reservoir**, sidestepping the artery–vein disambiguation problem. Trend-grade (~87 %) performance sets a realistic accuracy bar; a natural fusion target for the pulse-modulation math of §1.5.

### 1.5 — Proof-of-Concept Non-Invasive Estimation of Peripheral Venous Oxygen Saturation (arterio-venous compliance pulse-modulation) *(previously surfaced — foundational method ancestor)*
- **Year / Venue:** 2017 · *BioMedical Engineering OnLine* 16:60; DOI 10.1186/s12938-017-0351-x; PMC5437414.
- **Link:** https://biomedical-engineering-online.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/s12938-017-0351-x
- **Relevance:** **7/10** — the original non-invasive peripheral-venous (SpvO₂) optical concept.
- **Novelty:** Uses the **compliance difference between arteries and veins** to induce artificial respiration-like modulation that makes venous blood pulsatile, then reads the induced PPG to build a calibration model for regional venous sO₂; validated in 8 healthy volunteers with arm-lift/occlusion maneuvers against blood-gas references. Estimated SpvO₂ ran ≈3.1 ± 4.2 % below arterial saturation. (Its earlier sibling — *A new method for noninvasive venous blood oxygen detection*, 10.1186/s12938-016-0208-8, 2016; PMC4952148 — re-surfaced this run and is in-corpus.)
- **Integration insights:** The method ancestor of the §1.3 sternal-PPG and §1.1 IJV-pulse devices — every "make the vein pulsatile, then read it like a pulse oximeter" approach traces here (including the muscle-stimulation PPG variant). Its modest accuracy is exactly what the deep-diffuse-optics pulsation-decomposition ideas in Topic 2 aim to improve.

> **Non-optical non-invasive venous anchor (not ranked, in corpus):** *Development and Validation of a Noninvasive Model of Mixed Venous Oxygen Saturation in Heart Failure* (McDiarmid et al.; *JACC: Advances* 5(1):102484, Jan 2026; 10.1016/j.jacadv.2025.102484; PMC12869880; PubMed 41609283). **Cardiac-MRI T₂ mapping** yields imaging-derived SvO₂ (iSvO₂) validated in a 628-patient HF cohort — non-invasive and *true mixed-venous*, but MRI-based, not optical. Pulse-oximeter-like probe over the **external jugular vein**; correctly identified the EJV waveform and returned SvO₂ ≈ 70.4–75.3 % in 3 subjects — the earlier sibling of §1.1.
> **DNN + Monte-Carlo IJV NIRS (not ranked, in corpus):** *Quantifying Changes in Oxygen Saturation of the Internal Jugular Vein In Vivo Using Deep Neural Networks and Subject-Specific 3D Monte Carlo Models* (*Optics Letters* 49(10):2669, 2024). > **Wearable venous-optics anchor (not ranked, in corpus):** *Towards Simultaneous Non-Invasive Arterial and Venous Oxygenation Monitoring with a Wearable Optical E-Tattoo* (Tan et al., UT Austin / Lu group; IEEE EMBC 2023, doc 10340010; PubMed 38083768; patent US2025/0025074). Wrist e-tattoo measuring artery + vein pulses simultaneously; addresses artery/vein **crosstalk via spatial filtering** — the same disambiguation problem tackled optically. Re-surfaced again this run.

### 2.2 — Arterial Blood-Mediated Deep-Tissue Photoacoustic Oximetry (APM+): In-Vivo Human Radial-Vein sO₂ *(previously surfaced)*
- **Authors:** Sastry, Olick-Gibson et al.
- **Year / Venue:** 2026 · *Advanced Science* (Wiley), 10.1002/advs.76366; PMC13334587; PubMed 42397001. ⚠ egress-blocked; abstract re-confirmed via index snippet.
- **Link:** https://advanced.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/advs.76366 · https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13334587/
- **Relevance:** **9/10** — solves the fluence problem for deep-tissue PA sO₂; reports in-vivo human values on the radial artery–vein pair (n = 8).
- **Novelty:** Uses high, known arterial oxygenation as a local in-situ **fluence reference** to correct spectral coloring, cutting median sO₂ error to **2.9 %** (vs 9.8 % for linear unmixing); human **venous sO₂ median 72.3 %** (IQR 8.9 %, vs 34.4 % for linear unmixing) on the radial vein, robust through ex-vivo tissue up to 1.5 cm.
- **Integration insights:** Directly attacks the single biggest barrier to quantitative deep venous PA — unknown local fluence — using the artery as a self-calibrator, the same artery-as-reference logic §1.1 applies in the pulse domain.

### 2.3 — Oxygenation Heterogeneity Facilitates Spatiotemporal Flow-Pattern Visualization Inside Human Blood Vessels Using PACT *(previously surfaced)*
- **Year / Venue:** 2024 · PACT venue; PMC11161372; PubMed 38855671. ⚠ egress-blocked; abstract re-confirmed via snippet.
- **Link:** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11161372/

### 2.4 — Adaptive Spectroscopic Visible-Light OCT for Clinical Retinal Oximetry (ADS-vis-OCT) *(previously surfaced)*
- **Authors:** Chen Q., Nolen S.L., Spencer S.A., Yi J. (Johns Hopkins).
- **Year / Venue:** 2023 · *Communications Medicine* (Nature), s43856-023-00288-8; PubMed 37095177; PMC10126115.
- **Link:** https://www.nature.com/articles/s43856-023-00288-8 ⚠ egress-blocked; metadata via snippet.
- **Relevance:** **8/10** — resolves *per-vessel arterial and venous* retinal sO₂ non-invasively.
- **Novelty:** Adaptively removes vessel-specific spectral contaminants; RMSE ≈ 2.1 % vs pulse oximeter in major arteries, repeatability SD ≈ 2.3 % in *veins*; ex-vivo phantom agreement within 1 % bias across 0–100 % sO₂. Demonstrated across glaucoma, ischemia, DR, CRVO, sickle-cell retinopathy.
- **Integration insights:** Sets the accuracy ceiling for optical venous oximetry in an accessible vascular bed; the adaptive spectral-correction idea is transferable to PA/DOT venous inversion where spectral coloring dominates error (cf. §2.2). Its compute cost is directly addressed by the DGFT acceleration paper (standing lead below, same group).

### 2.5 — Rapid Quantitative Imaging of Heterogeneous Tissue Hemoglobin Dynamics Using Spatial Frequency Domain Imaging *(previously surfaced — first logged 2026-09-11 · SFDI, the named technique; venous-extraction-relevant but tissue-level)*
- **Authors:** Gordon T. Kennedy, Rebecca Rowland, Amaan Mazhar, David J. Cuccia, Anthony J. Durkin (Beckman Laser Institute, UC Irvine / Modulated Imaging lineage — the canonical SFDI-inventor group). ⚠ author list snippet-confirmed; not page-verified (egress-blocked).
- **Year / Venue:** 2026 · ***Journal of Vascular Research*** 63(4):255–264 (published 2026-07-16); **PMC13245941**; **PubMed 41824643**; Karger.
- **Link / DOI:** https://karger.com/jvr/article/63/4/255/946265/ · https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13245941/ · https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41824643/
- **Relevance:** **6/10** — **tissue-level StO₂, not intravascular SvO₂** (flagged), but the vascular-occlusion-test design directly interrogates the **venous / oxygen-extraction** compartment, and SFDI is one of the task's explicitly-named target techniques with no other recent (2026) representative in the ranked five.
- **Novelty:** Wide-field **SFDI** maps absolute StO₂ and total hemoglobin — resolved into a **superficial papillary-dermis compartment (HbT1)** and a **deeper compartment (HbT2)** — through a forearm **vascular occlusion test (VOT)** in 13 subjects (baseline → 4 min occlusion → release). Key result: **StO₂ differs significantly between microvascular-only and macrovascular ROIs at all time points**, with **microvascular ROIs showing the larger StO₂ drop during occlusion (higher oxygen extraction)**. Thesis: **spatial heterogeneity must be accounted for** when comparing tissue hemodynamics across time and subjects.
- **Integration insights:** (1) **The VOT is a venous-compartment assay** — during occlusion the StO₂ decline rate is set by tissue O₂ consumption draining the venous-weighted microvasculature, so SFDI's microvascular ROI reads a spatially-resolved *venous* desaturation, the imaging analogue of the respiration-isolated venous signal in §1.2/§1.1. (2) **Macro- vs microvascular ROI separation is the spatial version of the artery/vein disambiguation problem** that PACT (§2.3) and vis-OCT (§2.4) solve at the single-vessel scale. (3) **The HbT1/HbT2 depth split** parallels the depth-compensation ambition of the PA community (ISDC). Caveat: **volume-averaged tissue StO₂**, so it informs *how to read* the venous extraction signal, not an absolute SvO₂ number.

APM+ (§2.2) cuts across all four by using the artery as an in-situ fluence anchor; the DGFT lead sits orthogonal to all four on the *speed* axis that any of them will eventually need.
