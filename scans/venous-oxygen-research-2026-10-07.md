- **Authors:** Sastry, Olick-Gibson et al. (Caltech Optical Imaging Laboratory, L.V. Wang group).
- **Year / Venue:** 2026 · *Advanced Science* (Wiley), 10.1002/advs.76366; PMC13334587; PubMed 42397001. ⚠ egress-blocked; abstract re-confirmed via index snippet this run (Wiley + PMC; "July 2026 … arterial prior method APM+ … leverages high arterial blood oxygenation to calibrate the fluence").

### 2.5 — GenMC: Real-Time Generative Monte Carlo Surrogate for Quantitative Photoacoustic Imaging *(previously surfaced — first logged 09-27; PA fluence-correction with in-vivo venous sO₂ and skin-tone-bias correction)*
- **Authors:** Mengjie Shi, Feng He, Tom Vercauteren, Wenfeng Xia (King's College London). ⚠ author list search-derived (arXiv egress-blocked; cross-confirmed on ≥2 snippets).
- **Year / Venue:** 2026 · preprint **arXiv:2609.28261** (physics.med-ph), submitted Sept 2026. ⚠ arXiv egress-blocked; abstract cross-checked on ≥2 index snippets.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.28261 · https://arxiv.org/html/2609.28261
- **Relevance:** **7.5/10** — a genuinely new PA *fluence-quantification method* directly enabling quantitative venous sO₂ at depth; demonstrates in-vivo **venous oxygenation ≈ 74.49–77.93 %** and explicitly corrects a bias that most inflates *venous* estimates. Validated in blood-mimicking phantoms and **37 human volunteers spanning Fitzpatrick skin types III–V**. Scored below the four PA/vis-OCT anchors because it is a preprint fluence-correction enabler rather than a dedicated venous-validation study, but above the retinal *application* papers because it advances the measurement modality itself and reports venous values.
- **Novelty:** A **conditional GAN** learns tissue optical **fluence distributions** from anatomy + literature optical properties, with **anatomical priors from co-registered ultrasound**. Trained on Monte-Carlo synthetic data, it yields fluence maps in **< 30 ms/frame (~4 orders faster than conventional MC)**, enabling per-frame fluence compensation in real time. It targets the **melanin problem** — elevated melanin in darker skin causes superficial attenuation/clutter that **systematically overestimates venous and arterial sO₂** — and mitigates it in vivo.
- **Integration insights:** (1) Attacks the **same fluence/spectral-coloring bottleneck as APM+ (§2.2)** but by a learned-simulation route driven by co-registered US anatomy — the US-guided geometry the §2.1 human-IJV paradigm already uses. (2) Its real-time budget is the throughput analogue of the DGFT vis-OCT accelerator. (3) The **skin-tone-bias correction** is the single most program-relevant contribution — equitable venous oximetry across skin tones — addressed here *for the venous compartment specifically*. Caveats: preprint, venous values are in-vivo demonstrations not blood-gas-referenced, and it is a quantification method, not a standalone monitor.

## Cross-Topic Synthesis

- **Nothing new in the one-day window.** This is the seventh consecutive run (09-28, 09-29, 10-01, 10-02, 10-04, 10-06, 10-07) with no newly *published* in-scope optical paper (the 10-01 CANVOS and sublingual-vein entries were newly *surfaced* older work; the 10-06 capnodynamic SvO₂ trial is non-optical and excluded). The honest reading is that non-invasive *true-venous* optical oximetry remains a low-volume niche, not that the search missed something. No padding with arterial-only SpO₂ work.
- **The two topics are converging on one problem: fluence/spectral-coloring correction for the venous compartment.** APM+ (§2.2, arterial-prior physical calibration — median venous error 2.9 %), GenMC (§2.5, learned Monte-Carlo fluence + explicit melanin/venous-bias fix, validated across Fitzpatrick III–V), and dcBLL (standing lead, diffusion-corrected Beer-Lambert) are three independent routes — physical, learned, and analytic — to the *same* bottleneck that limits every deep PA and vis-OCT venous estimate. The melanin-corrected hybrid transmittance–reflectance StO₂ paper (re-surfaced this run) is a fourth, tissue-level, route to the skin-tone-equity half of that problem. This is the most active methodological front and the one most likely to produce the next genuine advance.
- **Vessel-type disambiguation ("which vessel is the vein?") is the recurring structural problem** linking Topic-1 surface methods (§1.1 IJV-pulse, §1.2 jugular-NIRS where baseline-calibrated *cerebral* NIRS out-tracked a sensor placed directly over the IJV, the e-tattoo artery–vein pairing, and the OL 49(10):2669 multi-channel-NIRS SijvO₂ model) with Topic-2 imaging (§2.1 PA-IJV, §2.3 PACT flow-from-oxygenation, §2.4 per-vessel vis-OCT, TD-HSI single-vessel). The clinical lesson from §1.2 — that vessel-*resolving* imaging beats surface averaging — is the strategic case for Topic 2.
- **Wearable/conformal form factor is the quietly recurring Topic-1 theme.** The §1.1 IJV flexible array, the UT-Austin 48-channel e-tattoo, and the miniaturized FD-DOS/FD-NIRS optode all push toward a skin-conformable, continuous, artery–vein-paired neck/wrist sensor — the physical embodiment the CANVOS trial is now testing clinically.
- **Clinical translation is the live edge.** The CANVOS ICU trial (standing lead) is the first interventional, blood-gas-referenced validation of the "reflectance PPG over the IJV" paradigm that §1.1 and the De Gruyter EJV probe pursue. Its first results would be the most consequential near-term event for this corpus — worth watching across coming scans. The capnodynamic SvO₂ trial (NCT06632197), though non-optical, is a reminder that the broader non-invasive-SvO₂ space is seeing parallel clinical-validation activity the optical methods will be benchmarked against.
- **Accuracy bars to beat, from the corpus:** commercial jugular NIRS (Mespere VO100, §1.4) and sternal-PPG trend-grade ~87 % concordance (§1.3) set the clinical floor; ADS-vis-OCT venous repeatability ~2.3–2.5 % (§2.4) and APM+ median error 2.9 % (§2.2) set the imaging ceiling in accessible/shallow beds. The open gap is **absolute, blood-gas-referenced, deep-vein** venous sO₂ in humans — which no corpus method has yet closed.
