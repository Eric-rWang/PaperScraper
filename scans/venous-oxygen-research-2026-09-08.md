### 1.1 — Monitoring Changes in Mixed Venous Oxygen Saturation Using Photoplethysmography on the Sternum — A Proof of Concept Study *(⭐ NEW to corpus this scan — first logged 2026-09-08)*
- **Authors:** ⚠ search-derived — not confirmed on index snippets (PubMed egress-blocked). Likely the Scandinavian intraosseous-sternal-PPG group behind the earlier porcine sternal-oximetry work (*J. Clin. Monit. Comput.* 10.1007/s10877-023-00980-z; sternal SpO₂ PMC6946764) — flagged for author verification next run.
- **Year / Venue:** 2026 · venue **not page-confirmed** (⚠ search-derived; likely *Physiological Measurement* or *J. Clinical Monitoring and Computing* per index snippets). PubMed 42527850.
- **Link / DOI:** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42527850/ ⚠ egress-blocked; DOI not captured — flagged for verification.
- **Relevance:** **8/10** — targets non-invasive, continuous **true mixed-venous SvO₂** with a pulmonary-artery-catheter reference; a genuinely new anatomical site (sternum) for venous oximetry.
- **Novelty:** Exploits **sternal intramedullary (intraosseous) blood**, whose saturation tracks central/mixed venous saturation, and reads it **transcutaneously with a PPG probe** on the sternum. In 15 anesthetized pigs, SvO₂ was driven down two ways — stepwise hypoxia (N₂/air mixtures) and staged hemorrhage (20 % + 20 % of blood volume) — while nSsO₂ was calibrated against pulmonary-artery SvO₂. Result: **linear nSsO₂–SvO₂ correlation and 87 % concordance (95 % CI 79.1–95.0)** for tracking SvO₂ changes. Unlike jugular methods, the sternum offers a flat, stable, central-compartment optical window that is easy to instrument and less motion-prone.
- **Integration insights:** A third distinct physical route to non-invasive venous sO₂, complementary to the IJV pulse-modulation device (§1.5) and jugular NIRS (§1.2, §1.4): here the **bone marrow acts as a naturally venous-weighted optical reservoir**, sidestepping the artery–vein disambiguation problem that dogs peripheral and neck sites. The 87 %-concordance/trend-only performance sets a realistic accuracy bar and, like §1.2 below, argues that the near-term clinical value of these methods is **trending**, not absolute single-point SvO₂. A natural fusion target for the arterio-venous compliance/pulse-modulation math of §1.5.

### 1.2 — Near-Infrared Spectroscopy for Estimating Mixed Venous Oxygen Saturation and Cardiac Output During Off-Pump Coronary Artery Bypass Grafting: A Prospective Observational Study *(⭐ NEW to corpus this scan — first logged 2026-09-08)*
- **Authors:** ⚠ search-derived — not confirmed on index snippets (PubMed egress-blocked).
- **Year / Venue:** 2026 · venue **not page-confirmed** (⚠ search-derived; anesthesia/monitoring journal). PubMed 42606675.
- **Link / DOI:** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42606675/ ⚠ egress-blocked; DOI not captured.
- **Relevance:** **7.5/10** — non-invasive SvO₂ surrogate via **jugular + cerebral NIRS**, validated head-to-head against **pulmonary-artery-catheter SvO₂** in adults; honest, quantitative agreement analysis.
- **Novelty:** Tests **two** non-invasive NIRS surrogates for mixed-venous SvO₂ in 25 OPCAB patients — (a) **rSjvO₂**, a NIRS sensor placed directly over the **left internal jugular vein** under ultrasound guidance, and (b) **baseline-calibrated cerebral NIRS (cal-rScO₂)** on the forehead — plus an attempt to derive continuous **cardiac output** from them. Findings are refreshingly candid: **rSjvO₂** had low bias (2.0 %) but wide LoA (−14.9/+18.9 %); **cal-rScO₂** performed better (bias 0.8 %, LoA −7.8/+9.4 %, excellent discrimination for SvO₂ < 65 %, good trending); **NIRS-derived cardiac output failed** (33 % percentage error, poor trending). So the venous-saturation surrogate works for trending while the derived-CO extension does not.
- **Integration insights:** The most rigorous 2026 adult validation yet of jugular/cerebral NIRS as an SvO₂ trend monitor, and a useful reality check on the whole non-invasive-venous program: **baseline-calibrated cerebral NIRS beat a sensor placed right over the IJV**, implying that for surface NIRS the tissue-compartment average can out-track a vessel-targeted reading whose signal is corrupted by artery/skin crosstalk. That directly motivates the vessel-resolving imaging methods of Topic 2 (PA, vis-OCT) and the pulse-gating strategy of §1.5, which isolate the venous compartment rather than averaging over it. The clean SvO₂ < 65 % discrimination is the clinically actionable endpoint (low-SvO₂ alarm).

### 1.3 — Non-Invasive Optical Quantification of Cerebral Tissue Oxygenation in Children with Single-Ventricle Congenital Heart Disease *(⭐ NEW to corpus this scan — first logged 2026-09-08 · tissue-level StO₂ + OEF, not true intravascular SvO₂)*
- **Authors:** ⚠ search-derived — not confirmed on index snippets.
- **Year / Venue:** 2026 · venue not page-confirmed (⚠ search-derived). PubMed 42476299.
- **Link / DOI:** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42476299/ ⚠ egress-blocked.
- **Relevance:** **6.5/10** — *tissue-level* StO₂ and oxygen-extraction fraction, **not** true intravascular SvO₂, **but** validated directly against invasive **superior-vena-cava (venous)** saturation, and OEF is the metabolic endpoint venous oximetry ultimately serves.
- **Novelty:** Compares **frequency-domain NIRS (FD-NIRS)** against clinical CW-NIRS and pulse oximetry for cerebral StO₂ and SaO₂ in **24 children with single-ventricle defects** at catheterization, with **invasive SVC and descending-aorta** saturations as ground truth. FD-NIRS gave more accurate absolute StO₂/SaO₂ than CW-NIRS/pulse oximetry, and — the notable step — delivered **stand-alone oxygen-extraction-fraction (OEF)** quantification. Prior FD-NIRS StO₂ correlated with invasive SvO₂ at R ≈ 0.74 (bias ≈ −2.8 %).
- **Integration insights:** Logged **with the explicit StO₂/OEF-not-SvO₂ caveat** the task requires. Its value to the venous program is twofold: (1) it validates a non-invasive optical readout against a genuine **central-venous (SVC)** reference in a hard, cyanotic population; (2) it delivers **OEF**, the same metabolic-extraction endpoint the EMBC IJV device (§1.5) targets via O₂ER — reinforcing that the field is converging on *extraction*, not just saturation. The FD-NIRS absolute-quantification advantage is a candidate calibration front-end for the vessel-targeted methods above.

### 2.4 — Adaptive Spectroscopic Visible-Light OCT for Clinical Retinal Oximetry (ADS-vis-OCT) *(previously surfaced — scan 06-11)*

- **Relevance:** 8/10 — resolves *per-vessel arterial and venous* retinal sO₂ non-invasively.

### 2.5 — Transabdominal Fetal Oximetry via Diffuse Optics: Principled Analysis and Demonstration in Pregnant Ovine Models *(⭐ NEW to corpus this scan — first logged 2026-09-08 · fetal SpO₂, method transferable to venous)*
- **Authors:** Weitai Qian, Rishad Raiyan Joarder, et al. (Soheil Ghiasi Lab, UC Davis). ⚠ author list search-derived (arXiv/PMC egress-blocked); primary names corroborated on ≥2 snippets.
- **Year / Venue:** 2025 (deposited Sep 25 2025) · preprint **arXiv:2509.21594** + **TechRxiv**; indexed **PMC12486049 / PubMed 41040802**. Journal-of-record not confirmed (⚠ likely preprint/under review).
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.21594 · https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12486049/ ⚠ both egress-blocked; metadata cross-checked on ≥2 snippets.
- **Relevance:** **6.5/10** — *fetal SpO₂*, not venous per se, but a **deep diffuse-optics pulsatile-oximetry** method whose core machinery (isolating one blood compartment's pulsation from diffuse transmittance through thick overlying tissue) is **directly transferable to non-invasive venous oximetry**; included per the task's "transferable technique" rule.
- **Novelty:** Derives, from first principles, fetal blood sO₂ (fSpO₂) from **non-invasively sensed diffuse light** transmitted through the maternal abdomen to the fetus and back, and introduces the **Exponential Pulsation Ratio (EPR)** as the discriminating feature, then **fuses multiple source–detector channels with machine learning**. Validated on both Monte-Carlo simulation and **in-vivo pregnant sheep**: MAE **4.81 %** (sim) / **6.85 %** (in-vivo), Pearson r = 0.81 / 0.71 — outperforming prior transabdominal approaches. This is deep (multi-centimeter) diffuse optics recovering a *specific vascular compartment's* saturation through heavy overlying tissue — the exact regime venous SvO₂ imaging must solve.

Lett. 49(10):2669) uses for anatomical variability. Substituting a venous compartment (respiration- or cuff-modulated) for the fetal one would turn this pipeline into a deep-tissue SpvO₂ estimator. Small-animal/ovine and not yet venous — hence the honest 6.5.

## Cross-topic synthesis

1. **A strongly productive cycle — four new papers, three of them true-venous, and two validated against a pulmonary-artery-catheter SvO₂ reference.** After a summer of mostly one-new-paper (or zero-new-paper) runs, this scan surfaced (a) a **sternal-PPG** proof-of-concept trending true mixed-venous SvO₂ at 87 % concordance (§1.1); (b) a rigorous **adult jugular/cerebral-NIRS vs PA-catheter SvO₂** study (§1.2); (c) an **FD-NIRS StO₂/OEF** pediatric study cross-checked against SVC (§1.3, tissue-level with caveat); and (d) a **deep diffuse-optics** transabdominal oximetry method whose pulsation-decomposition machinery is directly venous-transferable (§2.5). None was in the 49 prior scan files.

2. **The new anatomical diversity is the headline.** Topic 1 has, in a single day, added two entirely new venous optical *sites* — the **sternum** (bone-marrow blood as a venous-weighted reservoir, §1.1) and a **fresh PA-catheter-referenced test at the neck** (§1.2) — to the existing IJV (§1.5, §2.1), radial vein (§2.2), and retina (§2.4). Each site trades off differently on the field's two hard problems (artery–vein disambiguation; unknown fluence): the sternum sidesteps disambiguation via a naturally venous compartment; the neck confronts it directly and, tellingly, **cerebral-tissue NIRS out-tracked a sensor placed right over the IJV** (§1.2), a strong argument for the vessel-*resolving* imaging of Topic 2 over surface averaging.

3. **The field is converging on extraction, not just saturation.** The EMBC IJV device (§1.5, O₂ER), the FD-NIRS pediatric study (§1.3, OEF), and APM+'s deep-tissue accuracy (§2.2) all point past single-point sO₂ toward **oxygen-extraction** — the metabolic quantity catheters are actually used for. Any method that delivers extraction non-invasively (even trend-only) is more clinically compelling than one more saturation number.

4. **Accuracy anchors still frame the ceiling, and the honesty of the new work is welcome.** APM+ (§2.2, 2.9 % deep-tissue error, venous 72.3 %) and ADS-vis-OCT (§2.4, ~2.3 % venous repeatability) bracket the state of the art. Against that, the new entries report **trend-grade** performance — §1.1's 87 % concordance, §1.2's wide rSjvO₂ LoA and failed NIRS-CO, §2.5's ~5–7 % MAE, the serial-fundus paper's 8.4 % venous error — a consistent reminder that non-invasive **venous** measurement remains systematically harder than arterial, and that closing the gap means importing the fluence-correction/spectral-decoloring ideas the imaging anchors pioneered.
