### 1.1 — Non-invasive, Continuous Venous Oxygen Saturation and Oxygen Extraction Estimation from the Internal Jugular Vein *(previously surfaced — the flagship new-generation true-venous device; re-confirmed this run)*
- **Authors:** ⚠ search-derived (bioengineering group associated with the jugular-optical-sensing line).
- **Year / Venue:** 2025 · **IEEE EMBC 2025** proceedings. IEEE Xplore doc 11254941; PubMed 41335695; DOI 10.1109/EMBC58623.2025.11254941. ⚠ egress-blocked; metadata from search-index snippets (re-confirmed this run: SvO₂ estimates **65.70–80.91 %**, "within expected literature ranges," 6 healthy adults, flexible sensor array reading IJV pulsations; peripheral veins non-pulsatile so standard pulse oximetry cannot read SvO₂; "further validation with a larger cohort and comparison to blood gas analysis is necessary").
- **Link:** https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/11254941/ · https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41335695/ · https://doi.org/10.1109/embc58623.2025.11254941
- **Relevance:** **8.5/10** — directly targets non-invasive, continuous **true venous** sO₂ at the IJV plus cerebral O₂ extraction.
- **Novelty:** Flexible/conformal neck sensor array reads the **IJV pulse + breathing-induced modulation** (peripheral veins being non-pulsatile) to estimate SvO₂ ≈ **65.7–80.9 %** (SD 1.67 % for IJV-pulse, 4.26 % for breathing modulation) and a per-subject **cerebral O₂-extraction ratio (O₂ER)** in 6 healthy adults; co-registers carotid artery and IJV at one neck site, working upright and supine. Authors flag artery–vein **crosstalk mitigation** and larger-cohort blood-gas validation as next steps.
- **Integration insights:** The extraction-aware wearable endpoint of the whole Topic-1 program; the artery serves as an in-situ reference (same logic as APM+, §2.2). Sibling registry trial **NCT06004466** (National Taiwan University Hospital — plethysmographic device combining ultrasound imaging with optical tissue estimates for continuous non-invasive IJV saturation; Find-a-Trial listing reviewed 2026-05-15, enrolling ~100 surgical cancer patients aged 20–70) remains the independent clinical-translation counterpart; no published results yet. The UT-Austin **wearable optical e-tattoo** (2023 EMBC 10.1109/embc40787.2023.10340010; 2025 patent US2025/0025074; MRS Fall 2023 talk re-surfaced this run — simultaneous arterial+venous pulse → SaO₂/SvO₂ and O₂ consumption) is the conformal-array sibling pursuing the same neck/wrist artery–vein pair with spatial filtering for crosstalk.

### 1.2 — Near-Infrared Spectroscopy for Estimating Mixed Venous Oxygen Saturation and Cardiac Output During Off-Pump Coronary Artery Bypass Grafting: A Prospective Observational Study *(previously surfaced — first logged 2026-09-08; re-confirmed this run with detailed stats)*
- **Authors:** Oh et al. ⚠ first-author surname from snippet; full list not page-confirmed (PubMed/Springer egress-blocked).
- **Year / Venue:** 2026 · ***Journal of Clinical Monitoring and Computing*** (Springer); DOI **10.1007/s10877-026-01477-1**. PubMed 42606675. ⚠ re-hit this run via snippet with full numbers (see below).
- **Link / DOI:** https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10877-026-01477-1 · https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42606675/
- **Relevance:** **7.5/10** — non-invasive SvO₂ surrogate via **jugular + cerebral NIRS**, validated head-to-head against **pulmonary-artery-catheter SvO₂** in adults.
- **Novelty:** Tests two non-invasive NIRS surrogates in 25 OPCAB patients — (a) **rSjvO₂** over the left IJV (bias 2.0 %, wide LoA −14.9/+18.9 %) and (b) **baseline-calibrated cerebral NIRS (cal-rScO₂)** on the forehead (bias 0.8 %, LoA −7.8/+9.4 %); the calibrated cerebral measure reached **AUC 0.970 for detecting SvO₂ < 65 %** (sensitivity 95.9 %, specificity 89.8 %) with good trending ability. **NIRS-derived cardiac output failed** (clinically insufficient). The paper explicitly notes "a universally accepted threshold defining acceptable agreement for surrogate measures has not been established." Authors suggest jugular NIRS may help in selected patients (ultrasound pre-screening for IJV-sensor feasibility).
- **Integration insights:** The most rigorous 2026 adult validation yet of jugular/cerebral NIRS as an SvO₂ *trend* monitor. Baseline-calibrated cerebral NIRS **out-tracked a sensor placed right over the IJV** — a strong argument for the vessel-*resolving* imaging of Topic 2 over surface averaging. Clean SvO₂ < 65 % discrimination is the clinically actionable low-SvO₂-alarm endpoint.

### 1.3 — Monitoring Changes in Mixed Venous Oxygen Saturation Using Photoplethysmography on the Sternum — A Proof of Concept Study *(previously surfaced — first logged 2026-09-08; re-confirmed this run)*
- **Authors:** ⚠ search-derived — not page-confirmed. Likely the Scandinavian intraosseous-sternal-PPG group behind the earlier porcine sternal-oximetry work (*J. Clin. Monit. Comput.* 10.1007/s10877-023-00980-z).
- **Year / Venue:** 2026 · ***Medical & Biological Engineering & Computing*** (Springer); DOI **10.1007/s11517-026-03648-3**. PubMed 42527850. ⚠ egress-blocked; metadata via index snippet ("nSsO₂ calibrated against pulmonary-artery SvO₂ in anesthetized pigs during hypoxia and haemorrhage; further technical development warranted").
- **Link / DOI:** https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11517-026-03648-3 · https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42527850/
- **Relevance:** **8/10** — non-invasive, continuous **true mixed-venous SvO₂** with a pulmonary-artery-catheter reference; a genuinely new anatomical site (sternum) for venous oximetry.
- **Novelty:** Exploits **sternal intramedullary (intraosseous) blood**, whose saturation tracks central/mixed-venous saturation, read **transcutaneously with a PPG probe** on the sternum. In anesthetized pigs, SvO₂ was driven down by stepwise hypoxia and staged hemorrhage while the non-invasive sternal signal (nSsO₂) was calibrated against pulmonary-artery SvO₂: **linear nSsO₂–SvO₂ correlation, ~87 % concordance** for tracking SvO₂ changes.
- **Integration insights:** A distinct physical route to non-invasive venous sO₂: the **bone marrow acts as a naturally venous-weighted optical reservoir**, sidestepping artery–vein disambiguation — the surface, non-invasive analogue of the deep mucosal reservoirs used by the esophageal-RRS and PA-TEE probes. The 2025 bone-marrow MSOT paper (Nano Letters, PMC12893716; Topic-2 standing lead) independently demonstrated that bone-marrow sO₂ is non-invasively optoacoustically measurable and dynamically informative. The NIRSense CHF wearable's sternal sensor (NCT07874386) is a third independent bet on the same site. Trend-grade (~87 %) performance sets a realistic accuracy bar; a natural fusion target for the pulse-modulation math of §1.5.

### 1.5 — Proof-of-Concept Non-Invasive Estimation of Peripheral Venous Oxygen Saturation (arterio-venous compliance pulse-modulation) *(previously surfaced — foundational method ancestor; re-confirmed this run)*
- **Year / Venue:** 2017 · *BioMedical Engineering OnLine* 16:60; DOI 10.1186/s12938-017-0351-x; PMC5437414. ⚠ egress-blocked; re-confirmed this run via index snippet.
- **Link:** https://biomedical-engineering-online.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/s12938-017-0351-x · https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5437414/
- **Relevance:** **7/10** — the original non-invasive peripheral-venous (SpvO₂) optical concept.
- **Novelty:** Uses the **compliance difference between arteries and veins** to induce artificial respiration-like modulation that makes venous blood pulsatile, then reads the induced PPG to build a calibration model for regional venous sO₂; validated in 8 healthy volunteers with arm-lift/occlusion maneuvers against blood-gas references. Estimated SpvO₂ ran ≈3.1 ± 4.2 % below arterial saturation. (Siblings in corpus: *A new method for noninvasive venous blood oxygen detection*, 10.1186/s12938-016-0208-8, 2016, PMC4952148/PubMed 27436186, RMSE 5.31, r = 0.72; *Comparison of non-invasive peripheral venous saturations with venous blood co-oximetry* by Kyriacou's group, PMC5655584, r = 0.81; the 2007 artificial-finger-pulse during-bypass venous-oximetry paper, Echiadis et al., PubMed 17664681.)
- **Integration insights:** The method ancestor of the §1.3 sternal-PPG and §1.1 IJV-pulse devices — every "make the vein pulsatile, then read it like a pulse oximeter" approach traces here. The artery–vein crosstalk problem it first exposed is the same one the §1.1 spatial-filtering e-tattoo and the §2.5 spectral-ratio rule now attack. Its modest accuracy is exactly what the deep-diffuse-optics pulsation-decomposition ideas in Topic 2 aim to improve.

> **Standing context — non-invasive external-jugular reflectance probe + its ICU trial (previously surfaced, De Gruyter + ANZCTR; re-confirmed this run):** *Estimation of Venous Oxygen Saturation Through Non-Invasive Optical Sensing at the Jugular Veins* (cdbme-2024-2072, 10.1515/cdbme-2024-2072). Hill, Campbell, Chase, Pretty. Pulse-oximeter-like optical sensor on the external jugular vein; 3 subjects, SvO₂ 70.4/71.1/72.2 % (re-confirmed via snippet this run: EJV waveform correctly identified, SvO₂ within the healthy 60–90 % range; "calibration against blood gas analysers is still needed"; "no market device provides a continuous, non-invasive SvO₂ estimate"). The **CANVOS** reflectance-over-the-IJV ICU trial (ANZCTR **ACTRN12625000866404**; first logged 2026-10-01) is the same Christchurch group's clinical-translation step — compares a flexible optical sensor over the IJV (or femoral vein when jugular placement is not feasible) against blood-gas analysis in ICU patients; the surface-reflectance EJV sibling of the §1.1 IJV-pulse device.
> **Standing context — minimally-invasive esophageal resonance-Raman SvO₂ probe (previously surfaced, first logged 2026-10-23):** *Esophageal Resonance Raman Spectroscopy Tracks Mixed Venous Oxygenation ...* (Kheir/Romfh line; *Intensive Care Medicine Experimental* 10.1186/s40635-026-00989-z, online 2026-10-15). Esophageal RRS discriminated SvO₂ < 40 % with **AUC 0.98 vs transabdominal NIRS 0.82** in rats. Minimally-invasive/animal; the Raman twin of the PA-TEE probe below.
> **Standing context — minimally-invasive PA-TEE SvO₂ probe (previously surfaced, Wang lab, SPIE 2014/2016):** esophageal photoacoustic probe reading mixed-venous SvO₂ against the PA-catheter gold standard in swine (r² 0.99, bias 2.47 % in sheep per related patent), framed as a way to avoid the ≈10 % severe-complication risk of pulmonary-artery catheters — the photoacoustic twin of the esophageal-RRS paper.

### 2.1 — Noninvasive In Vivo Photoacoustic Measurement of Internal Jugular Venous Oxygenation in Humans *(previously surfaced — the Topic-2 anchor; re-confirmed this run)*
- **Authors:** Garcia-Uribe et al. (Wang lab). ⚠ arXiv egress-blocked; abstract re-confirmed this run on index snippets.
- **Year / Venue:** 2023 · preprint arXiv:2303.10775 (+ SPIE/Caltech-WUSTL lineage; precursor SPIE 8943E..1M, 2014).
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.10775 · https://arxiv.org/pdf/2303.10775 · https://authors.library.caltech.edu/records/5jp3j-jnz35
- **Relevance:** **9/10** — human, in vivo, PA, *venous* (IJV).
- **Novelty:** Dual-wavelength photoacoustic tomography co-registered with ultrasound (5 fps) in 7 healthy volunteers; average internal-jugular sijvO₂ **72 ± 7 %** (per-volunteer ~66–78 % per the results table this run; a conventional PA-amplitude method gives ≈66–67 % IJV vs ≈96 % carotid). >90 % of IJV drainage comes from intracranial structures, so the IJV reading can track global brain oxygen consumption; framed as a non-invasive alternative to fluoroscopy-guided jugular catheterization. A larger PA/US cohort (SKKU; 165 carotids in 117 volunteers) reports IJV **71.3 ± 6.4 %** vs carotid **93.0 ± 3.5 %**.
- **Integration insights:** The reference demonstration that deep-vein sO₂ is PA-measurable in humans. Everything else in Topic 2 either improves its quantification (GenMC standing lead; APM+ §2.2), extends the flow/vessel-type readout (PACT §2.3; ULM+PA standing lead), moves to another bed (retina §2.4, skin microvasculature §2.5), or translates it to a wearable (e-tattoo, Topic-1 standing context).

### 2.2 — Arterial Blood-Mediated Deep-Tissue Photoacoustic Oximetry (APM+): In-Vivo Human Radial-Vein sO₂ *(previously surfaced; re-confirmed this run)*
- **Authors:** Sastry et al. (Wang lab, Caltech). ⚠ re-confirmed this run via Caltech PDF + PMC snippet (received Jan 2026, accepted Jun 2026).
- **Year / Venue:** 2026 · *Advanced Science* (Wiley), 10.1002/advs.76366; PMC13334587; PubMed 42397001. Online early July 2026.
- **Link:** https://advanced.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/advs.76366 · https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13334587/ · https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42397001/
- **Relevance:** **8.5/10** — human, in vivo, PA, radial artery–vein pair with *venous* sO₂ reported.
- **Novelty:** Uses the arterial blood of an accompanying artery (high, known saturation) as an in-situ spectral reference to correct wavelength-dependent fluence for the adjacent deep vein. In 8 healthy adults (dual-wavelength 3D PAT of the wrist), APM+ gave a **median venous sO₂ of 72.3 % (IQR 8.9 %)** — concentrated around the typical healthy 60–80 % range — while the LUM comparator gave 75.2 % with much wider spread (IQR 34.4 %). Phantom (true 75 %): **APM+ 72 ± 2 %**; phantom median error **2.9 % (APM+) vs 9.8 % (LUM)**; validated through up to 1.5 cm ex vivo tissue. Authors explicitly propose extending to the **carotid–IJV pair plus flow to estimate oxygen consumption** — the same O₂ER endpoint as §1.1 — noting "most major arteries in our body have accompanying veins (e.g., the brachial artery-vein pair in the arm, the carotid artery-internal jugular vein in the neck)."
- **Integration insights:** The artery-as-reference trick is the PA analogue of the arterio-venous compliance idea (§1.5) and the co-registered artery of §1.1. Its phantom-error figures remain the best in-corpus demonstration that per-vessel fluence correction beats linear unmixing for the venous compartment.

- **Year / Venue:** 2024 · PACT venue; PMC11161372; PubMed 38855671. ⚠ egress-blocked; re-confirmed via snippet this run.
- **Link:** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11161372/ · https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38855671/
- **Relevance:** **8/10** — human, intravascular sO₂ heterogeneity and flow, PACT; recovers the parabolic laminar-flow wavefront inside a deep vein in vivo.
- **Novelty:** Shows intravascular oxygenation heterogeneity can be used as endogenous contrast to visualize the spatiotemporal flow pattern inside human vessels — resolving sO₂ *within* a single deep vessel lumen rather than a single vessel-average value.

### 2.4 — Adaptive Spectroscopic Visible-Light OCT for Clinical Retinal Oximetry (ADS-vis-OCT) *(previously surfaced; re-confirmed this run)*
- **Year / Venue:** 2023 · *Communications Medicine* (Nature), s43856-023-00288-8; PubMed 37095177; PMC10126115.
- **Link:** https://www.nature.com/articles/s43856-023-00288-8 · https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37095177/
- **Relevance:** **8/10** — resolves *per-vessel arterial and venous* retinal sO₂ non-invasively.
- **Novelty:** Adds an adaptive spectral-contamination-removal step to vis-OCT, giving accurate, repeatable sO₂ in retinal arteries and veins (phantom bias ≈1 % across 0–100 %; RMSE vs pulse oximeter 2.1 % major arteries; repeat SD 2.5 % small arteries / 2.3 % small veins in 18 volunteers). Typical retinal readings place venous sO₂ ≈ 72 % vs arterial ≈ 95 %. Clinical/baseline siblings in corpus: the 2022 parafoveal vis-OCT baseline (*Front. Med.* — arterial 92.1 ± 7.1 vol%, venous 48.4 ± 5.0 vol%; re-surfaced this run), retinal-vein-occlusion vis-OCT (venous sO₂ ~6.3 % lower in ischemic areas), and the glaucoma macular-AV-difference pilot (macular venous sO₂ *rises* with severity; medRxiv 2023.12.20.23300300). Standing modeling/technique upgrades: **dcBLL** (*Med. Biol. Eng. Comput.* 64:2177, 2026; PubMed 42008039), **DGFT** depth-gated Fourier transform (Nolen et al., *Opt. Lett.* 50(4):1061, 2025; PubMed 39951728), the 2025 bioRxiv PLIM-SLO cross-validation of vis-OCT venous sO₂ (2025.05.29.656845), and the NIH R01 (OHSU) wide-field deep-learning vis-OCT oximetry project for ROP (Find-a-Trial listing reviewed Jun 2026).
- **Integration insights:** The retina is the one bed where per-vessel *venous* sO₂ is already clinically mapped non-invasively; its adaptive spectral-unmixing is directly transferable to the PA/RSOM vessel-resolving problem elsewhere.

### 2.5 — Recording Skin Oxygenation by Dual-Wavelength Ultra-Wideband Optoacoustic Mesoscopy (DW-RSOM) *(previously surfaced — first surfaced 2026-10-15; re-confirmed this run)*
- **Authors:** ⚠ search-derived — Aguirre / Berezhnoi / Ntziachristos and colleagues (TUM / Helmholtz Munich RSOM group).
- **Year / Venue:** 2026 · ***npj Imaging*** (Nature portfolio); DOI **10.1038/s44303-026-00184-5**; PubMed **42432228** (2026 Jul 10;4(1):51); PMC **13415847**. ⚠ egress-blocked; metadata re-confirmed this run via Nature + press snippets.
- **Link:** https://www.nature.com/articles/s44303-026-00184-5 · https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42432228/ · https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13415847/
- **Relevance:** **7/10** — human, in-vivo, non-invasive optoacoustic imaging that **explicitly resolves venule-compartment oxygenation**. *Flagged:* **microvascular / skin venule-level sO₂**, not a named-vein SvO₂/ScvO₂ — closer to a vessel-resolved StO₂.
- **Novelty:** First dual-wavelength (**515 nm + 532 nm**, two diode-pumped solid-state lasers) ultra-wideband raster-scan optoacoustic mesoscopy — prior RSOM was single-wavelength. In 6 healthy volunteers during post-occlusive reactive hyperemia and a vascular occlusion test, maps microvascular sO₂ at single-microvessel level, **thresholding the 515/532 nm absorption ratio at 0.9 to label vessels below as venules, above as arterioles**. Imaging-based mean sO₂ **80 ± 10 %** vs **87.5 ± 2.5 %** by blood-gas (p = 0.097); rose to **88 ± 7 %** after cuff release. (A related 2021 multispectral-RSOM forearm-occlusion study reported ≈85 ± 4 % superficial vs ≈54 ± 7 % deeper-dermal vessel sO₂.)
- **Integration insights:** The first corpus entry to perform **arteriole-vs-venule spectral separation at the microvascular scale** non-invasively — the fine-grained complement to the deep-vein PA anchors (§2.1–§2.3) and per-vessel retinal vis-OCT (§2.4).
