### 1.1 — Non-invasive, Continuous Venous Oxygen Saturation and Oxygen Extraction Estimation from the Internal Jugular Vein *(previously surfaced — the flagship new-generation true-venous device; re-confirmed this run)*
- **Authors:** ⚠ search-derived (bioengineering group associated with the jugular-optical-sensing line).
- **Year / Venue:** 2025 · **IEEE EMBC 2025** proceedings. IEEE Xplore doc 11254941; PubMed 41335695; DOI 10.1109/EMBC58623.2025.11254941. ⚠ egress-blocked; metadata from search-index snippets (SvO₂ estimates **65.70–80.91 %**, 6 healthy adults, flexible sensor array reading IJV pulsations; peripheral veins non-pulsatile so standard pulse oximetry cannot read SvO₂).
- **Link:** https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/11254941/ · https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41335695/ · https://doi.org/10.1109/embc58623.2025.11254941
- **Relevance:** **8.5/10** — directly targets non-invasive, continuous **true venous** sO₂ at the IJV plus cerebral O₂ extraction.
- **Novelty:** Flexible/conformal neck sensor array reads the **IJV pulse + breathing-induced modulation** (peripheral veins being non-pulsatile) to estimate SvO₂ ≈ **65.7–80.9 %** (SD 1.67 % for IJV-pulse, 4.26 % for breathing modulation) and a per-subject **cerebral O₂-extraction ratio (O₂ER)** in 6 healthy adults; co-registers carotid artery and IJV at one neck site. Authors flag artery–vein **crosstalk mitigation** and larger-cohort blood-gas validation as next steps.
- **Integration insights:** The extraction-aware wearable endpoint of the whole Topic-1 program; the artery serves as an in-situ reference (same logic as APM+, §2.2). Sibling registry trial **NCT06004466** (National Taiwan University Hospital) remains the independent clinical-translation counterpart; no published results yet. The UT-Austin **wearable optical e-tattoo** (2023 EMBC 10.1109/embc40787.2023.10340010; 2025 patent US2025/0025074) and the **Vanderbilt 48-channel PPG e-tattoo** are conformal-array siblings pursuing the same neck artery–vein pair. The De Gruyter jugular-optical probe (Hill/Campbell/Chase/Pretty, cdbme-2024-2072, re-hit this run) is the proof-of-concept external-jugular analogue. **Standing context:** NIH **K99EB035645** (carotid+IJV → OEF/CMRO₂) is the funded continuation via the §2.1 PA route — a grant, not a paper.

### 1.2 — Near-Infrared Spectroscopy for Estimating Mixed Venous Oxygen Saturation and Cardiac Output During Off-Pump Coronary Artery Bypass Grafting: A Prospective Observational Study *(previously surfaced — first logged 2026-09-08; re-confirmed this run)*
- **Authors:** Oh et al. ⚠ author line search-derived — not page-confirmed (PubMed/Springer egress-blocked).
- **Year / Venue:** 2026 · ***Journal of Clinical Monitoring and Computing*** (Springer); DOI **10.1007/s10877-026-01477-1**. PubMed 42606675.
- **Link / DOI:** https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10877-026-01477-1 · https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42606675/
- **Relevance:** **7.5/10** — non-invasive SvO₂ surrogate via **jugular + cerebral NIRS**, validated head-to-head against **pulmonary-artery-catheter SvO₂** in adults.
- **Novelty:** Tests two non-invasive NIRS surrogates in 25 OPCAB patients — (a) **rSjvO₂** over the left IJV (bias 2.0 %, wide LoA −14.9/+18.9 %) and (b) **baseline-calibrated cerebral NIRS (cal-rScO₂)** on the forehead (bias 0.8 %, LoA −7.8/+9.4 %); the calibrated cerebral measure reached **AUC 0.970 for detecting SvO₂ < 65 %** (sensitivity 95.9 %, specificity 89.8 %). **NIRS-derived cardiac output failed** (≈33 % error).
- **Integration insights:** The most rigorous 2026 adult validation yet of jugular/cerebral NIRS as an SvO₂ *trend* monitor. Baseline-calibrated cerebral NIRS **out-tracked a sensor placed right over the IJV** — a strong argument for the vessel-*resolving* imaging of Topic 2 over surface averaging. A 2023 retrospective (*Sci. Rep.* s41598-023-49078-1 / PMC10709586) independently reports cerebral oximetry tracking SvO₂ with ~91 % concordance. Clean SvO₂ < 65 % discrimination is the clinically actionable low-SvO₂-alarm endpoint.

### 1.3 — Monitoring Changes in Mixed Venous Oxygen Saturation Using Photoplethysmography on the Sternum — A Proof of Concept Study *(previously surfaced — first logged 2026-09-08; re-confirmed this run via Springer snippet)*
- **Authors:** ⚠ search-derived — likely the Scandinavian intraosseous-sternal-PPG group (prior porcine work *J. Clin. Monit. Comput.* 10.1007/s10877-023-00980-z).
- **Year / Venue:** 2026 (online 30 Jul 2026) · ***Medical & Biological Engineering & Computing*** (Springer); DOI **10.1007/s11517-026-03648-3**. PubMed 42527850. ⚠ metadata via Springer index snippet ("calibrated against pulmonary-artery SvO₂ in anesthetized pigs during hypoxia and haemorrhage; further technical development warranted").
- **Link / DOI:** https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11517-026-03648-3 · https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42527850/
- **Relevance:** **8/10** — non-invasive, continuous **true mixed-venous SvO₂** with a pulmonary-artery-catheter reference; a genuinely new anatomical site (sternum) for venous oximetry.
- **Novelty:** Exploits **sternal intramedullary (intraosseous) blood**, whose saturation tracks central/mixed-venous saturation, read **transcutaneously with a PPG probe** on the sternum. In anesthetized pigs, SvO₂ was driven down by stepwise hypoxia and staged hemorrhage while the non-invasive sternal signal (nSsO₂) was calibrated against pulmonary-artery SvO₂: **linear nSsO₂–SvO₂ correlation, ~87 % concordance**.
- **Integration insights:** A distinct physical route to non-invasive venous sO₂: the **bone marrow acts as a naturally venous-weighted optical reservoir**, sidestepping artery–vein disambiguation. The 2025 bone-marrow MSOT paper (Nano Letters, PMC12893716; Topic-2 standing lead) independently demonstrated that bone-marrow sO₂ is non-invasively optoacoustically measurable. The Noninvasix **LIVOx** chest-worn probe (FDA breakthrough, Feb 2022) and the NIRSense CHF sternal wearable (NCT07874386) are further independent bets on the chest/sternal site.

### 1.5 — Proof-of-Concept Non-Invasive Estimation of Peripheral Venous Oxygen Saturation (arterio-venous compliance pulse-modulation) *(previously surfaced — foundational method ancestor; re-confirmed this run via PMC snippet)*
- **Year / Venue:** 2017 · *BioMedical Engineering OnLine* 16:60; DOI 10.1186/s12938-017-0351-x; PMC5437414.
- **Link:** https://biomedical-engineering-online.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/s12938-017-0351-x · https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5437414/
- **Relevance:** **7/10** — the original non-invasive peripheral-venous (SpvO₂) optical concept.
- **Novelty:** Uses the **compliance difference between arteries and veins** to induce artificial respiration-like modulation that makes venous blood pulsatile, then reads the induced PPG to build a calibration model for regional venous sO₂; validated in 8 healthy volunteers with arm-lift/occlusion maneuvers against blood-gas references. Estimated SpvO₂ ran ≈3.1 ± 4.2 % below arterial saturation. (Siblings re-hit this run: 10.1186/s12938-016-0208-8 / PMC4952148, RMSE 5.31, r = 0.72; the 2007 Echiadis finger-pulse paper, PubMed 17664681 / IOPscience 10.1088/0967-3334/28/8/012; the PMC5655584 co-oximetry comparison — "significant refinement needed for clinically meaningful peripheral venous sO₂".)
- **Integration insights:** The method ancestor of the §1.3 sternal-PPG and §1.1 IJV-pulse devices — every "make the vein pulsatile, then read it like a pulse oximeter" approach traces here. The artery–vein crosstalk problem it first exposed is the same one the §1.1 e-tattoo and the Topic-2 spectral-ratio methods now attack.

### 2.2 — Arterial Blood-Mediated Deep-Tissue Photoacoustic Oximetry (APM+): In-Vivo Human Radial-Vein sO₂ *(previously surfaced; re-confirmed this run via the Caltech coilab-hosted PDF and the Wiley/PMC snippets)*
- **Authors:** Sastry et al. (Caltech). ⚠ full author line not page-confirmed this run (egress-blocked).
- **Year / Venue:** 2026 · ***Advanced Science*** (Wiley); DOI **10.1002/advs.76366**; PubMed 42397001; PMC13334587.
- **Link:** https://advanced.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/advs.76366 · https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13334587/ · https://coilab.caltech.edu/documents/34878/Sastry-2026-AdvancedScience.pdf
- **Relevance:** **8.5/10** — human, in vivo, PA, radial artery–vein pair with *venous* sO₂ reported.
- **Novelty:** Uses the **known high oxygenation of arterial blood as an in-situ fluence reference** (APM+) to correct deep-tissue PA oximetry. In 8 volunteers imaged at the radial artery–vein pair, APM+ gave median **venous sO₂ 72.3 %** (IQR 8.9 %) versus conventional linear-unmixing 75.2 % with IQR 34.4 %; in a simulated vessel at true 70 %, LUM overestimated to 98.5 % while APM+ gave 69.2 %.
- **Integration insights:** Directly attacks the fluence-unmixing overestimation bottleneck that plagues every PA oximetry line (the same bottleneck the new MOBILE depth-resolved readout and the GenMC/Hybrid-Net learned-fluence methods target). The arterial-reference logic mirrors the §1.1 artery-as-reference neck sensor.

### 2.4 — Adaptive Spectroscopic Visible-Light OCT for Clinical Retinal Oximetry (ADS-vis-OCT) *(previously surfaced)*

### 2.5 — ★ NEW this run — Harnessing Dynamic Multiple-Scattering for Pixel-Wise Retinal Oximetry in Visible-Light OCT
- **Authors:** ⚠ **search-derived, UNVERIFIED** — a Science.org citation listing gives *Hui Yang, Chuanqing Zhou* as the likely author pair; not page-confirmed (ScienceDirect egress-blocked).
- **Year / Venue:** 2026 · ***Optics & Laser Technology*** **204**:116070; DOI **10.1016/j.optlastec.2026.116070**; ScienceDirect pii S0030399226014210. ⚠ exact online date unconfirmed (listed as a full-length article).
- **Link / DOI:** https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0030399226014210 · https://doi.org/10.1016/j.optlastec.2026.116070
- **Relevance:** **7/10** — resolves **per-vessel arterial AND venous** retinal sO₂ and extends it to **pixel-wise maps across vascular networks**, non-invasively (vis-OCT). ⚠ retinal-vessel-level, not a named deep-vein SvO₂.
- **Novelty:** Proposes a **spatio-temporal statistical gating** method that isolates the *multiple-scattering* (dynamic) component of turbid-medium light by mapping the internal light field onto a **random matrix** — using random-matrix theory (Tracy-Widom noise-floor estimation, Marchenko-Pastur compensation) to separate the dynamic blood signal from the static tissue background. This suppresses the static-background contamination that has blocked **pixel-wise** vis-OCT oximetry, extending sO₂ estimation beyond large vessels into the capillary/vascular network. (Dedup-checked: absent from the 346-identifier corpus; distinct from the in-corpus dcBLL diffusion-correction modeling paper and the ADS-vis-OCT method.)
- **Integration insights:** A genuine methods advance on the §2.4 vis-OCT venous-oximetry line — moving from **vessel-averaged** to **pixel-wise** per-vessel sO₂ is the step needed to read small veins and the venular network, the retinal analogue of the §2.5-former DW-RSOM venule-compartment goal. Because it attacks multiple-scattering contamination (the same physics dcBLL models and the whole-blood-scattering vis-OCT literature flags), it is directly complementary to the modeling-side corpus. **Watch for a journal-confirmed author list and in-vivo venous validation figures; metadata is currently snippet-only and flagged.**

## Cross-topic synthesis

- **Two new items, one shared theme: moving from vessel-averaged to vessel/pixel-resolved venous readout.** This run's new vis-OCT paper (§2.5) pushes retinal oximetry from vessel-averaged to **pixel-wise** per-vessel sO₂ (reaching small veins and the venular network), while MOBILE (§2.NEW-a) brings a **depth-resolving, miniaturized PA front-end** that separates dermal layers during a venous-occlusion challenge. Both attack the same core obstacle that defines the whole field — **disentangling the venous signal from arterial and static-tissue background** — from opposite ends (optical multiple-scattering gating vs acoustic depth/compartment resolution).
- **The fluence/scattering bottleneck is the field's center of gravity.** APM+ (§2.2, arterial-reference fluence correction), GenMC and Hybrid-Net (learned fluence), dcBLL (diffusion-corrected Beer-Lambert), and now the §2.5 random-matrix multiple-scattering gating are all solving one problem: light transport corrupts the raw spectral signal, and uncorrected linear unmixing overestimates venous sO₂ (often non-physically >100 %). Every credible venous-oximetry advance now carries an explicit fluence/scattering-correction step — that is the methodological throughline across both topics.
- **Topic 1 remains the true bottleneck.** No new **surface, named-vein, true-SvO₂/ScvO₂** method appeared again this run — the clinically decisive niche (jugular/sternal/peripheral) is still carried by the §1.1–§1.5 corpus, all trend-monitors needing larger blood-gas validation. The two new items are both **microvascular/retinal-vessel-level**, valuable as imaging technique but not yet a catheter-replacing deep-vein SvO₂. The honest state of the field is unchanged: **vessel-resolving imaging (Topic 2) is advancing faster than surface absolute-SvO₂ sensing (Topic 1)**, and the §2.1 human-IJV PA measurement remains the one result that most directly bridges the two.
- **Convergent endpoint — oxygen extraction.** The §1.1 neck O₂ER device, the §2.2 artery-referenced APM+, the §2.3 PACT flow+sO₂ work, and the ULM+PA co-registration lead all point at the same destination: **co-registered per-vessel flow and oxygenation → regional O₂ extraction / CMRO₂**, non-invasively. MOBILE's flow-plus-oxygenation, depth-resolved readout is one more vote for that fusion being the field's real goal, beyond a single SvO₂ number.
