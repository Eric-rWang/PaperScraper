### Esophageal Resonance Raman Spectroscopy Tracks Mixed Venous Oxygenation in Experimental Shock and Hypoxemia
- **Authors:** ⚠ search-derived — the **Kheir / Romfh resonance-Raman spectroscopy group** (Boston Children's Hospital / Harvard; the line behind the 2024 *Trauma Surgery & Acute Care Open* esophageal-RRS paper by **Garcia Mancebo, Sack, Romfh, Peng, Kheir**, DOI 10.1136/tsaco-2024-001480). Exact 2026 author list not page-confirmed.
- **Year / Venue:** 2026 · ***Intensive Care Medicine Experimental*** (Springer); DOI **10.1186/s40635-026-00989-z**; online **2026-10-15**.
- **Link / DOI:** https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s40635-026-00989-z · https://doi.org/10.1186/s40635-026-00989-z ⚠ egress-blocked; metadata from a single Springer index snippet, cross-referenced against the confirmed 2024 predecessor (TSACO 9(1):e001600 / DOI 10.1136/tsaco-2024-001480, DOAJ-indexed).
- **Relevance:** **6.5/10** — a **true mixed-venous SvO₂** readout by an **optical** method (resonance Raman), **NIRS-benchmarked**. *Flagged:* **minimally-invasive esophageal probe in a rat model**, not surface-non-invasive and not human — hence out of the surface-non-invasive ranked five.
- **Novelty:** A miniaturized **esophageal resonance Raman spectroscopy (RRS)** probe estimates esophageal oxyhemoglobin saturation (SEsO₂) in rat models of hemorrhage and hypoxia. SEsO₂ **discriminated SvO₂ < 40 % with AUC 0.98** (95 % CI 0.95–1.00), versus **transabdominal NIRS AUC 0.82** (95 % CI 0.70–0.95) — i.e. the esophageal Raman site tracked mixed-venous desaturation substantially better than surface NIRS in the same models. Builds directly on the group's 2024 result that esophageal and tongue RRS-ShbO₂ correlated with SvO₂ (eShbO₂ r = 0.705; tShbO₂ r = 0.724; liver poorly) and with lactate, and that an esophageal threshold of ~40 % predicted near-term mortality in rodent hemorrhagic shock. Earlier lineage: the 2014 buccal-mucosa RRS-vs-SvO₂/ScvO₂ swine study reporting RRS-StO₂ tracks central oxygenation during hemorrhage better than NIRS.
- **Integration insights:** The **minimally-invasive Raman analogue** of the minimally-invasive **PA-TEE** esophageal photoacoustic SvO₂ probe (Wang lab, SPIE 2016, in corpus). Both exploit a deep, mucosa-accessible site to read a venous-weighted saturation that tracks mixed-venous SvO₂ — a different physical route (vibrational Raman vs photoacoustic) to the same endpoint the surface jugular/sternal methods (§1.1–§1.4) chase. The head-to-head **RRS > NIRS** discrimination for low SvO₂ is a sharp quantitative argument that **site/vessel specificity beats surface tissue-averaging** — the same thesis the vessel-resolving Topic-2 imaging program rests on. Watch for a less-invasive (per-oral/sublingual) or human translation; that would move it toward the ranked set.

### 1.1 — Non-invasive, Continuous Venous Oxygen Saturation and Oxygen Extraction Estimation from the Internal Jugular Vein *(previously surfaced — the flagship new-generation true-venous device)*
- **Authors:** ⚠ search-derived (bioengineering group associated with the jugular-optical-sensing line).
- **Year / Venue:** 2025 · **IEEE EMBC 2025** proceedings. IEEE Xplore doc 11254941; PubMed 41335695; DOI 10.1109/EMBC58623.2025.11254941. ⚠ egress-blocked; metadata re-confirmed this run via PubMed snippet (41335695 again surfaced: peripheral veins are non-pulsatile so standard pulse oximetry cannot read SvO₂; the device estimates SvO₂ + O₂-extraction ratio within expected literature ranges).
- **Link:** https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/11254941/ · https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41335695/ · https://doi.org/10.1109/embc58623.2025.11254941
- **Relevance:** **8.5/10** — directly targets non-invasive, continuous **true venous** sO₂ at the IJV plus cerebral O₂ extraction.
- **Novelty:** Flexible/conformal neck sensor array reads the **IJV pulse + breathing-induced modulation** (peripheral veins being non-pulsatile) to estimate SvO₂ ≈ **65.7–80.9 %** (SD 1.67 % for IJV-pulse, 4.26 % for breathing modulation) and a per-subject **cerebral O₂-extraction ratio (O₂ER)** in 6 healthy adults; co-registers carotid artery and IJV at one neck site, working upright and supine. Authors flag artery–vein **crosstalk mitigation** and larger-cohort blood-gas validation as next steps.
- **Integration insights:** The extraction-aware wearable endpoint of the whole Topic-1 program; the artery serves as an in-situ reference (same logic as APM+, §2.2). Sibling registry trial **NCT06004466** (National Taiwan University Hospital — plethysmographic device combining ultrasound imaging with optical tissue estimates for continuous non-invasive IJV saturation) re-surfaced again this run as the independent clinical-translation counterpart; no published results yet. The UT-Austin **wearable optical e-tattoo** (2023 EMBC 10.1109/embc40787.2023.10340010; 2025 patent application US2025/0025074) is the conformal-array sibling pursuing the same neck/wrist artery–vein pair with spatial filtering for crosstalk.

### 1.2 — Near-Infrared Spectroscopy for Estimating Mixed Venous Oxygen Saturation and Cardiac Output During Off-Pump Coronary Artery Bypass Grafting: A Prospective Observational Study *(previously surfaced — first logged 2026-09-08)*
- **Authors:** ⚠ search-derived — not page-confirmed (PubMed/Springer egress-blocked).
- **Year / Venue:** 2026 · ***Journal of Clinical Monitoring and Computing*** (Springer); DOI **10.1007/s10877-026-01477-1**. PubMed 42606675. (Snippet dates publication to August 2026; re-surfaced this run.)
- **Link / DOI:** https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10877-026-01477-1 · https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42606675/ ⚠ egress-blocked; metadata via index snippet.
- **Relevance:** **7.5/10** — non-invasive SvO₂ surrogate via **jugular + cerebral NIRS**, validated head-to-head against **pulmonary-artery-catheter SvO₂** in adults.
- **Novelty:** Tests two non-invasive NIRS surrogates in 25 OPCAB patients — (a) **rSjvO₂** over the left IJV (bias 2.0 %, wide LoA −14.9/+18.9 %) and (b) **baseline-calibrated cerebral NIRS (cal-rScO₂)** on the forehead (bias 0.8 %, LoA −7.8/+9.4 %); the calibrated cerebral measure reached **AUC 0.970 for detecting SvO₂ < 65 %** (sensitivity 95.9 %, specificity 89.8 %). **NIRS-derived cardiac output failed** (≈33 % error). Corroborated by the 2023 retrospective (PMC10709586).
- **Integration insights:** The most rigorous 2026 adult validation yet of jugular/cerebral NIRS as an SvO₂ *trend* monitor. Baseline-calibrated cerebral NIRS **out-tracked a sensor placed right over the IJV** — a strong argument for the vessel-*resolving* imaging of Topic 2 over surface averaging, and a direct precedent for the new esophageal-RRS paper's RRS > NIRS result above. Clean SvO₂ < 65 % discrimination is the clinically actionable low-SvO₂-alarm endpoint.

### 1.3 — Monitoring Changes in Mixed Venous Oxygen Saturation Using Photoplethysmography on the Sternum — A Proof of Concept Study *(previously surfaced — first logged 2026-09-08)*
- **Authors:** ⚠ search-derived — not page-confirmed. Likely the Scandinavian intraosseous-sternal-PPG group behind the earlier porcine sternal-oximetry work (*J. Clin. Monit. Comput.* 10.1007/s10877-023-00980-z).
- **Year / Venue:** 2026 · ***Medical & Biological Engineering & Computing*** (Springer); DOI **10.1007/s11517-026-03648-3**. PubMed 42527850.
- **Link / DOI:** https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11517-026-03648-3 · https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42527850/ ⚠ egress-blocked; metadata via index snippet.
- **Relevance:** **8/10** — non-invasive, continuous **true mixed-venous SvO₂** with a pulmonary-artery-catheter reference; a genuinely new anatomical site (sternum) for venous oximetry.
- **Novelty:** Exploits **sternal intramedullary (intraosseous) blood**, whose saturation tracks central/mixed-venous saturation, read **transcutaneously with a PPG probe** on the sternum. In anesthetized pigs, SvO₂ was driven down by stepwise hypoxia and staged hemorrhage while the non-invasive sternal signal (nSsO₂) was calibrated against pulmonary-artery SvO₂: **linear nSsO₂–SvO₂ correlation, ~87 % concordance** for tracking SvO₂ changes.
- **Integration insights:** A distinct physical route to non-invasive venous sO₂: the **bone marrow acts as a naturally venous-weighted optical reservoir**, sidestepping artery–vein disambiguation — the surface, non-invasive analogue of the deep mucosal reservoirs used by the esophageal-RRS and PA-TEE probes. Trend-grade (~87 %) performance sets a realistic accuracy bar; a natural fusion target for the pulse-modulation math of §1.5.

### 1.5 — Proof-of-Concept Non-Invasive Estimation of Peripheral Venous Oxygen Saturation (arterio-venous compliance pulse-modulation) *(previously surfaced — foundational method ancestor)*
- **Year / Venue:** 2017 · *BioMedical Engineering OnLine* 16:60; DOI 10.1186/s12938-017-0351-x; PMC5437414.
- **Link:** https://biomedical-engineering-online.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/s12938-017-0351-x · https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5437414/
- **Relevance:** **7/10** — the original non-invasive peripheral-venous (SpvO₂) optical concept.
- **Novelty:** Uses the **compliance difference between arteries and veins** to induce artificial respiration-like modulation that makes venous blood pulsatile, then reads the induced PPG to build a calibration model for regional venous sO₂; validated in 8 healthy volunteers with arm-lift/occlusion maneuvers against blood-gas references. Estimated SpvO₂ ran ≈3.1 ± 4.2 % below arterial saturation. (Siblings re-confirmed this run via index snippets: *A new method for noninvasive venous blood oxygen detection*, 10.1186/s12938-016-0208-8, 2016, PMC4952148/PubMed 27436186, RMSE 5.31, r = 0.72 vs invasive; *Comparison of non-invasive peripheral venous saturations with venous blood co-oximetry*, PMC5655584, r = 0.81; the 2007 artificial-finger-pulse during-bypass venous-oximetry paper, Echiadis et al., PubMed 17664681.)
- **Integration insights:** The method ancestor of the §1.3 sternal-PPG and §1.1 IJV-pulse devices — every "make the vein pulsatile, then read it like a pulse oximeter" approach traces here. Its modest accuracy is exactly what the deep-diffuse-optics pulsation-decomposition ideas in Topic 2 aim to improve.

> **Standing context — non-invasive external-jugular reflectance probe (previously surfaced, De Gruyter):** *Estimation of Venous Oxygen Saturation Through Non-Invasive Optical Sensing at the Jugular Veins* (cdbme-2024-2072, 10.1515/cdbme-2024-2072). Hill, Campbell, Chase, Pretty. Pulse-oximeter-like optical sensor on the external jugular vein; 3 subjects, SvO₂ 70.4/71.1/72.2 %. Re-confirmed in-corpus this run (De Gruyter Brill snippet); authors note the 70–75 % estimates still need calibration against blood-gas analysers. The surface-reflectance EJV sibling of the §1.1 IJV-pulse device.
> **Standing context — minimally-invasive PA-TEE SvO₂ probe (previously surfaced, Wang lab, SPIE 2016):** esophageal photoacoustic probe reading mixed-venous SvO₂ against the PA-catheter gold standard in swine — the photoacoustic twin of the new esophageal-RRS paper above.

### 2.1 — Noninvasive In Vivo Photoacoustic Measurement of Internal Jugular Venous Oxygenation in Humans *(previously surfaced — the Topic-2 anchor)*
- **Authors:** Garcia-Uribe et al. (Wang lab). ⚠ arXiv egress-blocked; abstract re-confirmed this run on index snippets (arXiv abs/PDF + Caltech authors.library records).
- **Year / Venue:** 2023 · preprint arXiv:2303.10775 (+ SPIE/Caltech-WUSTL lineage; precursor SPIE 8943E..1M, 2014).
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.10775 · https://authors.library.caltech.edu/records/5jp3j-jnz35
- **Relevance:** **9/10** — human, in vivo, PA, *venous* (IJV).
- **Novelty:** Dual-wavelength photoacoustic tomography co-registered with ultrasound in 7 healthy volunteers; average internal-jugular sijvO₂ **72 ± 7 %** (re-confirmed this run). >90 % of IJV drainage comes from intracranial structures, so the IJV reading can track global brain oxygen consumption. ### 2.2 — Arterial Blood-Mediated Deep-Tissue Photoacoustic Oximetry (APM+): In-Vivo Human Radial-Vein sO₂ *(previously surfaced)*
- **Authors:** Sastry et al. (Wang lab, Caltech). ⚠ re-confirmed this run via Wiley + Caltech COILab PDF snippets ("Sastry-2026-AdvancedScience").
- **Year / Venue:** 2026 · *Advanced Science* (Wiley), 10.1002/advs.76366; PMC13334587; PubMed 42397001. Online early July 2026.
- **Link:** https://advanced.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/advs.76366 · https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13334587/
- **Relevance:** **8.5/10** — human, in vivo, PA, radial artery–vein pair with *venous* sO₂ reported.
- **Novelty:** Uses the arterial blood of an accompanying artery as an in-situ spectral reference to correct wavelength-dependent fluence for the adjacent deep vein. In 8 healthy adults (dual-wavelength 3D PAT of the wrist), APM+ gave a **median venous sO₂ of 72.3 % (IQR 8.9 %)**; the LUM comparator gave 75.2 % with much wider spread (IQR 34.4 %). Phantom (true 75 %): **APM+ 72 ± 2 %** (ground-truth-closest). Phantom median error **2.9 % (APM+) vs 9.8 % (LUM)**. Authors explicitly propose extending to the **carotid–IJV pair plus flow to estimate oxygen consumption** — the same O₂ER endpoint as §1.1 — noting "most major arteries have accompanying veins" (re-confirmed via snippet this run).
- **Integration insights:** The artery-as-reference trick is the PA analogue of the arterio-venous compliance idea (§1.5) and the co-registered artery of §1.1; "most major arteries have an accompanying vein" is the generalization argument that makes this broadly applicable to deep veins.

### 2.4 — Adaptive Spectroscopic Visible-Light OCT for Clinical Retinal Oximetry (ADS-vis-OCT) *(previously surfaced)*
- **Year / Venue:** 2023 · *Communications Medicine* (Nature), s43856-023-00288-8; PubMed 37095177; PMC10126115.
- **Link:** https://www.nature.com/articles/s43856-023-00288-8 · https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37095177/
- **Relevance:** **8/10** — resolves *per-vessel arterial and venous* retinal sO₂ non-invasively.
- **Novelty:** Adds an adaptive spectral-contamination-removal step to vis-OCT, giving accurate, repeatable sO₂ in retinal arteries and veins across vessel diameters in 18 volunteers (RMSE vs pulse oximeter 2.1 % major arteries; repeat SD 2.5 % small arteries / 2.3 % small veins). - **Integration insights:** The retina is the one bed where per-vessel *venous* sO₂ is already clinically mapped non-invasively; its adaptive spectral-unmixing is directly transferable to the PA/RSOM vessel-resolving problem elsewhere.

### 2.5 — Recording Skin Oxygenation by Dual-Wavelength Ultra-Wideband Optoacoustic Mesoscopy (DW-RSOM) *(previously surfaced — first surfaced 2026-10-15)*
- **Authors:** ⚠ search-derived — Aguirre / Berezhnoi / Ntziachristos and colleagues (TUM / Helmholtz Munich RSOM group).
- **Year / Venue:** 2026 · ***npj Imaging*** (Nature portfolio); DOI **10.1038/s44303-026-00184-5**; PubMed **42432228** (2026 Jul 10;4(1):51); PMC **13415847**. ⚠ egress-blocked; metadata cross-checked on ≥3 snippets.
- **Link:** https://www.nature.com/articles/s44303-026-00184-5 · https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42432228/ · https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13415847/
- **Relevance:** **7/10** — human, in-vivo, non-invasive optoacoustic imaging that **explicitly resolves venule-compartment oxygenation**. *Flagged:* **microvascular / skin venule-level sO₂**, not a named-vein SvO₂/ScvO₂ — closer to a vessel-resolved StO₂.
- **Novelty:** First dual-wavelength (**515 nm + 532 nm**) ultra-wideband raster-scan optoacoustic mesoscopy — prior RSOM was single-wavelength. In 6 healthy volunteers during post-occlusive reactive hyperemia and a vascular occlusion test, maps microvascular sO₂ at single-microvessel level, **thresholding the 515/532 nm absorption ratio at 0.9 to label vessels below as venules, above as arterioles**. Imaging-based mean sO₂ **80 ± 10 %** vs **87.5 ± 2.5 %** by blood-gas (p = 0.097); rose to **88 ± 7 %** after cuff release.
- **Integration insights:** The first corpus entry to perform **arteriole-vs-venule spectral separation at the microvascular scale** non-invasively — the fine-grained complement to the deep-vein PA anchors (§2.1–§2.3) and per-vessel retinal vis-OCT (§2.4). The 515/532 nm ratio rule is a concrete, transferable artery–vein disambiguation rule; its occlusion/hyperemia protocol is the same maneuver used to make veins readable in §1.5.

> **Standing lead — GenMC: Real-Time Generative Monte Carlo Surrogate for Quantitative Photoacoustic Imaging** *(first logged 09-27; re-confirmed this run).* Shi, He, Vercauteren, Xia (King's College London). 2026 · preprint **arXiv:2609.28261**. ## Cross-topic synthesis (2026-10-23)

- **The one new item this run reframes the "non-invasive" boundary.** The esophageal resonance Raman paper (ICMx, 2026-10-15) is the first new-to-corpus entry in weeks that is simultaneously **optical**, **true mixed-venous SvO₂**, and **NIRS-benchmarked** — but it reaches the venous reservoir through the **esophagus (minimally invasive) in a rat model**. It therefore sits beside the minimally-invasive **PA-TEE** photoacoustic SvO₂ probe (§1 standing context) as the *other* internal-mucosal route to SvO₂. Both make the same bet as the entire vessel-resolving program: **go where the venous blood actually is, rather than averaging surface tissue.**

- **A convergent quantitative thesis is now stated three independent ways.** (1) The new esophageal RRS **out-discriminated transabdominal NIRS for SvO₂ < 40 %** (AUC 0.98 vs 0.82). (2) The OPCAB NIRS study (§1.2) found baseline-calibrated cerebral NIRS **out-tracked a sensor placed directly over the IJV**. (3) The "maximum fluence" PAM result shows **venous sO₂ is more fluence-biased than arterial**, which surface tissue-averaging cannot correct. All three say the same thing: **site/vessel specificity and fluence/scatter correction beat surface tissue-averaging for the venous compartment specifically** — the strategic case for Topics 1→2 crossover (vessel-resolving PA/vis-OCT/RSOM and fluence-correction methods like GenMC and APM+).

- **The two still-unsolved problems are unchanged and shared across both topics.** (i) **Artery–vein disambiguation / crosstalk** — addressed by co-registered-artery referencing (§1.1, APM+ §2.2), the DW-RSOM 515/532 nm ratio rule (§2.5), and intrinsically per-vessel retinal vis-OCT (§2.4). (ii) **Absolute-quantification bias at depth** — addressed by fluence correction (GenMC; APM+; eMSOT) and now independently quantified by the "maximum fluence" PAM result. The new RRS probe sidesteps (i) entirely by reading a venous-weighted mucosal site, trading non-invasiveness for site specificity.

- **Honest state of the field.** Surface-non-invasive human venous oximetry remains a **low-volume niche**: no new surface-non-invasive human venous paper this window; the ranked tens carry forward. The genuine frontier motion continues to be (a) **translation** of the IJV-pulse wearable (§1.1) and its registry sibling NCT06004466, (b) **extension** of APM+ from the radial pair to the carotid–IJV pair with flow for O₂-consumption, and (c) **cross-site method transfer** of the fluence-correction and artery–vein-separation tricks. Today's addition is a reminder that **minimally-invasive internal-mucosal optics (Raman, PA-TEE) can beat surface NIRS on venous specificity** — a benchmark the fully-non-invasive methods must aim to match.
