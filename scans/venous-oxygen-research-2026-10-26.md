## Topic 1 — Non-invasive monitoring of venous blood oxygen (SvO₂ / ScvO₂ / peripheral venous)

*One new venous-specific primary result this window (§1.1, a conference abstract). The remaining anchors carry forward, re-verified this run. The 2017 compliance-method ancestor is folded into standing context below. This remains a genuine low-publication-volume niche.*

### 1.1 — Continuous Venous Oxygen Saturation Monitoring Using a CRRT-Integrated Optical Sensor *(NEW this run — ASN Kidney Week 2026 abstract)*
- **Authors:** ⚠ search-derived — lead author **Bailey Antonowicz** (University of Michigan) per the ASN program listing; full author list not page-confirmed (`WebFetch` on asn-online.org DNS-egress-blocked this run).
- **Year / Venue:** 2026 · **ASN Kidney Week 2026** (Denver), poster **TH-PO0864**, session Thu 2026-10-22, Exhibit Hall A. controlId 4556555.
- **Link:** https://www.asn-online.org/education/kidneyweek/2026/program-abstract.aspx?controlId=4556555 ⚠ egress-blocked; metadata via search-index snippet.
- **Relevance:** **6/10** — targets **true central-venous sO₂ (ScvO₂)** optically against a PAC-SvO₂ reference, which is squarely on-topic; **capped** because the sensor is **inline on the extracorporeal CRRT circuit, not transcutaneous/non-invasive in the surface sense**, and the data are a 9-patient conference abstract with wide limits of agreement.
- **Novelty:** First report (to this corpus) of an **optical sensor built into the CRRT dialysis circuit** reading ScvO₂ continuously from blood drawn through an internal-jugular dialysis catheter. In 9 critically ill patients with concurrent PAC monitoring, Bland-Altman vs PAC SvO₂ gave **bias 7.1 %, 95 % LoA −12.3 % / +26.4 %**. The appeal is that it adds **no new invasive access** — it reads an existing venous circuit optically and continuously, where ScvO₂ is otherwise an intermittent blood-gas draw.
- **Integration insights:** A distinct deployment point for optical venous oximetry — **the extracorporeal circuit as an optically accessible venous reservoir**, conceptually adjacent to the sternal-marrow reservoir (§1.4) and the esophageal mucosal probes (standing context): each reads a naturally venous-weighted site to **sidestep artery–vein disambiguation** entirely. The wide LoA (±~19 %) sets the honest accuracy bar: this is trend-grade, consistent with every other non-catheter venous method in this corpus, and well short of replacing a blood-gas analyser. Natural next step the abstract implies: larger cohort, and whether the optical path (vs the circuit's existing flow sensors) adds value over continuous-ScvO₂ fiberoptic catheters.

### 1.2 — Non-invasive, Continuous Venous Oxygen Saturation and Oxygen Extraction Estimation from the Internal Jugular Vein *(previously surfaced — the flagship transcutaneous true-venous device)*
- **Authors:** ⚠ search-derived (the Hill/Chase/Pretty jugular-optical-sensing bioengineering line; shares lineage with the De Gruyter EJV probe, standing context).
- **Year / Venue:** 2025 · **IEEE EMBC 2025** proceedings. IEEE Xplore doc 11254941; PubMed 41335695; DOI 10.1109/EMBC58623.2025.11254941. ⚠ egress-blocked; metadata from prior-run PubMed snippets, re-confirmed this run via search index.
- **Link:** https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/11254941/ · https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41335695/ · https://doi.org/10.1109/embc58623.2025.11254941
- **Relevance:** **8.5/10** — directly targets non-invasive, continuous **true venous** sO₂ at the IJV plus cerebral O₂ extraction, transcutaneously.
- **Novelty:** Flexible/conformal neck sensor array reads the **IJV pulse + breathing-induced modulation** (peripheral veins being non-pulsatile) to estimate SvO₂ ≈ **65.7–80.9 %** (SD 1.67 % for IJV-pulse, 4.26 % for breathing modulation) and a per-subject **cerebral O₂-extraction ratio (O₂ER)** in 6 healthy adults; co-registers carotid artery and IJV at one neck site, working upright and supine. Authors flag artery–vein **crosstalk mitigation** and larger-cohort blood-gas validation as next steps.
- **Integration insights:** The extraction-aware transcutaneous-wearable endpoint of the whole Topic-1 program; the artery serves as an in-situ reference (same logic as APM+, §2.2). The **new §-standing "Unmixing the Neck" paper** (Cao & Rodriguez-Villegas, below) is the clean enabler for exactly this device's hardest problem — isolating the jugular venous pulse from carotid arterial pulsation in a neck PPG. Sibling registry trial **NCT06004466** (National Taiwan University Hospital — ultrasound imaging fused with optical tissue estimates for continuous IJV saturation) remains the independent clinical-translation counterpart; no published results yet.

### 1.3 — Near-Infrared Spectroscopy for Estimating Mixed Venous Oxygen Saturation and Cardiac Output During Off-Pump Coronary Artery Bypass Grafting *(previously surfaced — first logged 2026-09-08)*
- **Authors:** ⚠ search-derived — not page-confirmed (PubMed/Springer egress-blocked).
- **Year / Venue:** 2026 · ***Journal of Clinical Monitoring and Computing*** (Springer); DOI **10.1007/s10877-026-01477-1**. PubMed 42606675.
- **Link / DOI:** https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10877-026-01477-1 · https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42606675/ ⚠ egress-blocked; metadata via index snippet.
- **Relevance:** **7.5/10** — non-invasive SvO₂ surrogate via **jugular + cerebral NIRS**, validated head-to-head against **pulmonary-artery-catheter SvO₂** in adults.
- **Novelty:** Tests two non-invasive NIRS surrogates in 25 OPCAB patients — (a) **rSjvO₂** over the left IJV (bias 2.0 %, wide LoA −14.9/+18.9 %) and (b) **baseline-calibrated cerebral NIRS (cal-rScO₂)** on the forehead (bias 0.8 %, LoA −7.8/+9.4 %); the calibrated cerebral measure reached **AUC 0.970 for detecting SvO₂ < 65 %** (sensitivity 95.9 %, specificity 89.8 %). **NIRS-derived cardiac output failed** (≈33 % error). Corroborated by the 2023 retrospective (PMC10709586) and the Geneva external-jugular-vs-ScvO₂ validation.
- **Integration insights:** The most rigorous 2026 adult validation yet of jugular/cerebral NIRS as an SvO₂ *trend* monitor. Baseline-calibrated cerebral NIRS **out-tracked a sensor placed right over the IJV** — a strong argument for the vessel-*resolving* imaging of Topic 2 over surface averaging. Clean SvO₂ < 65 % discrimination is the clinically actionable low-SvO₂-alarm endpoint.

### 1.4 — Monitoring Changes in Mixed Venous Oxygen Saturation Using Photoplethysmography on the Sternum — A Proof of Concept Study *(previously surfaced — first logged 2026-09-08)*
- **Authors:** ⚠ search-derived — not page-confirmed. Likely the Scandinavian intraosseous-sternal-PPG group behind the earlier porcine sternal-oximetry work (*J. Clin. Monit. Comput.* 10.1007/s10877-023-00980-z).
- **Year / Venue:** 2026 · ***Medical & Biological Engineering & Computing*** (Springer); DOI **10.1007/s11517-026-03648-3**. PubMed 42527850.
- **Link / DOI:** https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11517-026-03648-3 · https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42527850/ ⚠ egress-blocked; metadata via index snippet.
- **Relevance:** **8/10** — non-invasive, continuous **true mixed-venous SvO₂** with a pulmonary-artery-catheter reference; a genuinely new anatomical site (sternum) for venous oximetry.
- **Novelty:** Exploits **sternal intramedullary (intraosseous) blood**, whose saturation tracks central/mixed-venous saturation, read **transcutaneously with a PPG probe** on the sternum. In anesthetized pigs, SvO₂ was driven down by stepwise hypoxia and staged hemorrhage while the non-invasive sternal signal (nSsO₂) was calibrated against pulmonary-artery SvO₂: **linear nSsO₂–SvO₂ correlation, ~87 % concordance** for tracking SvO₂ changes.
- **Integration insights:** A distinct physical route to non-invasive venous sO₂: the **bone marrow acts as a naturally venous-weighted optical reservoir**, sidestepping artery–vein disambiguation — the surface, non-invasive analogue of the deep mucosal reservoirs (esophageal-RRS, PA-TEE) and the new CRRT-circuit reservoir (§1.1). Trend-grade (~87 %) performance sets a realistic accuracy bar.

### 2.1 — Noninvasive In Vivo Photoacoustic Measurement of Internal Jugular Venous Oxygenation in Humans *(previously surfaced — the Topic-2 anchor)*
- **Authors:** Garcia-Uribe et al. (Wang lab). ⚠ arXiv egress-blocked; abstract re-confirmed this run on index snippets (arXiv abs/PDF + Caltech authors.library records 5jp3j-jnz35 and 0pb76-cp433 + WUSTL profiles).
- **Year / Venue:** 2023 · preprint arXiv:2303.10775 (+ SPIE/Caltech-WUSTL lineage; precursor SPIE 8943E..1M, 2014).
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.10775 · https://authors.library.caltech.edu/records/5jp3j-jnz35
- **Relevance:** **9/10** — human, in vivo, PA, *venous* (IJV).
- **Novelty:** Dual-wavelength photoacoustic tomography co-registered with ultrasound (5 fps) in 7 healthy volunteers; average internal-jugular sijvO₂ **72 ± 7 %** (re-confirmed this run). >90 % of IJV drainage comes from intracranial structures, so the IJV reading can track global brain oxygen consumption; framed as a non-invasive alternative to fluoroscopy-guided jugular catheterization. A larger PA/US cohort (SKKU; 165 carotids in 117 volunteers) reports IJV **71.3 ± 6.4 %** vs carotid **93.0 ± 3.5 %** — corroborating the ≈72 % IJV figure.
- **Integration insights:** The reference demonstration that deep-vein sO₂ is PA-measurable in humans. Everything else in Topic 2 either improves its quantification (GenMC standing lead; APM+ §2.2), extends the flow/vessel-type readout (PACT §2.3), moves to another bed (retina §2.4, skin microvasculature §2.5), or translates it to a wearable (LIVOx / e-tattoo, Topic-1 standing context).

### 2.2 — Arterial Blood-Mediated Deep-Tissue Photoacoustic Oximetry (APM+): In-Vivo Human Radial-Vein sO₂ *(previously surfaced)*
- **Authors:** Sastry et al. (Wang lab, Caltech). ⚠ re-confirmed this run via Caltech COILab PDF snippet ("Sastry-2026-AdvancedScience"; Received 5 Jan 2026, Revised 5 Jun 2026, Accepted 8 Jun 2026).
- **Year / Venue:** 2026 · *Advanced Science* (Wiley), 10.1002/advs.76366; PMC13334587; PubMed 42397001. Online early July 2026.
- **Link:** https://advanced.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/advs.76366 · https://coilab.caltech.edu/documents/34878/Sastry-2026-AdvancedScience.pdf · https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13334587/
- **Relevance:** **8.5/10** — human, in vivo, PA, radial artery–vein pair with *venous* sO₂ reported.
- **Novelty:** Uses the arterial blood of an accompanying artery (high, known saturation) as an in-situ spectral reference to correct wavelength-dependent fluence for the adjacent deep vein. In 8 healthy adults (dual-wavelength 3D PAT of the wrist), APM+ gave a **median venous sO₂ of 72.3 % (IQR 8.9 %)**; the LUM comparator gave 75.2 % with much wider spread (IQR 34.4 %). Phantom (true 75 %): **APM+ 72 ± 2 %**; phantom median error **2.9 % (APM+) vs 9.8 % (LUM)**; validated through up to 1.5 cm ex vivo tissue. Authors explicitly propose extending to the **carotid–IJV pair plus flow to estimate oxygen consumption** — the same O₂ER endpoint as §1.2.
- **Integration insights:** The artery-as-reference trick is the PA analogue of the arterio-venous compliance idea (standing context) and the co-registered artery of §1.2; "most major arteries have an accompanying vein" is the generalization argument that makes this broadly applicable to deep veins. Its phantom-error figures remain the best in-corpus demonstration that per-vessel fluence correction beats linear unmixing for the venous compartment.

### 2.3 — Oxygenation Heterogeneity Facilitates Spatiotemporal Flow-Pattern Visualization Inside Human Blood Vessels Using PACT *(previously surfaced)*
- **Year / Venue:** 2024 · PACT venue; PMC11161372; PubMed 38855671. ⚠ egress-blocked; re-confirmed via snippet this run.
- **Link:** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11161372/ · https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38855671/
- **Relevance:** **8/10** — human, intravascular sO₂ heterogeneity and flow, PACT; recovers the parabolic laminar-flow wavefront inside a deep vein in vivo.
- **Novelty:** Shows intravascular oxygenation heterogeneity can be used as endogenous contrast to visualize the spatiotemporal flow pattern inside human vessels — resolving sO₂ *within* a single deep vessel lumen rather than a single vessel-average value.

### 2.4 — Adaptive Spectroscopic Visible-Light OCT for Clinical Retinal Oximetry (ADS-vis-OCT) *(previously surfaced)*
- **Year / Venue:** 2023 · *Communications Medicine* (Nature), s43856-023-00288-8; PubMed 37095177; PMC10126115.
- **Link:** https://www.nature.com/articles/s43856-023-00288-8 · https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37095177/
- **Relevance:** **8/10** — resolves *per-vessel arterial and venous* retinal sO₂ non-invasively.
- **Novelty:** Adds an adaptive spectral-contamination-removal step to vis-OCT, giving accurate, repeatable sO₂ in retinal arteries and veins across vessel diameters in 18 volunteers (RMSE vs pulse oximeter 2.1 % major arteries; repeat SD 2.5 % small arteries / 2.3 % small veins). Typical retinal readings place venous sO₂ ≈ 72 % vs arterial ≈ 95 %. Clinical siblings in corpus: retinal-vein-occlusion vis-OCT (venous sO₂ ~6.3 % lower in ischemic areas) and the 2025 glaucoma macular-AV-difference pilot (TVST 2802559; Johns Hopkins, 48 eyes/35 subjects). Standing modeling/technique upgrades: **dcBLL** (*Med. Biol. Eng. Comput.* 64:2177, 2026; PubMed 42008039) and **DGFT** depth-gated Fourier transform (Nolen et al., *Opt. Lett.* 50(4):1061, 2025; PubMed 39951728); capillary-level vis-OCT (PNAS 10.1073/pnas.1918546117).
- **Integration insights:** The retina is the one bed where per-vessel *venous* sO₂ is already clinically mapped non-invasively; its adaptive spectral-unmixing is directly transferable to the PA/RSOM vessel-resolving problem elsewhere.

### 2.5 — Recording Skin Oxygenation by Dual-Wavelength Ultra-Wideband Optoacoustic Mesoscopy (DW-RSOM) *(previously surfaced — first surfaced 2026-10-15)*
- **Authors:** ⚠ search-derived — Aguirre / Berezhnoi / Ntziachristos and colleagues (TUM / Helmholtz Munich RSOM group).
- **Year / Venue:** 2026 · ***npj Imaging*** (Nature portfolio); DOI **10.1038/s44303-026-00184-5**; PubMed **42432228** (2026 Jul 10;4(1):51); PMC **13415847**. ⚠ egress-blocked; metadata cross-checked on prior-run snippets + re-confirmed this run.
- **Link:** https://www.nature.com/articles/s44303-026-00184-5 · https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42432228/ · https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13415847/
- **Relevance:** **7/10** — human, in-vivo, non-invasive optoacoustic imaging that **explicitly resolves venule-compartment oxygenation**. *Flagged:* **microvascular / skin venule-level sO₂**, not a named-vein SvO₂/ScvO₂ — closer to a vessel-resolved StO₂.
- **Novelty:** First dual-wavelength (**515 nm + 532 nm**) ultra-wideband raster-scan optoacoustic mesoscopy — prior RSOM was single-wavelength. In 6 healthy volunteers during post-occlusive reactive hyperemia and a vascular occlusion test, maps microvascular sO₂ at single-microvessel level, **thresholding the 515/532 nm absorption ratio at 0.9 to label vessels below as venules, above as arterioles**. Imaging-based mean sO₂ **80 ± 10 %** vs **87.5 ± 2.5 %** by blood-gas (p = 0.097); rose to **88 ± 7 %** after cuff release.
- **Integration insights:** The first corpus entry to perform **arteriole-vs-venule spectral separation at the microvascular scale** non-invasively — the fine-grained complement to the deep-vein PA anchors (§2.1–§2.3) and per-vessel retinal vis-OCT (§2.4). The 515/532 nm ratio rule is a concrete, transferable artery–vein disambiguation rule; its occlusion/hyperemia protocol is the same maneuver used to make veins readable in the 2017 compliance method.

> **Standing lead — GenMC: Real-Time Generative Monte Carlo Surrogate for Quantitative Photoacoustic Imaging** *(first logged 09-27; re-confirmed this run via arXiv HTML/PDF snippet).* Shi, He, Vercauteren, Xia (King's College London). 2026 · preprint **arXiv:2609.28261** (submitted 23 Sep 2026). A PA fluence-quantification method enabling quantitative venous sO₂ at depth; fluence maps in <30 ms/frame (~4 orders-of-magnitude MC speed-up); with correction yields in-vivo **venous sO₂ ≈ 78.05 %** (vs MC baseline 78.36 %; uncorrected linear-unmixing overestimate ≈99.98 %); validated in phantoms and **37 human volunteers spanning Fitzpatrick III–V**. ~7.5/10; a strong candidate to re-enter the ranked five on journal publication.
> **Standing lead — 3D PACT with ultrasound localization priors (NEW this run; NOT venous-specific):** Huo H, Xu Y, Yao R, Lowerison M, Song P, Yao J. *Photoacoustics* 2026 Oct;51:100861 (Duke/Yao lab). A real 2026 PA-imaging advance using ultrasound-localization priors for deep 3D PA tomography; **no venous-oxygenation readout reported**, so logged as a methods/instrumentation lead only (~5/10 for this corpus), relevant insofar as better deep 3D PACT underpins deep-vein sO₂ mapping (§2.1–§2.3).
> **Standing lead — "Maximum fluence for accurate functional photoacoustic microscopy"** *(already in corpus).* *Photoacoustics* (ScienceDirect S2213597926000571). Reports that, versus saO₂, **svO₂ shows a larger increase with increasing laser fluence** — a direct quantification of the fluence-dependent bias that most inflates *venous* PA estimates, reinforcing the fluence-correction thesis of GenMC and APM+ (§2.2). Related classic fluence-correction work: Naser et al. local fluence correction (*IEEE TMI* 2019, SO₂ error 10.1 %→2.8 %); Zhou et al. diffusion-dipole vs Monte-Carlo (PMC7210453); deep-learning iterative fluence correction (arXiv:2312.01727). Methods line, ~6/10.
> **Standing lead — learned qPA oximetry (venous-transferable):** *Deep Learning-Driven Quantitative Spectroscopic PA Imaging for Segmentation and sO₂ Estimation* (Hybrid-Net; arXiv:2512.15394) estimates sO₂ + vessel segmentation without explicit fluence estimation (trained at 700/850 nm); eigenspectra MSOT / eMSOT (*Nat. Commun.* 2016, ncomms12121) remains the deep-tissue sO₂ foundation; distribution-informed wavelength-flexible data-driven oximetry (arXiv:2403.14863). Learned-inversion line feeding the venous-quantification problem.
